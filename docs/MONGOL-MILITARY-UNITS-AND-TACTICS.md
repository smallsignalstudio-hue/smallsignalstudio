# MONGOL EMPIRE MILITARY UNITS AND TACTICS — Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Doc)

> English research notes for a tower-defence game bible (same project family as Chinese / Viking / Persian / Greek TD research).  
> **Method:** Prefer **contemporary and near-contemporary sources** (*Secret History of the Mongols*, Rashīd al-Dīn, Juwaynī / Juvaini, Chinese Yuan / Jin / Song materials, European travellers) and **archaeology** over film, games, and “eternal Mongol horde” stereotypes. Flag every claim with an evidence class. Do **not** invent fixed TO&Es, universal kit lists, or a single timeless “Mongol army.”  
> **Hard rule:** **Mongol Empire ≠ one static army.** Early steppe confederacy forces differ from imperial multiethnic hosts that incorporate Chinese engineers, Persian/Khurasanian siege specialists, Rus’ and other auxiliaries, and regional levies. Treat **period and theatre** as first-class design axes.

### Evidence tags (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Archaeology (burials, weapon finds, horse gear, siege residue, camp sites where known) |
| **CHR** | Contemporary / near-contemporary chronicles & travellers: *Secret History*; Rashīd al-Dīn; Juwaynī; Chinese Yuan/Jin/Song historiography; Carpini, Rubruck, Marco Polo, etc. |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarship (Morgan, May, Allsen, Smith, Kennedy, Di Cosmo, Rossabi, Buell, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Popular myth, film (*Mongol*, Hollywood “Horde”), wargame clichés, meme tactics presented as fact |

| Source | Nature / caution |
|---|---|

| **Rashīd al-Dīn**, *Jāmiʿ al-tawārīkh* | Ilkhanid court historian (early 14th c.); access to Mongol genealogies/records; Persian administrative lens; invaluable for organisation, conquest narrative, multiethnic empire. **CHR** |

## 0. Framing: what “the Mongol army” was

**What it is not.** A permanent national infantry corps with one uniform, one doctrine, and unchanging siege mastery from 1206 to 1368. **FOLK** if presented that way.

**What it is.** A **decimal-organised cavalry-centred military system** originating in steppe politics under Temüjin / Chinggis Khan, then scaled into an **imperial multiethnic war machine** that absorbed subject specialists. Phases for this brief:

1. **Pre-unification / early unification (late 12th–c. 1206–1211)** — steppe confederacy warfare: horse archery, feuds, limited siege craft vs fortified Jin/Xia cities. **CHR** (*Secret History*) + **MOD**
2. **Early imperial conquests (c. 1211–1241)** — northern China, Khwarazm, Russia/Eastern Europe; rapid learning curve in siege via captured artisans and Chinese/Khitan/Jurchen expertise. **CHR** + **MOD**
3. **Mid-century successor khanates** — Ögödei / Möngke era campaigns; Hülegü in Iran/Iraq; deepening administrative armies. **CHR** (Juwaynī, Rashīd) + **MOD**
4. **Yuan / late conquest (Khubilai)** — Song conquest (Xiangyang debates), Korea, Japan amphibious failures, southern Chinese infantry/naval realities. **CHR** (Yuan/Song) + **MOD**

**Multiethnic reality (non-negotiable).** Imperial field forces and especially **siege trains** included:
- Mongol / Turkic steppe cavalry cores  
- **Chinese** (and earlier Jin/Khitan) **engineers, artillerists, infantry labour**  
- **Persian / Central Asian** specialists (notably counterweight trebuchet narratives at Xiangyang)  
- **Rus’**, Alan, Georgian, Armenian, Korean, and other auxiliaries by theatre  
Do not flatten these into “all Mongols on horses.” **CHR** + **MOD** (Allsen on specialist transfer)

**Evidence quality (framing).**
| Topic | Quality |
|---|---|
| Decimal vocabulary (arban / jagun / mingghan / tumen) | **High** as organisational language (**CHR**); **medium** for full-strength tumens in the field |
| Horse-archer supremacy on open steppe / plains | **High** (**CHR** travellers + campaign outcomes) |
| Exact kit percentages (how many “heavy” vs “light”) | **Low–medium** — reconstructions vary (**MOD**) |
| Early siege competence without specialists | **Low** — sources emphasise learning & incorporation (**CHR**/**MOD**) |
| Film “Mongol berserker horde” | **FOLK** |

**Game-use potential:** Faction tech spine = **early steppe raid army** → **engineer unlock via conquered peoples** → **khanate theatre packs** (China siege, Iran/Iraq cities, European winter limits, Japan naval fail state). Player “Mongols” should feel like a **coalition command**, not a single unit roster.

## 1. Decimal organisation

### 1.1 Units: arban / jagun / mingghan / tumen

**What the words mean (conventional).**
| Mongolian term | Approximate size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Arban** (*arban*) | **10** | Smallest decimal cell; mutual responsibility rhetoric in narratives. **CHR** |
| **Jagun** / *jaγun* / *zuun* | **100** | Ten arbans. **CHR** |
| **Mingghan** (*mingγan*) | **1,000** | Core operational cluster in many modern readings. **CHR** + **MOD** |
| **Tumen** (*tümen*) | **10,000** (ideal) | Often a **nominal** ceiling; field strengths frequently understrength (**MOD** May, Morgan, Smith debates) |

**Placement.** *Secret History* and Persian/Chinese accounts present decimal appointment as a **Chinggisid reorganisation tool**—breaking old tribal war-band loyalties into artificial decimal commands under appointed noyan. **CHR**

**Do not assume.** That every tumen always mustered 10,000 effectives; that decimal labels equal modern battalions with fixed TO&E; that non-Mongol auxiliaries always sat inside the same decimal purity. **MOD**

**Controversies.**
1. **Paper vs present strength:** Modern estimates of total Mongol manpower vary widely; treat campaign “tumen counts” as **order-of-battle language**, not census. **MOD**
2. **Continuity:** Decimal military language has deeper Inner Asian antecedents (Xiongnu *etc.* in Chinese historiography)—Mongol usage is a **specific institutionalisation**, not unique invention *ex nihilo*. **MOD** (Di Cosmo context)
3. **Social mixing:** Decimal units could incorporate conquered peoples; “Mongol tumen” ≠ ethnically pure. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Recruitment UI in tens/hundreds/thousands; **tumen** as late army-cap banner that is expensive and rarely at full HP/stack size—teach players “nominal vs real.”

### 1.2 Command, discipline, signal

**What sources emphasise.** Harsh discipline; collective punishment motifs; horn / flag / messenger coordination; pre-battle planning and assigned roles in set-piece actions. Carpini and others stress organisation and obedience as Europeans’ surprise. **CHR**

**Nerge (great hunt) as training metaphor.** Mass hunting drives—animals driven into a contracting ring—appear in Mongol political culture and are widely read by **MOD** scholars as a **model for encirclement warfare**. Useful analogy; do not treat every battle as a literal hunt ritual. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Global “discipline” aura; shared cooldown on retreat/feint abilities across decimal stacks; hunt-minigame that unlocks encircle tech.

## 2. Cavalry: light and heavy

### 2.1 Light cavalry / horse archers (core)

**What.** Mounted archers with **composite bows**, multiple remounts where logistics allow, mobility-first doctrine: skirmish, probe, exhaust, pursue. Primary **offensive signature** of early–mid Mongol field armies on suitable terrain. **CHR** (Carpini, Chinese campaign narratives) + **ARCH** (bow remains / arrow typology where available) + **MOD**

**Remounts.** Multiple horses per warrior is a **recurring** claim in travellers and modern syntheses—enables sustained movement rates relative to infantry-heavy foes. Exact ratios vary by status/wealth; do not invent a universal “5 horses each” as law. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Fast lane units with kiting, high map speed, poor performance in mud/forest/fort chokepoints; remount passive = reduced fatigue.

### 2.2 Heavier cavalry / lancers / armoured horse

**What.** Not absent. Sources and archaeology support **lamellar / scale / leather–metal mixes**, helmets, and shock/lance roles especially among elites and in later multiethnic hosts. “All Mongols = unarmoured swarm” is **FOLK**. **CHR** + **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Spectrum, not binary.** Better model: a **continuum** from lightly equipped horse-archers to better-protected close-combat cavalry—proportions shift by period, theatre, and captured armour supply. **MOD**

**Controversies.** How “cataphract-like” late western khanate cavalry became; how much Jin/Song armour was reused; whether European accounts overstate or understate protection. Leave as **design dial**, not locked fact. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Mid/late unlock “armoured noyan cavalry”—slower, better vs towers’ arrow storms, costlier; unlocked via loot/engineer economy.

### 2.3 Horses as system

**What.** Steppe horse husbandry underwrites strategy: grazing logistics, winter hardiness debates, and limits when campaigning in humid south China or amphibious theatres. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Southern China / Japan problem.** Cavalry dominance **declines** where terrain, climate, fortification density, and naval requirements dominate—Yuan needed Chinese infantry/naval resources. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Biome penalties; “pasture supply” resource; southern maps force engineer/infantry mix.

## 3. Composite bow and missile doctrine

### 3.1 Bow construction and use

**What.** **Composite reflex** bow (horn, sinew, wood core—classic steppe technology family). Powerful for size; shot from horseback with thumb-draw traditions in Inner Asia. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Carrying capacity.** Quivers and multiple arrow types appear in accounts and finds; specialised armour-piercing vs signalling/incendiary arrows appear in broader Eurasian military culture—assign Mongol-specific subtypes only when a source supports the theatre. **CHR**/**ARCH**/**MOD** — do not invent fantasy arrow tech trees without labels.

**Rate of fire / accuracy.** Travellers marvel; modern experiments exist (**MOD**/experimental archaeology)—use as **flavour bounds**, not spreadsheet gospel.

**Game-use:** Signature ranged cavalry weapon; humidity/rain debuff optional (composite bow vulnerability trope—**MOD** discusses; don’t over-absolutise).

### 3.2 Massed harassment vs decisive volley

**What doctrine looks like in sources.** Prolonged missile pressure, selective close engagement, refusal of fair infantry slugfests when mobility advantage exists. **CHR**

**Not.** A gunpowder-era firing line. **FOLK** if “Mongol muskets” appear early without Yuan/Ming gunpowder context.

## 4. Signature field tactics

### 4.1 Feigned retreat

**What.** Deliberate flight to disorder pursuers, then counterattack—reported by European and Asian observers as a Mongol hallmark. **CHR** (Carpini and battle narratives across theatres)

**Design caution.** Feigned retreat is an **Inner Asian / steppe toolkit classic**, not a Mongol copyright; also easy for chroniclers to claim after any disorderly chase. Mark as **well-attested pattern**, not magic win button every battle. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Active ability: unit “breaks,” enemy towers/units overextend chase path, ambush spawns on flanks; counterplay = disciplined refuse-chase doctrine for defenders.

### 4.2 Encirclement and multi-column advance

**What.** Converging columns, wing wraps, leaving intentional gaps that become kill-sacs—often linked by **MOD** writers to *nerge* hunting logic. Campaign narratives (Khwarazm, European 1241, etc.) show coordinated multi-prong movements at operational scale. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Dual-lane spawn pressure; “gap” hazard tiles that punish player clustering wrong; fog-of-war columns.

### 4.3 Scouting, spies, and intelligence

**What.** Advance screens, captured locals as guides, merchant networks, and deliberate reconnaissance before main blows—emphasised in conquest narratives and modern operational studies. **CHR** + **MOD** (May)

**Game-use:** Reveal fog buildings; spy debuff on enemy towers (reduced range until purged); merchant caravan events that sell map intel.

### 4.4 Psychological warfare and submission politics

**What.** Reputation for massacre after resistance vs relative safety after surrender appears as a **strategic communication** pattern in Islamic, Chinese, and European sources (with obvious moral and rhetorical distortion). **CHR** — use carefully in game tone.

**Game-use:** Diplomacy toggle: cities that open gates deny you loot but deny defenders militia spawn; “terror aura” accelerates rout of low-morale towers—label ethically.

### 4.5 Seasonal and logistical tempo

**What.** Campaigning tied to grass, river freeze (famous in some northern Chinese / river-crossing contexts), and fodder. Overextension and climate punish cavalry empires. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Season modifier panel; winter freeze bridges lanes; summer drought weakens pasture resource.

## 5. Siege warfare — learning curve, not starting talent

### 5.1 Early limits

**What.** Against Jin and Tangut/Xia fortified cities, early Mongol forces struggle until they **incorporate engineers, artisans, and artillery crews** from conquered populations. Treating 1206 Mongols as instant fortress-eaters is **FOLK**. **CHR** + **MOD**

### 5.2 Traction trebuchets and Chinese artillery traditions

**What.** East Asian siege arsenals already used **traction trebuchets** (*pao* 砲 and related) manned by pulling crews, plus crossbow artillery, towers, rams, and mining—Mongol command **adopts and redirects** these systems. **CHR** (Chinese) + **MOD** (Needham tradition; Allsen on technology transfer)

**Game-use:** Early “captured Jin artillery team” unlock; crew-intensive traction trebuchet = many labour units to fire.

### 5.3 Counterweight trebuchets — Xiangyang debate node

**What (Yuan–Song).** The long Song defence of **Xiangyang / Fancheng** (commonly dated peaking c. **1268–1273**) is a key TD reference: riverine fortress pair, blockade, and introduction of **counterweight trebuchets** often called *huihui pao* 回回砲 (“Western/Muslim trebuchets”) in Chinese sources, associated with engineers commonly named **Ismāʿīl** and **ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn** sent from the Ilkhanate sphere under Khubilai’s war. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Debates / design honesty.**
| Issue | Status |
|---|---|
| Exact mechanical novelty vs prior Chinese traction systems | **Discussed in MOD**—counterweight = step-change in many readings |
| How decisive the new *pao* were vs blockade/starvation/naval control | **Debated**—do not single-cause the fall |
| “Mongols invented trebuchets” | **False** — **FOLK** |
| Persian/Islamic engineering contribution | **Attested in Chinese narrative tradition** — keep multiethnic credit |

**Game-use:** Mid/late tech “Ilkhanid engineer embassy” that upgrades traction → counterweight; Xiangyang map as set-piece.

### 5.4 Mining, blockade, and labour armies

**What.** Sapping/mining, dyke/river manipulations in Chinese wars, mass labour for earthworks, and **blockade** as attrition tool appear across theatres. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Sub-tower miner units; hunger meter on city keeps; labour camps as buildables that anger subject populations (revolt risk).

### 5.5 Case nodes for the bible

#### Zhongdu (Jin middle capital; modern Beijing area) — 1215 capture context
**What.** Prolonged Mongol pressure on Jin capital region culminating in **1215** seizure/devastation narratives after earlier campaigns (from 1211). Illustrates early imperial learning: cavalry army forced into **siege + negotiation + massacre politics** against Chinese urban defence. Exact operational details vary by source cluster. **CHR** (Chinese / *Secret History* context) + **MOD**

**TD lesson:** Early-game Mongol attacker is strong in field, **weak in pure wall DPS** until engineer tech.

#### Baghdad — 1258 (Hülegü)
**What.** Ilkhanid capture of Abbasid Baghdad after investment; sources describe large multiethnic armies, siege engines, and catastrophic sack. Numbers in chronicles are rhetorical—use for **tone and multiethnic siege**, not literal headcounts. **CHR** (Islamic histories; Rashīd tradition) + **MOD** (Kennedy et al.)

**TD lesson:** Mid-game “Persian theatre” attacker with heavy engine stack + subject levies; defender is urban militia + walls, not steppe duel.

#### Xiangyang / Fancheng — debates above
**TD lesson:** Best “fair fortress duel” map for Mongol imperial period; teaches naval/river + counterweight + time.

### 5.6 Incendiaries and gunpowder adjacency

**What.** Chinese theatres already feature gunpowder weapons in Song–Jin–Yuan conflicts (bombs, fire-lances, etc. in Needham’s dossier). Mongol command **uses the Chinese toolkit** rather than arriving with a separate steppe gunpowder doctrine. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Optional late Song loot tech; do not give early Chinggis gunpowder rockets as default **FOLK**.

## 6. Infantry and naval arms — limits

### 6.1 Infantry

**What.** Steppe Mongol core is cavalry-centred; **infantry appears as subject levies, garrison troops, labour, and Chinese/Korean etc. contingents** under imperial command—especially in southern conquests. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Do not.** Invent a native Mongol pike phalanx as the primary identity. **FOLK**

**Game-use:** Infantry as **aux unlocks** tied to conquered regions; necessary on siege maps; weak on open steppe vs enemy cavalry if misused.

### 6.2 Naval warfare — Korea and Japan

**Korea (Koryŏ).** Repeated campaigns; eventual vassalage dynamics; naval/coastal operations require local shipping and manpower. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Japan invasions (Bun’ei 1274; Kōan 1281).** Yuan–Korean–Chinese fleets; landing and castle/fort resistance; storms (kamikaze tradition in Japanese memory) destroy much of the fleets—**exact weight of weather vs Japanese defence vs logistical failure** is a **MOD** debate. Outcome clear: **amphibious projection fails** to conquer Japan. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Hard limit for design.** Mongol/Yuan power is **not** naturally blue-water dominant; naval success depends on **subject shipbuilding**. Two Japan attempts are perfect **fail-state campaigns**. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Naval tech gated behind Korea/Chinese shipyards; Japan map with storm disaster cards; teach players that cavalry meta dies at the shoreline.

## 8. Defending a steppe camp / ordu

### 8.1 What an ordu is

**Ordu / ordo** — camp, royal encampment, mobile court; political and military centre, not a stone keep. Rubruck and Carpini describe camp layouts, tents (*ger*), guards, and court ritual. **CHR**

### 8.2 Defensive problems (honest)

Steppe camps are **vulnerable to surprise, night risk, and superior scouting**—ironic, given Mongol offence scouting. Wagon laagers, sentry rings, and rapid cavalry sortie are the logical responses discussed in travellers and modern reconstruction—**avoid inventing a single “standard fortification blueprint”** without a source. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Women, families, herds** present in camp politics (sources discuss camp society)—defence is protection of **people and animals**, not only banners. **CHR**

### 8.3 TD defender fantasy that still respects evidence

**Do (evidence-aligned):**
- Concentric **sentry pickets** (arban-scale watch)  
- **Remount pool** inside camp for rapid counter-sortie  
- **Wagon / cart obstacles** as soft walls (known Eurasian steppe practice broadly; specify when using Mongol **CHR**)  
- **Smoke/signal** alerts across decimal chain  
- **Flee upward mobility** — camp can displace; stone tower cannot  

**Don’t:**
- Turn ordu into a medieval concentric stone castle by default (**FOLK**)  
- Assume every camp had identical ditch–palisade–tower stacks without citing a case (**assumption ban**)

**Game-use:** Ordu defence mission = protect herd HP + ger cluster; win by surviving until relief column; lose if khan’s standard tent falls. Enemy AI uses feigned retreat against your sortie—mirror match teaching tool.

## 10. Designer do / don’t

### Do
- Split eras: **early steppe** vs **imperial multiethnic**.  
- Put **decimal organisation** in the UI language (arban→tumen).  
- Make **engineers subject-gated**, not innate Mongol starting kit.  
- Credit **Chinese traction artillery** and **Islamic counterweight** narratives at Xiangyang.  
- Use **feigned retreat, encirclement, scouting** as signature field tools.  
- Show **remounts and pasture** as resources.  
- Treat **Japan** as amphibious limit lesson.  
- Let **ordu defence** be mobile-camp security, not a stone castle clone.  
- Tag film/game tropes **FOLK**.  
- Keep surrender/massacre politics historical but **tonally responsible**.

### Don’t
- Depict one eternal “Horde infantry spam.” **FOLK**  
- Assume every tumen = exactly 10,000 effectives.  
- Give Chinggis **counterweight trebuchets** in 1206.  
- Claim Mongols **invented** the composite bow or feigned retreat uniquely.  
- Ignore **Chinese, Persian, Rus’, Korean** specialists.  
- Make naval conquest of Japan a balanced easy mode.  
- Equate **Marco Polo marvel numbers** with logistics truth.  
- Use **horned shamans + infinite arrow rain** as history. **FOLK**  
- Flatten women of the *Secret History* into mass cavalry units without evidence.  
- Copy **Hollywood siege** without *pao* labour mechanics.  
- Treat *Secret History* speeches as after-action GPS logs.
