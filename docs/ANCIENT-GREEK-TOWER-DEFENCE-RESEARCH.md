# ANCIENT GREEK POWER — Exhaustive Multi-Polis Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Bible)

> Dense notes for a **multi-act / multi-theatre** campaign bible.  
> **Hard rule: Greece is not one kingdom.** “Ancient Greece” is a **cultural-linguistic sphere of hundreds of poleis**, leagues, tyrannies, federations, and later kingdoms — not a unitary ethnostate with one throne.  
> Separate **Mycenaean palaces**, **Archaic city-states**, **Persian War coalitions**, **Athenian naval empire**, **Sparta**, **Thebes**, **Macedon**, and **Hellenistic successor kingdoms** as distinct political systems that share language, gods (with local variants), and competitive honour culture.  
> Companion style to `MAYA-AZTEC-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / `VIKING-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / `CHINESE-IMPERIAL-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / Egyptian / Persian / Roman TD research (same project).

### Source classes (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Archaeology / bioarchaeology / paleoclimate / landscape (Linear B tablets as *administrative* finds, palace destructions, fortification sequences, ship sheds, Long Walls foundations, siege works) — often without a matching “battle diary” |
| **LIT** | Literary historiography & rhetoric: Herodotus, Thucydides, Xenophon, Polybius, Arrian, Diodorus, Plutarch, Pausanias, Demosthenes, etc. — partisan, rhetorical, invaluable; **Homeric epic is literature, not Bronze Age documentary** |
| **INSCR** | Inscriptions: decrees, tribute lists, casualty lists, treaties, dedications, ephebic texts, royal letters — contemporary elite voice, laconic or formulaic |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or live debate (Finley, Snodgrass, Cartledge, Hornblower, Rhodes, Hanson, van Wees, Ober, Worthington, Heckel, Billows, Chaniotis, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Later legend, Romantic Philhellenism, Hollywood 300, Percy Jackson cosmologies, tourist-oracle kitsch — **abilities/skins yes; chronology no** |

**Legend vs history rule for design:** Prefer **LIT** cross-checked with **ARCH/INSCR/MOD** for Classical–Hellenistic “what happened.” Prefer **ARCH** (+ Linear B) for Mycenaean politics; do **not** backfill Homeric heroes into palace ledgers. Use **FOLK** only as optional skin toggles (never silent chronology). No Percy Jackson as history.

**Naming rule:** Prefer **polis names** (Athens, Sparta/Lakedaimon, Thebes, Corinth, Argos, Syracuse…), **ethne** (Boeotians, Thessalians, Aetolians), **leagues** (Delian, Peloponnesian, Achaean, Aetolian), **kingdoms** (Macedon, Antigonid, Seleucid, Ptolemaic). “Greece” / “Hellene” is cultural self-ascription under pressure (**LIT** Herodotus), not a single army.

**Chronology note:** Absolute years for Classical events often rest on **LIT** synchronisms (Olympiads, archon lists) + later chronographers; Mycenaean absolute dating is ceramic + radiocarbon (**ARCH/MOD**). Homeric “Trojan War” date is legendary, not a calendar fact.

**Script / language note:** Classical names in familiar English scholarly forms (Thucydides, Alcibiades, Epaminondas); Linear B terms transliterated (wanax, lawagetas, po-ti-ni-ja). Distinguish **Mycenaean Greek** administrative vocabulary from Classical pantheon labels.

**Design takeaway:** Greek power is a **relay of systems** — palace → polis competition → coalition → naval empire → land hegemony experiments → Macedonian kingdom → Hellenistic military-industrial states — not one continuous “Greek empire” meter.

## 2. STRONGEST PEAKS — Why strong (multiple theatres)

### 2.1 Persian War coalition 480–479 — **Pan-Hellenic defensive coalition peak**
**Why strong**
- **Temporary strategic unity** under existential pressure: Spartan land leadership + Athenian naval mass + Peloponnesian League depth (**LIT** Herodotus).
- **Theatre complementarity:** Thermopylae delay, Artemisium attrition, Salamis geography trap, Plataea decisive land battle, Mycale Ionian front (**LIT**).
- **Ideology weapon:** “freedom of the Greeks” as recruitment and memory tech — later recycled by Athens as imperial cover (**LIT/MOD**).

**Caveat:** Not a lasting state. Immediate post-war rivalry (Sparta vs Athens) proves the coalition was a **single-use ultimate** (**LIT**).

**Game tone:** Multi-player alliance meter; if any major polis defects, Salamis map fails; victory unlocks “Memory of 480” aura that later empires abuse.

### 2.2 Athenian naval empire (mid-5th c., esp. ~460s–430s) — **Trireme + tribute fiscal-military peak**
**Why strong**
- **Sea control:** Trireme fleets, ship sheds at Piraeus, Long Walls making Athens a fortified island (**ARCH/LIT**).
- **Fiscal engine:** Delian League tribute → Athenian navy payroll → coercive empire (**INSCR** tribute lists; Thucydides **LIT**).
- **Democratic manpower:** Rowers as political class; empire and democracy co-produced (**MOD** Ober, Strauss).
- **Soft power:** Building program, festivals, drama as prestige projection (**ARCH/LIT**).

**Why ranked strongest “polis-era imperial” moment:** Closest approach to lasting Greek hegemony *from one city* before Macedon — still a **hegemonic naval league**, not territorial bureaucracy (**MOD**).

**Game tone:** Tribute lanes on Aegean map; revolt waves; Long Walls as keep-link; Pericles “avoid land battle” doctrine card.

### 2.3 Sparta after 404 — **Land hegemony peak (brittle)**
**Why strong**
- **Military brand:** Homoioi phalanx prestige after defeating Athens; Harmosts and garrisons abroad (**LIT** Xenophon).
- **Peloponnesian League** residual structure + Persian gold earlier in Ionian War (**LIT**).
- **Psychological dominance:** Opponents expect Spartan invincibility until Leuctra (**LIT/MOD**).

**Why brittle:** Tiny citizen core; helot dependency; poor naval/fiscal soft power; alienates allies (Corinthian War); Theban occupation blowback (**LIT**). Peak is **hard-power prestige**, not sustainable empire architecture.

**Game tone:** Tiny elite stack with huge aura; helot revolt meter; overextension debuff stacks yearly.

### 2.4 Thebes 371–362 — **Tactical innovation / federal peak**
**Why strong**
- **Leuctra system shock:** Deepened left, Sacred Band, cavalry use — doctrine beats brand (**LIT**).
- **Strategic follow-through:** Liberation of Messenia removes Sparta’s helot base; Messene as anti-Sparta fortress-city (**ARCH/LIT**).
- **Boeotian League** as federal alternative to single-polis empire (**MOD**).

**Caveat:** Depends on Epaminondas/Pelopidas genius; Mantinea 362 wins battle, loses architect (**LIT**). Shortest classic peak.

**Game tone:** Formation editor unlock; “free the helots” mission permanently nerfs Sparta faction.

### 2.5 Alexander 334–323 — **Expeditionary combined-arms peak**
**Why strong**
- **Philip’s inheritance:** Sarissa phalanx + Companion cavalry + siege engineers + Balkan manpower (**LIT/MOD**).
- **Operational tempo:** Granicus → Issus → Tyre → Gaugamela → Central Asia — logistics and boldness (**LIT** Arrian).
- **Fusion politics:** Satrap reuse, city foundations, marriage policy — empire as moving HQ (**LIT/MOD**).

**Caveat:** Personalist; dies without stable succession → Diadochi (**LIT**). Strongest *offensive* Greek-led peak, not a polis peak.

**Game tone:** Mobile royal keep; siege train tech tree; “no heir” sudden death if king falls without succession card.

### 2.6 Hellenistic kingdoms (Antigonid / Seleucid / Ptolemaic) — **Military-tech & siege peaks**
**Why strong**
- **Arms race:** War elephants, advanced torsion artillery, mega-ships (Ptolemaic), professional mercenaries (**LIT** Polybius; **ARCH**).
- **Fiscal scale:** Nile grain (Ptolemies), Asian tax base (Seleucids), Macedonian homeland levy (Antigonids) — beyond Classical polis budgets (**MOD**).
- **Fortress & city networks:** Planned cities, garrison colonies, scientific engineering (**ARCH**).

**Caveat:** Constant Diadochi/league wars; Rome eventually out-institutions them (**LIT** Polybius). Tech peak ≠ eternal victory.

### Peak summary

| Peak | Strength type | Signature systems | Bias warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 480–479 coalition | Existential alliance | Fleet + choke points + shared memory | Temporary; not a state |
| Athenian naval empire | Fiscal-naval hegemony | Tribute, triremes, Long Walls | Ally → subject revolt risk |
| Sparta post-404 | Prestige land hegemony | Harmosts, phalanx aura | Brittle demography |
| Thebes 371–362 | Doctrinal/federal shock | Sacred Band, Messenia liberation | Leader-dependent |
| Alexander 334–323 | Expeditionary empire | Phalanx–cavalry–siege triad | Succession cliff |
| Hellenistic kingdoms | Military-industrial | Artillery, elephants, mega-fleets | Fragmentation & Rome |

## 3. WEAKEST POINTS — Why weak

### 3.1 Stasis (civil war inside the polis) — **Structural solvent**
**Why weak**
- Factional massacre/exile cycles (Corcyra 427 as Thucydides’ paradigm) destroy trust, manpower, and walls from within (**LIT**).
- External powers (Athens, Sparta, Persia, later Macedon/Rome) exploit stasis as entry virus (**LIT/MOD**).

**Design lesson:** “Democracy vs oligarchy” is not flavour text — it is a **map hazard** that opens gates.

### 3.2 Sicilian Expedition 415–413 — **Strategic overreach**
**Why weak**
- Distant amphitheatre; split command (Nicias/Alcibiades/Lamachus); local cavalry inferiority; Syracuse learns Athenian siege craft (**LIT** Thucydides).
- Loss of fleet + hoplites gut empire reserve; Ionian War becomes survivable for Sparta + Persian gold (**LIT**).

### 3.3 404 Athenian defeat — **Naval empire reverse**
**Why weak**
- Aegospotami (405): fleet caught ashore; grain route cut; starvation + Long Walls demolition (**LIT** Xenophon).
- Shows Athenian strength (walls + navy) had a single logistics throat (Black Sea grain via Hellespont).

### 3.4 Chaeronea 338 — **Loss of polis strategic independence**
**Why weak**
- Philip’s system beats Classical coalition improvisation; Sacred Band destroyed; Athens kept alive but subordinated (**LIT**).
- League of Corinth institutionalizes Macedonian hegemony — poleis remain culturally vibrant, strategically junior (**MOD**).

### 3.5 Diadochi fragmentation 323–281 — **Empire without institutional unity**
**Why weak**
- Alexander’s conquest shared among generals; continuous war burns the very resources that made conquest possible (**LIT**).
- Greek mainland becomes a **secondary theatre** fought over by larger kingdoms and leagues (**MOD**).

### 3.6 Roman conquest (168–146, coda 31) — **Institutional predator**
**Why weak**
- Roman manpower, alliances, and persistence outlast Hellenistic kings and leagues; Corinth 146 as exemplary destruction (**LIT** Polybius).
- Greek military culture continues under Rome; *political* autonomy does not (**MOD**).

### 3.7 Other recurring weaknesses
- **Helot / dependent labour risk** (Sparta): victory abroad, fear at home (**LIT**).
- **Persian gold as double agent:** funds Greek wars against each other (**LIT**).
- **Hoplite agrarianism limits** vs cavalry/Asia logistics (**MOD** van Wees/Hanson debate — flag, don’t cartoon).

## 5. MYTHOLOGY FOR ABILITIES — Pantheon carefully tagged (Mycenaean ≠ Classical)

> Separate **Mycenaean religion (poorly known)** from the **Classical Olympian system**. Linear B gives *names and offerings*, not Homeric psychology. Homeric gods are **LIT** epic characters. Mystery cults = light touch. **No Percy Jackson as history** (**FOLK** only if skin-toggled).

### 5.1 Mycenaean religion — unknowns first (**ARCH**)
| Element | What we know | What we do **not** know | TD use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wanax** | Palace king title in Linear B | Exact ritual theology of kingship | Palace-keep buff; lost after collapse |
| **Potnia** (*po-ti-ni-ja*) | “Mistress” divine title; local epithets | Whether = later Athena/Artemis/etc. | Local mistress shrine, not one global goddess |
| Named gods in tablets | Forms related to Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, Hermes, Dionysus?, Athena? appear in Linear B | Continuity details; myths; personalities | Name-seeds only; greyed lore until Classical act |
| Sanctuaries | Peak sanctuaries, caves, palace shrines (**ARCH**) | “Olympian twelve” structure | Pre-pantheon animist fog of war |
| Afterlife / heroes | Sparse; later hero cults may reuse Bronze sites | Homeric underworld as Mycenaean fact | Do not paste Achilles into Pylos |

**Rule:** Mycenaean prologue abilities = **palace logistics, bronze weapons, chariot elites, anonymous Potnia wards** — not full Olympian kit.

### Anti-FOLK misuse checklist (mythology)

## 8. OBSCURE / DEBATED HOOKS (20+)

1. **Linear B military absences:** Tablets track rations, wheels, armour sets — not Homeric battle narratives (**ARCH**).  
2. **“Dorian invasion”:** 19th-c. story; **MOD** prefers complex collapse + later ethnogenesis myths (**LIT** origin tales ≠ archaeology).  
3. **Hoplite “middle-class revolution”:** Snodgrass/Hanson models debated; van Wees argues longer elite dominance (**MOD**).  
4. **Phalanx depth & othismos:** Shoving match vs spear fencing — live debate (**MOD**); design either as stance modes.  
5. **Spartan agoge extremes:** Late sources exaggerate; Classical reality harsher than tourist **FOLK** but not comic (**LIT/MOD**).  
6. **Crypteia:** Helot-terror institution — attestation thin/late; use carefully (**LIT/MOD**).  
7. **Tribute list politics:** ATL shows empire as spreadsheet coercion (**INSCR**).

10. **Persian darics funding Greek wars:** Gold as invisible unit spawner (**LIT**).

12. **Sacred Band:** Elite pair unit — historicity of pair-bonding debated in intensity (**LIT/MOD**).  
13. **Iphicratean peltasts:** Light infantry meta-shift before Philip (**LIT**).  
14. **Epaminondas’ “inverted” order at Leuctra:** Refuses mirror match (**LIT**).

## 9. “DO NOT ASSUME” CHECKLIST (15+)

1. Do **not** assume ancient Greece was one kingdom or one army.  
2. Do **not** treat Homer as a Bronze Age war correspondent.  
3. Do **not** equate Mycenaean *potnia* tablets with fully formed Classical Olympians.  
4. Do **not** assume all Greeks fight as identical hoplites in every century.  
5. Do **not** assume Sparta was always the strongest land power (Leuctra exists).  
6. Do **not** assume Athens = “good democracy” and empire = accidental — coercion is documented (**INSCR/LIT**).  
7. Do **not** assume Persian Wars created lasting Greek unity.  
8. Do **not** assume Herodotus’ numbers are census data.  
9. Do **not** assume Thucydides is neutral camera footage — he is analytical literature (**LIT/MOD**).  
10. Do **not** assume Xenophon’s admiration of Sparta is the whole story.  
11. Do **not** assume Alexander “Hellenized the world” uniformly — local continuities persist (**MOD**).  
12. Do **not** assume Hellenistic = decadent decline; military tech often peaks then.  
13. Do **not** assume Chaeronea ends Greek *culture* — it ends strategic autonomy.  
14. Do **not** assume oracle advice was clear or apolitical.  
15. Do **not** use Percy Jackson, 300, or tourist myths as chronology.  
16. Do **not** make Athena the default buff for Argives, Thebans, and Spartans alike without cult justification.  
17. Do **not** assume “Dorian invasion” as a dated campaign act.  
18. Do **not** assume women, slaves, metics, helots are absent from power systems — they are load-bearing and usually silenced in elite **LIT**.  
19. Do **not** assume Roman victory means Greek military irrelevance overnight — leagues and phalanxes still matter until institutional defeat.  
20. Do **not** merge Seleucid / Ptolemaic / Antigonid into “the Greek Empire.”

## 12. DESIGNER ONE-PAGE — STRONGEST VS WEAKEST

**Strongest when:** existential coalition holds (480–479); tribute + triremes + Long Walls align (Athens mid-5th); doctrine innovates against brand (Leuctra); Macedonian combined arms + siege logistics run hot (Philip/Alexander); Hellenistic treasuries fund artillery/elephants.

**Weakest when:** stasis opens the gates; Sicily-scale overreach burns the fleet; Hellespont grain throat is cut (405–404); brittle Spartan citizen core meets Theban reform; Chaeronea proves polis coalitions cannot match kingdom systems; Diadochi fragment conquest; Rome outlasts leagues and phalanxes (168–146).

**Campaign spine reminder:** Mycenaean prologue → Persian Wars coalition → Golden Age Long Walls → Peloponnesian collapse → Theban/Macedon hinge → Hellenistic siege age — with **every act labelled by polis, league, or kingdom**, never “one Greek king” fantasy.
