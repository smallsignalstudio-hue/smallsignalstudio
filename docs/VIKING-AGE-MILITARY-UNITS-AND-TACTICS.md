# VIKING AGE MILITARY UNITS AND TACTICS — Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Doc)

> English research notes for a tower-defence game bible (same project family as Chinese / Maya / Egyptian TD research).  
> **Method:** Prefer **archaeology** + **contemporary chronicles** (Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Frankish annals, Byzantine texts, Arabic travellers) over Victorian romance, TV, and games. Flag every claim with an evidence class. Do **not** invent TO&Es, regiment sizes, or “standard kit lists.”  
> **Hard myths:** **Horned helmets = FOLK / Victorian stage costume** (not Viking Age combat kit). **Berserkir / úlfheðnar** = separate historicity debate (see §2.4)—do not treat as confirmed battlefield unit type.

### Evidence tags (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Archaeology (graves, settlements, wrecks, weapon finds, fortifications) |
| **CHR** | Contemporary or near-contemporary chronicles / travellers (ASC, Annals of St Bertin, Ibn Fadlan, Byzantine historians, etc.) |
| **SAGA** | Later Icelandic saga / kings’ saga literature (often written decades–centuries after events; literary craft, not after-action reports) |
| **LAW** | Scandinavian law codes (Gulathing, Frostathing, etc.—often 11th–13th c. redactions preserving earlier layers unevenly) |
| **RUN** | Runestones / runic inscriptions |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarship (Price, Roesdahl, Williams, Siddorn, Griffith, Hjardar & Vike, Hedenstierna-Jonson, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Popular myth, Victorian invention, games, TV tropes |

### Core primary / near-primary sources
| Source | Nature / caution |
|---|---|

## 0. Framing: who “Vikings” were

**What the word means.** Old Norse *víkingr* / *í víking* points to **raiding / overseas enterprise**, not an ethnicity or a single people. “Viking Age” is a modern period label (roughly late 8th–mid/late 11th c.) for Scandinavian expansion, trade, settlement, and warfare. **MOD**

**Not a unitary kingdom.** Early Viking Age Scandinavia = patchwork of **local chieftains, petty kings, jarls**, and later consolidating royal powers (Denmark earlier centralization signals; Norway and Sweden different trajectories). Treating “the Viking army” as one national force is false. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Raiders / traders / settlers.** Same populations did all three. Monastery raids (Lindisfarne 793—**CHR**/ASC tradition), slave and silver trade, and settlement (Danelaw, Normandy, Ireland, Iceland, Rus’ river towns) are **coexisting strategies**, not sequential personality types. **CHR** + **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Regional emphases (light, not absolute).**
| Region (broad) | Common modern shorthand | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Danes** | North Sea / English / Frankish coasts; later kingdom-building; Danelaw | Labels in chronicles are outsider ethnonyms—fluid |
| **Norwegians** | Insular west (Scotland, Ireland, Isles); North Atlantic settlement | Overlap with Danish activity |
| **Swedes / eastern Scandinavians** | Baltic–Rus’ rivers toward Byzantium & Islamic silver | “Rus’” identity debated (**CHR** Ibn Fadlan, Byzantine, Arabic + **MOD**) |

**Military social layers (conceptual, not a parade TO&E).**
1. **Bondi / bóndi** — free farmer-householders expected to own weapons and serve when called. Backbone of local defence and later formalized naval levy. **LAW** + **MOD**
2. **Hirð / hird** — king’s or chieftain’s **professional retainer household**; fed, armed, rewarded; higher kit and cohesion than levy. **SAGA** + **LAW** + **MOD**
3. **Huskarl / húskarl** (housecarl) — household warrior; in England under Cnut etc. becomes famous as standing elite (**CHR** / English sources); ON usage overlaps with retainer vocabulary. Do not invent fixed company sizes. **CHR** + **MOD**
4. **Leiđangr / leidang / leding** — **fleet levy** of districts providing ships, crews, and arms for royal/coastal service. Best documented in **later** Norwegian law (**LAW** Gulathing et al.); earlier existence inferred from **SAGA**/skaldic and sparse **CHR**; exact 9th-c. form debated (**MOD** Lund et al.).
5. **Jarl retinues** — regional magnate war-bands; political competition engines. **CHR** + **SAGA** + **RUN**

**Evidence quality (framing).**
| Topic | Quality |
|---|---|
| Raiding + overwintering as strategy | **High** (**CHR** ASC, Frankish; **ARCH** camps) |
| Ship technology | **High** (**ARCH**) |
| Exact unit sizes / drill manuals | **Low** — do not invent |
| Leidang as early Viking Age institution | **Medium–low** for 9th c.; clearer in later law |
| Horned helmets | **False** (**FOLK**) |

**Game-use potential:** Faction is **war-band + ships + seasonal levy**, not “Kingdom of Vikings infantry corps.” Age unlocks: early hit-and-run longship raids → overwintering camps / longphuirt → Danelaw settlement armies → late royal leidang fleets + Varangian prestige unlock.

## 1. Soldier types

### 1.1 Bondi levy (farmer-warriors)

**What.** Free men with land/household obligations; expected minimum arms appear in later law (spear, shield, axe or sword; bows per row-bench in naval levy clauses). **LAW** (Gulathing tradition)

**Role.** Local defence, ship crews, mass of large expeditions when kings can compel. Quality and armour highly variable—most burials and texts imply **shield + spear** as common floor, not mail+helmet. **ARCH** + **LAW** + **MOD**

**Controversies.** Projecting 12th–13th c. law kit lists onto 793–850 raids is anachronistic. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Cheap numerous melee; poor armour; strong when massed in shield-wall; weak alone vs towers.

### 1.2 Hirðmenn / huskarlar (retainers)

**What.** Full-time or near-full-time household warriors of kings/jarls; loyalty tied to gift-giving, feasting, honour. Higher chance of swords, mail, helmets—still **elite-expensive**, not universal. **SAGA** + **ARCH** (rich weapon graves) + **MOD**

**England note.** Cnut’s housecarls become a named standing element in English politics (**CHR**); do not back-port English late housecarl image to all 9th-c. Scandinavian crews.

**Game-use:** Medium-cost durable infantry; aura buff to nearby bondi; expensive to replace.

### 1.3 Jarl / chieftain retinues

**What.** Personal followings of regional lords; core of early Viking Age expedition leadership before strong kingship. **CHR** + **SAGA** + **RUN**

**Game-use:** Hero units / faction leaders; recruitment buildings = halls, not barracks blocks.

### 1.4 Berserkir and úlfheðnar — LITERARY vs archaeological debate (flag separately)

**Literary picture.** Sagas and skaldic tradition describe *berserkir* (“bear-shirts” etymology debated) and *úlfheðnar* (“wolf-coats”) as frenzied elite fighters, sometimes Odin-linked, sometimes lawless duellists. **SAGA**

**Archaeology.** No confirmed “berserker uniform” kit assemblage. Animal-warrior motifs exist in art (Migration Period onward; Vendel/Viking iconography)—**ritual/ideological**, not proof of a standing frenzy corps. **ARCH** + **MOD** (Price *Viking Way* discusses seiðr, war magic, animal warrior ideology carefully)

**Historicity debate (do not resolve falsely).**
| Position | Summary | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Cultic / initiatory warriors | Real social role with ritual ecstasy | **MOD** (argued) |
| Elite champion rhetoric | Literary exaggeration of brave retainers | **SAGA**/sceptical **MOD** |
| Post-hoc outlaw stereotype | Later legal/social category projected backward | **LAW** + **MOD** |
| TV / game “steroid unit” | Fantasy | **FOLK** |

**“Bite the shield”.** Saga performance motif of frenzy—not a drill manual. **SAGA** / **FOLK** if presented as standard tactic.

**Game-use:** If used, label as **legendary / contested unlock**, high risk–high reward, not a core historical roster staple. Prefer “Odinic champion” flavour over werewolf armour.

### 1.5 Thralls (þræll)

**What.** Enslaved people—major product of raids (**CHR** + **ARCH** slave economies). Generally **non-combat labour** / status property; not a soldier class. Occasional coerced rowing or camp labour is plausible but **not** a documented thrall phalanx. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Economy / build speed / moral hazard events—not a combat line unit.

### 1.6 Varangian Guard (late elite career path)

**What.** Byzantine imperial guard/tagma recruiting northerners (and later Anglo-Saxons) from 10th c.; axe-famous in Byzantine and Norse memory. **CHR** (Byzantine) + **RUN** (Swedish stones mentioning Greece/ Byzantium service) + **SAGA** (Harald Hardrada cycle—literary)

**Placement.** **Late** Viking Age prestige path, not an early Lindisfarne-era unit. Service abroad ≠ “Viking national guard.”

**Game-use:** Endgame elite mercenary unlocked via eastern trade route; Byzantine gold economy sink.

### 1.7 Women and war (brief, evidence-led)

**What.** Most combatants in texts and weapon graves read as male; rare weapon graves with female osteology and the Birka Bj.581 debate show **complexity**, not a Valkyrie army. **ARCH** + **MOD** (Price et al. on Bj.581). Do not invent shieldmaiden TO&E; do not erase anomaly graves.

**Game-use:** Optional rare hero / narrative—never default mass unit.

## 2. Weapons and protective equipment

### 2.1 Spear — primary weapon

**What.** Most common battlefield arm: thrusting spear for line fighting; lighter throwing spears/javelins also attested. Socketed iron heads abundant in graves. **ARCH** + **LAW** (mandatory kit item) + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Default bondi / line unit weapon; reach bonus in shield-wall.

### 2.2 Axe — bearded axe, “Danish” / broad axe debates

**What.**
- **Bearded axe (skeggøx)**: lower blade “beard” extends cutting edge; tool–weapon continuum; hooking possible. **ARCH** + **MOD**
- **Broad / long-hafted fighting axe**: two-handed forms appear in late Viking / early medieval contexts; English sources associate heavy axes with Scandinavian/housecarl fighters in 11th c.—**do not** call every axe a “Danish axe” as a formal type-name without care. **CHR** + **ARCH** + **MOD**
- Tool axes vs war axes: overlap is real; context (grave wealth, shaft length, blade form) matters. **ARCH**

**Controversies.** Popular “every Viking dual-wields Dane axes” = **FOLK**. One-handed axe + shield is common reconstruction; two-handed axe implies different shield use / status / period. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Cheap armour-crack vs shield durability; late huskarl two-handed axe as anti-shield-wall specialist.

### 2.3 Sword — prestige sidearm

**What.** Pattern-welded and later more homogeneous blades; often named, inherited, high-status. Petersen typology still foundational for hilt forms. **ARCH** + **SAGA** (named swords—literary) + **MOD**

**Not** the numerical primary weapon of levy masses—spears/axes dominate counts. **ARCH**

**Game-use:** Officer / hirð unlock; high cost; morale aura.

### 2.4 Seax / sax

**What.** Single-edged knife/short sword continuum; utility and backup weapon across North Sea cultures (not uniquely “Viking”). **ARCH**

**Game-use:** Secondary attack after spear break; stealthed camp raid tool flavour.

### 2.5 Bow and arrows

**What.** Bows required in some levy clauses (per thwart/row-bench). **LAW** Hunting and war archery both exist; mass English-style longbow armies are **not** the Scandinavian signature. Arrowheads common archaeologically. **ARCH** + **LAW**

**Ship-borne archery.** Plausible harassment from ships/riverbanks (**CHR** siege/raid narratives imply missile fire; exact drill sparse). **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Support unit / ship ability; not Chinese-crossbow equivalent mass doctrine.

### 2.6 Sling?

**What.** Possible as low-status/skirmish missile (universal Old World weapon) but **poorly evidenced** as a named Viking Age specialty. Treat as optional flavour, not roster core. **MOD** (argument from absence) / weak **ARCH**

### 2.7 Javelin / throwing spear

**What.** Light spears for opening volleys before contact—common early medieval pattern; ON vocabulary and finds support throwing heads. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Opening volley ability before melee lock.

### 2.8 Shield — round, boss, construction

**What.** Typically **round**, wood planks, central **iron boss** (*umbo*), handgrip behind boss; leather/rawhide facing debated in extent; painted faces attested in art/texts. Diameter commonly reconstructed ~80–100 cm range from finds/fragments—avoid fake precision. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Use.** Individual defence **and** formation fighting; active punching/angling emphasized by some experimental archaeologists vs purely static “wall.” **MOD** (Warming et al. debate)

**Game-use:** Core defensive stat; shield-wall formation ability; boss-bash stun.

### 2.9 Mail (hringserkr / brynja)

**What.** Iron ring mail—**expensive**, elite. Gjermundbu grave includes mail context; rings appear at Birka and elsewhere but full shirts rare in burial survival. **ARCH**

**Game-use:** Rare heavy upgrade for hirð; economy sink.

### 2.10 Lamellar — Birka / eastern contact

**What.** Hundreds of iron lamellae from Birka’s **Garrison** (c. early–mid 10th c.); typology points to **steppe / Turkic / eastern** armour tradition, not “standard Norse kit.” **ARCH** (Stjerna; Hedenstierna-Jonson) + **MOD**

**Controversy.** One suit vs scrap recycling debates exist; either way, **not** evidence that Scandinavian armies were lamellar-armoured. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Eastern-contact cosmetic / rare drop for Rus’ route factions—not default Viking armour.

### 2.11 Helmets — Gjermundbu and spectacle tradition

**What.**
- **Gjermundbu helmet** (Norway, late 10th c. grave): best-known reconstructable Viking Age **spectacle** (ocular) helmet; fragmented, restored; elite context with weapons/mail. **ARCH**
- Other spectacle fragments: Tjele (Denmark), Lokrume (Gotland), Kyiv, etc.—show tradition continuity from earlier Vendel/Valsgärde spectacle helmets. **ARCH**
- Yarm (England) and other finds complicate “only one helmet” popular claims—say **extremely rare archaeologically**, not “nobody wore helmets.” Organic/leather heads possible but hard to prove. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Horned helmets.** 19th-c. romantic / Wagnerian costume. **FOLK**. Pre-Viking ritual horned figures exist in art—**not** Viking combat helms. **ARCH**/art ≠ battle kit.

**Game-use:** Helmet = rare elite visual; never horns. Spectacle helm as legendary artefact unlock.

## 3. Formations and tactics

### 3.1 Skjaldborg / shield-wall

**What.** ON *skjaldborg* (“shield-fort”) and cognate early medieval shield-walls: dense infantry line presenting shields, spears projecting. Attested as a **concept** in Norse literature and parallel Germanic practice. **SAGA** + **CHR** (English/Frankish battle language) + **MOD**

**Debate.** How static vs dynamic; how tightly overlapped; how long sustained. Experimental work argues active shield use, not a passive stockade. **MOD**

**Evidence quality:** High for **close-order shield infantry fighting** as normal; medium for choreographed “interlock drill.”

**Game-use:** Default defensive formation—damage reduction frontal, weak flanks/rear, slow move.

### 3.2 Svinfylking — “boar snout” wedge

**What.** Literary formation: wedge (“boar’s snout”) to pierce a line; described in sagas and later medieval military lore; sometimes linked to Odinic tactics in tradition. **SAGA** + later Latin/medieval theory

**Historicity.** Plausible as a **named idea** for a dense assault column/wedge; **not** confirmed as parade-ground standard with fixed angles. Treat as literary–theoretical. **MOD** caution

**Game-use:** Costly temporary formation to break a wall—high casualties if failed (matches “wedge gamble” fiction).

### 3.3 Feigned flight

**What.** Chroniclers sometimes accuse Scandinavian/Norman forces of feigned retreats (Norman cavalry at Hastings is the famous **CHR** case—Norman, not pure “Viking infantry”). For Viking Age Scandinavians, feigned flight appears in narrative toolkits but is easy for monastic writers to allege. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Risky special ability with morale checks—label as debated stratagem.

### 3.4 Raid tactics

**Pattern (well evidenced):**
1. Coastal/riverine approach by ship. **CHR** + **ARCH**
2. Strike rich, poorly defended targets (monasteries, open towns). **CHR**
3. Seize movable wealth and captives. **CHR**
4. Withdraw before kingdom-scale field armies concentrate—or take tribute (*Danegeld*). **CHR** ASC
5. From mid-9th c., **overwintering** in fortified/seasonal camps, then campaigning inland. **CHR** + **ARCH** (Repton, Torksey, etc.)

**Game-use:** Attackers win by tempo and loot objectives, not always by destroying the keep; defenders win by early warning, fortified burhs, denying beach time.

### 3.5 Amphibious assault

**What.** Beach, row into estuaries, up rivers (Seine, Loire, Thames, Irish rivers, east European river roads). Ships are the operational mobility system. **CHR** + **ARCH**

**Game-use:** Water edges = spawn lanes; portage tokens move ships between rivers.

### 3.6 Ship-borne archery / missiles

**What.** Missile harassment from ships during river sieges and coastal attacks appears in chronicle narratives; details thin. **CHR** + **MOD**

### 3.7 Cavalry rarity vs later adoption

**Scandinavia.** Viking Age Scandinavian warfare is **primarily infantry + ships**. Horses used for travel/status; mounted fighting less central than in Frankish/Norman worlds. Riding gear appears in rich graves (e.g. Gjermundbu context includes riding equipment)—elite mobility ≠ cavalry army. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Abroad / later.** Danelaw and especially **Normandy** show Scandinavian elites adopting continental cavalry culture; 11th-c. Norman knighthood is a **successor synthesis**, not “Vikings on horses with horns.” **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Cavalry = late cultural-assimilation unlock for Norman/Danelaw factions, not core early roster.

## 4. Ships as a weapons system

### 4.1 Longship (*langskip*) and related terms

**What.** Clinker-built, shallow-draft war/transport oared ships with sail; flexible crews. Archaeology: Gokstad, Skuldelev warship types, etc. **ARCH**

**Terminology caution.**
| Term | Notes | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| *langskip* | Generic “long ship” | ON / **SAGA** |
| *skeið* | Long warship class term in texts | **SAGA**/ON |
| *dreki* / dragon | Poetic/prow-ornament association; not a hull TO&E type for every ship | **SAGA** + **FOLK** overuse |
| *knarr* | Broader, deeper **cargo** ocean trader | **ARCH** + ON |

Do not treat saga ship-name poetry as naval registry.

### 4.2 Beaching and surprise

**What.** Shallow draft allows beach landings close to targets—core operational advantage vs deep-keel southern warships in many coastal zones. **ARCH** + **MOD** + **CHR** patterns

**Game-use:** Beach assault points; tidal timers.

### 4.3 Portage

**What.** Hauling ships over land between waterways—especially critical on **Rus’** river routes (and attested as a technique in northern warfare traditions). **CHR**/Arabic–Byzantine geographic traditions + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Special movement over isthmus tiles at high labour cost.

### 4.4 River warfare (east)

**What.** Swedish/Rus’ movement via rivers/lakes toward Black Sea/Caspian; combination of trade, raid, and polity formation (novgorod/kiev debates). **CHR** (Byzantine, Arabic) + **ARCH** + **RUN**

### 4.5 Fleet sizes in chronicles

**What.** ASC and Frankish annals give ship counts (hundreds, 350, etc.). Treat as **order-of-magnitude / rhetorical**; chroniclers inflate; “ship” ≠ constant crew size. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Wave size flavour text ≠ literal spawn math.

### 4.6 Naval battle

**What.** Lashing ships together, fighting as floating platforms, appears in Norse battle tradition (e.g. literary accounts of major sea battles). **SAGA** + **MOD**; archaeological proof of specific battle tactics limited.

## 6. Best attack methods vs best defence methods (TD design)

### 6.1 Best attack methods (Viking / Norse aggressor toolkit)

1. **Mobility strike:** spawn from water, hit economy buildings (monastery/town), extract loot before boss fight. Matches raid logic. **CHR**
2. **Multi-point beach landings:** force defender to split towers. **CHR** pattern
3. **River corridor push:** ignore front gate; win via estuary lane. **CHR**
4. **Overwintering meta:** after first raid wave, build a **camp** on-map that reduces travel time / enables heavier follow-up (Great Army model). **CHR** + **ARCH**
5. **Tribute win condition:** threaten destruction until defender pays gold (Danegeld)—political victory. **CHR** ASC
6. **Shield-wall grind** only when forced into field battle; not always optimal. **MOD**
7. **Night/fog raid flavour** from chronicle surprise rhetoric—use lightly. **CHR**
8. **Target soft sacred sites** first (high loot, low towers)—historically accurate, ethically label as raiding violence in UX. **CHR**
9. **Alliance / mercenary mix** (Irish kings hiring Norse, etc.)—attacker can buy local guides. **CHR**
10. **Late axe huskarls** as anti-wall specialists once camps established. **CHR**/11th-c. image + **MOD**

### 6.2 Best defence methods (against Viking Age attackers)

1. **Early warning + fortified refuge (burh model):** civilians and wealth inside walls; deny easy loot. **CHR** + **ARCH**
2. **Guard the water:** chain booms, piles, harbour chains, river stakes—force beaching into kill zones. **ARCH** + **CHR**
3. **Deny beaches:** coastal watch towers, rapid response fyrd/levy. **CHR** English + Frankish bridge/fort responses
4. **Bridge forts / river blocking** (Frankish Seine defence adaptations). **CHR**
5. **Refuse open battle** until attacker is land-bound and hungry; cut forage. **MOD** reading of successful responses
6. **Pay tribute as stall**—buys time, not permanent safety (ASC cycle). **CHR**
7. **Hardened churches/monasteries** (historical lag—many were soft targets; game can show upgrade path after first burn). **CHR**
8. **Counter-raiding fleets** once kingdom organizes leidang-like response (late). **LAW** + **SAGA**
9. **Cavalry response** where terrain/culture allows (Frankish/Norman)—chase raiders inland. **CHR**
10. **Winter campaigning against camps** (English recoveries; digging out overwinterers). **CHR** + **ARCH**

## 7. Obscure / gameable details (20+)

Each item tagged; contested items marked.

1. **Raven banner (*hrafnsmerki*).** Appears in English/Irish/Norse tradition as a magical victory/defeat omen banner. **CHR**/insular + **SAGA**; historicity of “living raven woven” details dubious. **MOD** caution  
2. **Landwaster (*Landøyðan*).** Name of Harald Hardrada’s banner in saga tradition. **SAGA**

6. **Holmgang.** Formal duel on a marked ground/skin; legal violence channel. **LAW** + **SAGA**  
7. **Berserker “bite the shield”.** Literary frenzy gesture. **SAGA** / **FOLK** if universalized

9. **Danegeld / heregeld.** Pay-off mechanics. **CHR** ASC  
10. **Ship burial / boat grave ideology.** Status theatre (Oseberg, Gokstad, Ladby, etc.)—not combat tactic but culture unlock. **ARCH**  
11. **Ulfberht swords.** Inscribed high-quality blades circulating in the North—trade/tech prestige. **ARCH**  
12. **Jelling stones / royal runic propaganda.** State formation messaging under Harald Bluetooth. **RUN**  
13. **Sunken ship harbour defences / piles.** Passive naval defence. **ARCH**  
14. **Birka Garrison eastern kit.** Lamellar, bows, martial elite cosmopolitanism. **ARCH**

## 8. Designer do / don’t

### Do
- Put the **ship** at the centre of operations (spawn, logistics, retreat).  
- Show **social mix**: bondi levy + hirð elites + jarl politics.  
- Keep **mail and helmets rare**; spears/shields common.  
- Use **ASC/Frankish** raid–overwinter–tribute cycles as campaign structure.  
- Flag **berserkers**, **blood-eagle**, **svinfylking**, **feigned flight** as contested/literary where used.  
- Differentiate **Danish / Norwegian / Swedish–Rus’** theatres lightly via maps and unlocks.  
- Let defenders win with **burhs, river chains, watch, refusal of open battle**.  
- Treat **Varangians** as late eastern career prestige.  
- Cite evidence classes in internal design docs when locking “flavour facts.”

### Don’t
- **Horned helmets.** Ever. (**FOLK**)  
- Invent **regimental TO&Es**, company numbers, or “standard issue loadouts” for all warriors.  
- Make **berserkers** a primary historical infantry class.  
- Equate **TV Vikings** costumes with archaeology.  
- Present **blood-eagle** as documented common rite.  
- Give every warrior a **pattern-welded sword + lamellar + spectacle helm**.  
- Depict early Vikings as a **unified national army** with modern ranks.  
- Overstate **Scandinavian cavalry**.  
- Treat **saga speeches** as after-action reports.  
- Use **dreki** as a technical hull class for every longship.  
- Flatten thralls into comic sidekicks or combat battalions.  
- Assume **leidang law codes** describe 793 kit exactly.
