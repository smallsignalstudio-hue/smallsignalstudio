# JAPANESE POWER — Exhaustive Multi-Period Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Bible)

> Dense notes for a **multi-act / multi-regime** campaign bible.  
> **Hard rule: Japan is not one kingdom forever.** “Japan” is a **long archipelago political tradition** that passes through **Yamato kingship**, **ritsuryō imperial bureaucracy**, **Heian court aristocracy**, **Kamakura bakufu**, **Muromachi (Ashikaga) shogunate**, **Sengoku warlord polycentrism**, **unification under Nobunaga–Hideyoshi–Ieyasu**, and **Edo Tokugawa peace** — not a unitary ethnostate with one continuous army doctrine.  
> Separate **court (kuge)**, **warrior governments (bakufu)**, **provincial warriors (bushi / kokujin)**, **ikki leagues**, **temple-shrine complexes**, and **domain (han)** systems as distinct power stacks that share language, kami cults (with local variants), and competitive honour culture.  
> Companion style to `VIKING-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / `CHINESE-IMPERIAL-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / `ANCIENT-GREEK-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / Maya–Aztec / Egyptian / Persian / Roman TD research (same project).

### Source classes (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Archaeology / landscape / castle archaeology / paleoclimate / material culture (kofun tombs, *jōkamachi* plans, castle earthworks, Mongol invasion sites, arquebus finds, Edo urban layers) — often without a matching “battle diary” |
| **LIT** | Literary historiography, chronicles, war tales, diaries, legal codes: *Nihon shoki*, *Kojiki*, *Shoku Nihongi*, *Heike monogatari*, *Taiheiki*, *Azuma kagami*, *Gukanshō*, war letters, domain chronicles, Jesuit reports — partisan, rhetorical, invaluable; epic war tales are literature, not after-action reports |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or live debate (Farris, Friday, Conlan, Berry, Hall, Totman, Turnbull caution, Asakawa, Mass, Goble, Pantzer, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Later legend, *kabuki*/film samurai kitsch, anime kami cosmologies, tourist ninja villages, “kamikaze as guaranteed divine wind” folklore, yokai encyclopedias as combat classes — **abilities/skins yes; chronology no** |

**Legend vs history rule for design:** Prefer **LIT** cross-checked with **ARCH/MOD** for “what happened.” Prefer **ARCH** for Kofun politics and castle geometry; do **not** backfill *Kojiki* gods into Sengoku TO&Es. Use **FOLK** only as optional skin toggles (never silent chronology). No anime pantheon as history.

**Naming rule:** Prefer **period + regime** (Yamato, Heian court, Kamakura bakufu, Muromachi, Sengoku daimyō X, Tokugawa bakufu). “Samurai Japan” is a pop label, not a single faction. Prefer **uji / clan names** (Taira, Minamoto, Hōjō, Ashikaga, Oda, Toyotomi, Tokugawa), **temple complexes** (Enryaku-ji, Ishiyama Hongan-ji), **domains**.

**Chronology note:** Traditional era names (*nengō*) and court chronicles give year-anchors from the classical period onward; early Yamato absolute dating leans on **ARCH** (kofun typology, Chinese/Korean synchronisms) + **LIT** (*Nihon shoki* teleology). Sengoku battle details often rest on later domain histories and war tales — inflate carefully (**MOD**).

**Script / language note:** Familiar English scholarly forms (Nobunaga, Hideyoshi, Ieyasu, Yoshitsune); Japanese terms italicised on first use (*bakufu*, *shōgun*, *daimyō*, *ashigaru*, *jōkamachi*). Distinguish **Shinto kami cult** (local, shrine-based) from **Buddhist warrior deities** and from **FOLK yokai skin packs**.

## 2. STRONGEST PEAKS — Why strong (multiple regimes)

### 2.1 Late Kofun / early Yamato consolidation (c. 5th–7th c.) — **Monumental elite peak**
**Why strong**
- **Landscape ideology:** Keyhole *kofun* broadcast hierarchy across the Kinai and beyond (**ARCH**).
- **Continental military package:** Horse gear, iron weapons, and peninsula connections raise coercive capacity (**ARCH/LIT**).
- **Uji coordination under Yamato paramountcy:** Not a modern nation, but a **hegemonic core** capable of projecting into frontiers (**MOD**).

**Why ranked an early peak:** Visible **material kingship** before later court novels matter. Still multipolar among uji (**MOD**).

**Game tone:** Tomb-keep prestige; horse unlock; Korean/Chinese tech trade routes as research lanes.

### 2.2 Nara–early Heian ritsuryō state (late 7th–9th c.) — **Administrative military peak**
**Why strong**
- Codified corvée, provincial governors, and frontier armies (Emishi wars) under an imperial legal OS (**LIT**).
- Capital planning and temple-state complexes as **logistics + ideology keeps** (**ARCH**).
- Writing, census, and tax claims — paper power that later warriors will hijack (**MOD**).

**Caveat:** On-the-ground collection always leaky; “ritsuryō army” is not a standing national conscript clone of modern states (**MOD** Farris).

**Game tone:** Levy meters; provincial rebellion waves; Emishi forest maps as attrition theatres.

### 2.3 Kamakura bakufu after Genpei + Hōjō law (1180s–mid-13th c.) — **Warrior government peak**
**Why strong**
- **Dual polity:** Court remains sacral/cultural; bakufu monopolises warrior discipline and estate enforcement (**LIT/MOD**).
- *Shugo/jitō* network = distributed tower governors on proprietary landscapes (**LIT**).
- *Goseibai shikimoku* (1232) — law as cohesion tech after succession shocks (**LIT**).
- Minamoto brand + Hōjō regency pragmatism: violence turned into **office** (**MOD**).

**Why ranked strongest “bushi as state” moment before Sengoku:** Institutionalised warrior rule without yet dissolving into total warlord polycentrism.

**Game tone:** Estate lawsuits as side missions; loyalty contracts; east capital (Kamakura) vs Kyoto tension meter.

### 2.4 Mongol defence of Kyushu (1274 / 1281) — **Existential coastal defence peak**
**Why strong**
- Rapid coastal fortification, stone walls at Hakata, coordinated vassal response under bakufu pressure (**LIT/ARCH**).
- Fighting on home littoral with short supply vs amphibious invaders (**MOD**).
- Psychological cohesion narrative (later “divine wind”) — useful as **morale tech**, dangerous as history (**FOLK/MOD**).

**Why a peak:** Rare moment of **near-national defensive coordination** under Kamakura. Cost: reward crisis afterward (see weaknesses).

**Game tone:** Beach towers; ship-burn objectives; storm as **RNG modifier tagged FOLK/MOD**, not auto-win cheat.

### 2.5 Sengoku daimyō consolidation + Nobunaga–Hideyoshi unifiers (mid–late 16th c.) — **Hard-power military peak**
**Why strong**
- **Castle + *jōkamachi* systems:** Integrated military-urban keeps (**ARCH**).
- **Ashigaru mass + firearms:** Scale shift from elite mounted archery ideals toward organised infantry fire (**LIT/ARCH/MOD**).
- Nobunaga’s ruthlessness toward temple-military rivals; Hideyoshi’s surveys, sword hunt, hostage/class policies — convert conquest into **admin lock** (**LIT/MOD** Berry).
- Logistics, river/road control, and alliance betrayal mastery define winners more than “honour duels” (**MOD**).

**Why often the TD “main fantasy” peak:** Highest density of recognisable battles, keeps, and unit variety — still **not** a single kingdom until the unifiers force it.

**Game tone:** Domain economy; gunpowder tech tree; temple league bosses; Korea expedition as optional overreach act.

### 2.6 Sekigahara → Osaka destruction → early Tokugawa settlement (1600–1615+) — **Regime-finaliser peak**
**Why strong**
- Ieyasu’s coalition management at **Sekigahara** (defection warfare) (**LIT/MOD**).
- Osaka campaigns remove the last Toyotomi alternative keep (**LIT/ARCH**).
- Early Edo builds *bakuhan* freeze: castles limited, *daimyō* reshuffled, violence monopolised (**LIT**).

**Caveat:** Peak of **political settlement**, not of field-army creativity — leads directly into atrophy coda.

**Game tone:** Betrayal cards; final siege map; unlock “peace laws” that nerf future builds.

### Peak summary

| Peak | Strength type | Signature systems | Bias warning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late Kofun / Yamato | Monumental hegemony | Tombs, horse gear, uji coalition | Not modern Japan |
| Ritsuryō Nara–early Heian | Admin-military | Codes, capitals, Emishi frontiers | Leaky local control |
| Kamakura bakufu / Hōjō | Warrior law-state | *Shugo/jitō*, shikimoku | Court still exists |
| Mongol defence 1274/81 | Coastal existential | Hakata walls, vassal levy | Storm ≠ sole cause |
| Sengoku → Nobunaga/Hideyoshi | Castle-gun unification | *Jōkamachi*, ashigaru, surveys | Polycentrism until forced |
| Sekigahara–Osaka–early Edo | Settlement monopoly | Coalition flip, siege endgame, freeze laws | Peace kills military edge |

## 3. WEAKEST POINTS — Why weak

### 3.1 Late Heian provincial leakage / Taira overreach (12th c.) — **Court–warrior mismatch**
**Why weak**
- Court culture peaks while coercive muscle shifts to provincial warriors (**LIT/MOD**).
- Taira dominance in capital alienates rival networks → Genpei backlash (**LIT**).
- Naval-aristocratic Taira power collapses at **Dannoura** when coalition and logistics fail (**LIT**).
- **Design:** Playing “court prestige only” without warrior contracts loses the map.

### 3.2 Post-Mongol reward crisis (1280s–1333) — **Victory without solvency**
**Why weak (for Kamakura)**
- Defence success creates unpaid claims; bakufu cannot distribute enough estates (**LIT/MOD** Conlan).
- Legitimacy erodes; Go-Daigo and Ashikaga exploit fractures (**LIT**).
- **Design lesson:** Winning coastal waves without treasury/estate payout spawns rebel spawners inland.

### 3.3 Ōnin War & early Sengoku (1467+) — **Centre deleted**
**Why weak (for “national” defence fantasy)**
- Kyoto as hub burns/politically fragments; Ashikaga shōguns become puppets or bystanders (**LIT/ARCH**).
- Authority atomises to daimyō, *ikki*, temples — **no single player keep** (**MOD**).
- **Design:** Hub world destroyed; campaign shifts to regional ladders.

### 3.4 Imjin War overreach (1592–1598) — **Expeditionary logistics failure**
**Why weak**
- Japanese forces win early battles but cannot secure lasting occupation against Korean resistance + Ming intervention (**LIT/ARCH/MOD**).
- Naval denial (Yi Sun-sin tradition) and fortress networks choke supply (**LIT/MOD**).
- Hideyoshi’s death collapses political will (**LIT**).
- **Design:** Overseas act with naval interdiction meter; home domains strip-mined for little permanent gain.

### 3.5 Coalition fragility at Sekigahara (1600) — **Alliance as glass cannon**
**Why weak (for Western army)**
- Paper superiority undone by **side-switching**, delayed commits, command disunity (**LIT/MOD**).
- Field position and trust matter more than banner counts (**MOD**).
- **Design:** Betrayal timer; fog-of-loyalty; not a pure DPS check.

### 3.6 Edo peace military atrophy (17th–mid-19th c.) — **TD coda / soft decline**
**Why weak (as war machine)**
- *Sankin-kōtai*, castle restrictions, and status freeze convert bushi into **stipended urban consumers/administrators** (**LIT/MOD** Totman et al.).
- Skill fade in large-scale combined-arms; coastal defence ossifies until bakumatsu shock (**MOD**).
- Peace is a **design feature**, not a failure of story — the Tokugawa win condition *is* demilitarisation of rivals.
- **Design:** After Osaka, tower HP remains but **unit XP caps**, gunpowder drills become festivals, and “wave threat” shifts to famine/riot/foreign ships (epilogue).

### 3.7 Temple–league blind spots (Sengoku) — **Ideology keeps that bleed states**
**Why weak (for daimyō who ignore them)**
- Enryaku-ji, Hongan-ji, and *ikkō-ikki* mobilise populations outside samurai etiquette (**LIT/MOD**).
- Nobunaga’s brutal solutions show the cost of letting religious keeps scale (**LIT**).
- **Design:** Neutral sacred zones that spawn zealots if neglected.

### Kamikaze debates (mandatory design flag)
- Contemporary and near sources mention storms damaging fleets; later ideology elevates **kamikaze** (“divine wind”) as kami intervention (**LIT → FOLK** trajectory).
- **MOD** consensus: weather mattered **and** Japanese defence, walls, logistics, and Mongol/Koryŏ operational limits mattered. Storms were not a scheduled superweapon.
- **Design rule:** Storm is a **weather card** with historicity slider — never a silent auto-clear of the 1281 map.

## 5. MYTHOLOGY FOR ABILITIES — Kami, Buddhist warrior gods, yokai (carefully tagged)

> Do **not** treat anime pantheons, *Okami*-style pop Shinto, or tourist ninja magic as medieval force structure.  
> Separate **Shinto kami cults** (shrine networks, *uji* ancestors, state ritual), **Buddhist deities and wisdom kings** (temple armies, talismans), and **FOLK yokai/oni skins** (Edo encyclopedias, *kabuki*, modern media).  
> Locality matters: Hachiman of Usa ≠ generic “war god card” without shrine politics (**MOD**).

### 5.3 Oni / yokai — FOLK skins only (careful)

| Motif | Use in TD | Do not do |
|---|---|---|
| **Oni** | Oni masks as terror cosmetics; *setsubun* seasonal event waves | Treat as a historical infantry class in Genpei TO&E |
| **Tengu** | Mountain ambush skin for warrior-monk elites | Claim tengu trained Ushiwakamaru as chronology |
| **Kitsune** | Inari-linked scout illusions (FOLK toggle) | Replace logistics with “fox magic” as unmarked history |
| **Yūrei / onryō** | Horror map modifier after massacres (e.g. temple burns) | Make ghosts decide Sekigahara |
| **Kappa / miscellaneous Edo yokai** | Optional comedy/horror DLC lanes | Stuff 12th-c. battles with *hyakki yagyō* as fact |

**Anti-FOLK misuse checklist (mythology)**
1. Amaterasu did not personally command at Nagashino.  
2. “Kamikaze” is not a kami ability with a fixed cooldown in 1281.  
3. Yokai encyclopedias (e.g. later illustrated bestiaries) ≠ Kamakura field manuals.  
4. Ninja magic / *Naruto* cosmologies = **FOLK**.  
5. Syncretism (*honji suijaku*) means kami–Buddha overlays are historical theology — fine if labelled, not if mashed into one anime roster.

## 6. HISTORICAL FIGURES — Heroes / bosses / antagonists

| Figure | Theatre | Dates (approx.) | Role | Evidence / caution |

6. **Kamikaze did not solo-win 1281** — weather + defence + logistics (**MOD**).  
7. **Mongol invasions ≠ proof of eternal divine protection aura** — unpaid vassals topple Kamakura later.  
8. **Ashikaga shōgun ≠ Sengoku supreme commander** after Ōnin.

10. **Nobunaga “demon king” is partly later brand** — use as skin, not sole personality OS (**FOLK**).  
11. **Hideyoshi’s peace at home ≠ competence in Korea** — Imjin is a different skill check.  
12. **Sekigahara was not a simple East numbers crush West** — defections decide (**MOD**).  
13. **Osaka Winter “truce” was not kind** — moat fill is a weapon.  
14. **Edo samurai were not constantly duelling in streets as military practice** — bureaucracy + status theatre (**MOD**).  
15. **Sakoku was not absolute airtight isolation** — controlled trade windows (**MOD**).  
16. **Ninja were not a mass army class with magic** — espionage/irregulars unevenly attested; pop ninja = **FOLK**.  
17. **Oni/yokai are not historical unit types** — **FOLK** skins only.  
18. **Amaterasu / Hachiman buffs are cult-ideology systems**, not documented DPS auras at named battles.  
19. **Temple monks can be soldiers** — but glowing Myōō ultimates need labels.

## 12. DESIGNER ONE-PAGE — STRONGEST VS WEAKEST

**Strongest when:** regime OS matches the fight — ritsuryō levies on frontiers; Kamakura contracts on estates; Hakata walls against amphibious shock; Sengoku castles + ashigaru guns + surveys; Ieyasu-style coalition betrayal mastery; early Tokugawa legal freeze after Osaka.

**Weakest when:** court prestige outruns coercion (late Heian); victory goes unpaid (post-Mongol); the capital hub is deleted (Ōnin); overseas supply dies (Imjin); alliances flip (Sekigahara West); peace laws succeed so well that the military meta atrophies (Edo coda).

**Campaign spine reminder:** Yamato/Ritsuryō → Genpei/Kamakura → Mongol Shore → Ōnin/Sengoku → Unifiers/Sekigahara → Osaka & Edo atrophy coda — with temple leagues, northeast frontiers, and Korea as labelled parallel theatres, never flattened into “one eternal samurai kingdom.”
