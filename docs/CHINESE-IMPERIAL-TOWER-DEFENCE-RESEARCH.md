# CHINESE IMPERIAL POWER — Exhaustive Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Bible)

> Dense notes for campaign design across **late Shang → Zhou feudalism → Spring–Autumn / Warring States → Qin unification → Han → Three Kingdoms (historical) → Sui–Tang peaks → An Lushan rupture → Song vs Liao/Jin/Yuan → Ming wall era → Qing conquest (brief)**.  
> **Method:** Separate attested history from *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* / folklore / later cult. Flag controversies. Peaks and weaknesses named explicitly.  
> Companion style to `PERSIAN-IMPERIAL-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / Egyptian / Roman TD research (same project; different civilization).

### Source classes (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATT** | Contemporary or near-contemporary primary: inscriptions, bronze vessels, bamboo/wood slips, seals, coins, archaeology with firm context |
| **TXT** | Received historiographical tradition: *Shiji*, *Hanshu*, *Sanguozhi*, *Zizhi tongjian*, dynastic *shu*, memorials — often decades–centuries later; invaluable but partisan/literary |
| **ARCH** | Archaeology / material culture without a matching “battle diary” (tombs, walls, mass graves, weapon dumps, kiln armies) |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or live debate (Loewe, Twitchett, Lewis, di Cosmo, Standen, Lorge, Yates, Pines, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Novel, drama, temple cult, hagiography, oral romance (*Sanguo yanyi*, Yue Fei legends, Guandi, Eight Immortals, dragon lore) — **abilities yes; chronology no** |

**Legend vs history rule for design:** Prefer **ATT/ARCH** for “what happened”; use **TXT** as the backbone narrative with inflation/teleology flagged; use **FOLK** freely for *abilities, boss lines, hero skins*, but never let Luo Guanzhong overwrite Chen Shou without a label.

**Chronology note:** Pre-841 BCE Western Zhou absolute dates are soft (bronze-inscription relative chronology + debated astronomical fixes). From late Western Zhou / Eastern Zhou onward, year-counts are firmer. Qin–Qing use conventional CE/BCE.

**Script note:** Pinyin primary; traditional characters once at first mention where useful.

## 2. STRONGEST PEAKS — Why strong

### 2.1 Qin (esp. late Warring States → 221–210) — **Legalist military-administrative machine**
**Why strong**
- **Admin:** Commandery–county (*junxian*) replaces feudal layers in conquered lands; standardized script, axle widths, weights — reduces friction of empire-scale logistics (**TXT** + **ATT** seals/slips).
- **Law & incentives:** Rank-by-military-merit traditions associated with Shang Yang; severe collective liability; meticulous statutes on grain, labour, armour, conscription visible in **Shuihudi** Qin slips (**ATT**). This is not cartoon “cruelty alone” — it is **bureaucratic penetration of village society for war**.
- **Army:** Mass crossbow infantry, engineered sieges, coordinated multi-year campaigns; ability to replace losses via demographic/administrative depth (**MOD** Miyake on expedition composition; Changping as logistics war as much as battle).
- **Economy:** Aggressive land/population capture; migrant/conscript pools; proto-total mobilization chapters in *Shangjunshu* debated as post-Changping demographic anxiety (**MOD** Pines).
- **Tech:** Iron weapons widespread by late Warring States; powerful composite crossbows; wall-building as operational engineering.

**Game tone:** “Quota war” — farms feed ranks; legalism cards raise output and desertion risk; beacon/road network as map vision.

**Caveat:** Peak lasts ~15 years as *empire*; the *state of Qin* was strong for generations before 221. Distinguish **Qin kingdom peak** vs **Qin empire peak**.

### 2.2 Han Wudi (141–87 BCE) — **Fiscal-military offensive peak vs steppe**
**Why strong**
- **Strategy:** Breaks *heqin* appeasement cycle; deep strikes into Ordos/Gansu/Mongolia corridors; aims to sever Xiongnu right wing and open Western Regions (**TXT** *Shiji*/*Hanshu*; framework **MOD** di Cosmo).
- **Army:** Large cavalry arms built at ruinous cost; horse procurement (including Dayuan war for “blood-sweating” horses — **TXT** with legendary colour); combined chariot/infantry legacy fading relative to mounted archery arms race.
- **Admin/economy:** Salt-iron monopolies, currency controls, selling offices/ranks — extractive state capacity for war (**TXT** *Yantie lun* debates show elite pushback).
- **Ideology:** Cosmic emperor under Heaven; omens & suburban sacrifice reformed — war framed as ordering the world (**TXT**).

**Why ranked a “peak”:** First sustained Chinese imperial projection that treats the steppe as a system to be broken, not merely raided away — at the price of later fiscal exhaustion and popular strain (**MOD**).

**Game tone:** Horse meter + desert attrition; monopoly buildings; envoy/assassin ops in oasis towers.

### 2.3 Tang Taizong → early Xuanzong (esp. 626–755 before An Lushan) — **Cosmopolitan institutional peak**
**Why strong**
- **Army (early):** *fubing* rotational militia tied to agricultural colonies — cheap internal control when it works; elite guards; later supplemented by long-service frontier professionals (**MOD**).
- **Command:** Li Shimin’s early career = personal cavalry generalship + political ruthlessness (Xuanwu Gate) — rare emperor who was a proven field commander (**TXT**).
- **Diplomacy-military:** Dual face — Chinese Son of Heaven + “Heavenly Qaghan” for steppe politics; marriage alliances; protectorates in Central Asia.
- **Economy:** Equal-field (*juntian*) ideals early; canal/grain logistics to Chang’an; Silk Road revenue and prestige goods.
- **Tech/culture:** Armoured cavalry, fortified cities, multiethnic recruitment (Turks, Sogdians, etc.) — strength *and* later loyalty risk.

**Why often ranked the apex of “classical” imperial China:** Combination of internal bureaucratic competence, Eurasian diplomatic repertoire, and offensive capacity before institutional inversion (frontier > centre).

**Game tone:** Multiethnic unit roster; reputation with steppe factions; capital grain UI.

### 2.4 Early Ming (Hongwu–Yongle, ~1368–1424) — **Recovery-autocratic peak (optional fourth)**
**Why strong**
- **Political-military:** Zhu Yuanzhang rebuilds agrarian base, hereditary military households (*junhu*), weisuo garrisons — attempt to freeze a self-supplying army (**TXT/MOD**).
- **Offense:** Northern campaigns; Yongle’s personal Mongolian expeditions; naval projection under Zheng He (prestige/diplomacy more than lasting conquest — **MOD** debate on aims).
- **Admin:** Hyper-strong autocracy after abolition of chancellorship; harsh purges — high temporary control, brittle trust.
- **Ideology:** Restore “China” after Yuan; Neo-Confucian orthodoxy hardening.

**Caveat:** By mid–late Ming the same institutions ossify; cavalry/horse supply problems return; Great Wall becomes the signature *defensive* system — still formidable as TD fantasy, but not the Yongle offensive peak.

### Peak summary for designers

| Peak | Strength type | Signature systems |
|---|---|---|
| Qin (kingdom→empire) | Legalist extraction + mass crossbow war | Ranks-for-heads, roads, slips bureaucracy |
| Han Wudi | Fiscal offensive vs Xiongnu | Horse economy, monopolies, desert columns |
| Tang early–high | Cosmopolitan empire | *fubing*→frontier pros, dual qaghan face |
| Early Ming | Autocratic reconstitution | Military households, northern drills, later wall tech |

## 3. WEAKEST POINTS — Why weak

### 3.1 Late Western Zhou → Spring–Autumn fragmentation (from 771)
**Why weak**
- Royal demilitarization relative to regional lords; king becomes ritual referee.
- Arms races among states; alliances (*hezhong*) unstable.
- Transition costs: chariot aristocracy obsolescing before new mass systems mature.

**Design:** Map breaks into colour-coded *guo*; Zhou king = weak buff tower, not army.

### 3.2 Qin imperial collapse (210–206) — **Fifteen-year empire**
**Why weak (after seeming invincible)**
- **Succession failure:** Palace intrigue (Zhao Gao/Li Si traditions **TXT**) decapitates competent command.
- **Overmobilization:** Walls, palace, tomb, campaigns → exhausted corvée; first revolts start among conscripts delayed by weather (**TXT** Chen Sheng).
- **Thin legitimacy:** Legalist fear without Zhou-style moral hegemony; conquered elites & people wait for crack (**MOD**).
- **Scale mismatch:** Administrative unity real, but political consent shallow — revolt spreads faster than Qin can remobilize.

**Design lesson:** “Max law / min loyalty” meter; winning unification unlocks collapse scenario.

### 3.3 Late Eastern Han warlordism (c. 184–220)
**Why weak**
- Eunuch–consort kin factionalism hollows court.
- Yellow Turban + frontier pressures empower local generals with private clients.
- Economy of violence: grain regions = power (Cao Cao’s *tuntian* colonization is the *solution* that also marks centre’s failure).

**Design:** Empire HUD darkens; players become warlords; imperial seals as rare items.

### 3.4 An Lushan Rebellion (755–763) — **Institutional inversion**
**Why weak**
- *fubing* decay; frontier *jiedushi* hold permanent professional armies.
- An Lushan accumulates **three** governorships (Fanyang, Pinglu, Hedong) — ~150–180k quality troops vs hollow capital forces (**TXT/MOD**).
- Multiethnic frontier machine loyal to paymaster-general.
- Even after rebellion, Tang survives only by empowering other governors & Uighur aid — **centrifugal new normal**.

**Design:** “Governor autonomy” tech that boosts border defence and quietly steals throne probability.

### 3.5 Song military weakness vs cavalry empires (despite tech)
**Why weak *relatively***
- **Structural:** Song founding deliberately shackles generals (no Tang warlord rerun); rotation, civil supremacy, weak horse lands after 1127.
- **Strategic geography:** Liao/Jin/Yuan control steppe-forest horse reservoirs; Song pays indemnities or bleeds in northern plains.
- **Not “no innovation”:** Gunpowder bombs, fire-lances, sophisticated navies, traction trebuchets, city defence — **ATT/ARCH** — yet operational art struggles against heavy cavalry & later Mongol logistics (**MOD** Lorge: Song is militarily serious, still strategically disadvantaged).
- **Jingkang 1127:** Political-military failure → loss of North China heartland.

**Design:** High tech tree, low cavalry cap; win by walls, rivers, gunpowder, diplomacy — or lose open-field chapters.

### 3.6 Ming fall (to rebellion + Qing, 1644)
**Why weak**
- Fiscal crisis, climate/famine stress (**MOD** debates on degree), peasant-military rebellions (Li Zicheng).
- Manchu state-building in Northeast produces a superior conquest coalition (banners + allied Mongols + later Han collaborators).
- Strategic surprise of dual blow: rebel take Beijing → frontier general (Wu Sangui) bargains with Qing.
- Long prior symptoms: Tumu 1449; party strife; hollow military households.

**Design:** Multiple enemy factions at once; wall alone cannot fix empty granaries.

## 4. SIGNATURE BATTLES — Tactical takeaways

Legend: ★ = historically solid core · ※ = literary/uncertain · ✦ = **FOLK** usable as ability only

### 4.1 Muye (~1046) — Zhou vs Shang ★※
- Zhou coalition defeats Shang; Shang forces partly defect in tradition (**TXT**).
- **Takeaway:** Legitimacy shock + defection mechanic; “Mandate meter” flips mid-battle.

### 4.2 Maling (341) — Sun Bin vs Pang Juan ★※
- Ambush/forced march deception; tree-inscription legend (“Pang Juan dies under this tree”) is storytelling polish (**TXT**).
- Yinqueshan proves Sun Bin textual tradition real (**ATT**) — not proof of every anecdote.
- **Takeaway:** Fog-of-war traps; “maimed strategist” hero skin (knee punishment tradition **TXT**).

### 4.3 Changping (262–260) — Bai Qi vs Zhao Kuo ★※
| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **TXT ★** | Long attrition; Qin replaces Wang He with Bai Qi (secretly); Zhao replaces Lian Po with Zhao Kuo; Qin cuts supply; Zhao breakout fails; mass killing of captives reported |
| **ARCH** | Skeletons/mass burials in Gaoping region support large-scale death assemblage (not a body-count audit of 400k+) |
| **MOD** | Numbers & “buried alive” detail contested/inflated; deeper causes = Qin strategic depth + Zhao diplomatic isolation, not only “armchair Zhao Kuo” trope (Wu 2024 line) |

**Takeaway for TD:** Siege-of-an-army in the field — supply lines as destructible conveyors; false-retreat feints; atrocity unlocks terror debuff and legitimacy hit.

### 4.4 Guandu (200) — Cao Cao vs Yuan Shao ★
- Inferior Cao destroys Yuan supply at Wuchao; Yuan’s coalition fractures (**Sanguozhi** **TXT**).
- **Takeaway:** Logistics node raid > fair field fight; faction distrust bombs.

### 4.5 Red Cliffs / Chibi (208) ★※✦ — **careful historicity**
| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **TXT ★** | Cao Cao’s southern expedition; disease/naval inexperience; Sun Quan–Liu Bei alliance; fire attack associated with Huang Gai; Cao withdraws — north/south stalemate institutionalized |
| **FOLK ✦** | Zhuge Liang borrowing arrows; altar calling east wind; “empty fort” is a different episode; Huarong Path mercy — *Yanyi* theatre |
| **MOD** | Exact site(s) debated (multiple Chibi toponyms); Cao’s troop totals wildly inflated in romance memory |

**Takeaway:** River-chokepoint map; fire-ship wind direction as weather system; **separate toggle** “Historical mode / Romance mode.”

### 4.6 Fei River (383) ★※
- Former Qin under Fu Jian advances south with multiethnic host; Jin elite Northern Headquarters troops under Xie Xuan; negotiated partial withdrawal turns into rout in traditional narrative (**TXT** *Jin shu* / *ZZTJ*).
- **MOD:** Rogers (1968) “myth” challenge → mostly rejected; Diefenbach (2012) et al.: **event real, scale & choreography exaggerated**.
- **Takeaway:** Multiethnic morale fragility; feigned/confused retreat contagion; “do not let the enemy cross in good order” as doctrine card.

### 4.7 Talas (751) — Tang vs Abbasid (Ziyad) ※★
- Tang loses Central Asian contest; paper-makers story is later anecdote, not battle-core (**MOD** caution).
- **Takeaway:** Optional western map; overextension marker one generation before An Lushan.

### 4.8 An Lushan outbreak & Tongguan (755–56) ★
- Rapid seizure of Luoyang; Tang political panic; Geshu Han destroyed after court forces a bad advance (**TXT**).
- **Takeaway:** Boss starts *already inside* your outer towers; capital loyalty collapse events.

### 4.9 Xiangyang–Fancheng (1268–73) ★ — Song last hinge
- Years of Mongol blockade; Chinese traction trebuchets vs walls; 1272–73 introduction of **huihui pao** counterweight engines under Muslim engineers (Ismail / Al-Din traditions **TXT**) breaks stalemate (**MOD** transmission debates — see §8).
- **Takeaway:** Siege tech race; “import engineer” special; twin-city link HP.

### 4.10 Lake Poyang (1363) — Zhu Yuanzhang vs Chen Youliang ★
- Massive lake fleet battle in rebellion endgame; fire and boarding (**TXT**).
- **Takeaway:** Naval TD lane; tower = war-junk.

### 4.11 Tumu (1449) ★
- Ming political army blunders into Oirat mobile warfare; emperor captured.
- **Takeaway:** Emperor-unit on field = high risk; steppe kite vs slow infantry brick.

## 5. MYTHOLOGY / IDEOLOGY → Special abilities (era-tag carefully)

**Critical rule:** Chinese religious-military imagination **accretes**. Do not give Shang oracle priests a Guandi ult, or Qin Legalists a full Eight Immortals roster.

### 5.1 Mandate of Heaven (*Tianming*) — Zhou → all later dynasties
- **ATT/TXT:** Zhou bronze & *Shijing*/*Shangshu* ideology: Heaven withdraws mandate from corrupt rulers (Shang as negative exemplar).
- **Ability seed:** Dynasty aura; omens (eclipses, floods, comet) as map modifiers; rebel spawn when Mandate meter low.
- **Not** monotheism — Heaven as cosmic moral-political will.

### 5.2 Yellow Emperor (*Huangdi*) — mythic culture hero
- **FOLK/TXT** retrospective: ancestor of Hua-Xia, inventor tropes, cosmic war vs Chi You.
- **Era-tag:** Usable as *remote mythic prologue* or Han-and-later ancestral liturgy — **not** a dated Shang historical king (**MOD**).
- **Ability:** Civilizational “founding tech” pantheon node.

### 5.3 Dragons (*long*) & weather sovereignty
- Dragon = rain, rivers, imperial charisma (emperor’s “dragon body” rhetoric **TXT**).
- **Ability:** Rain to slow fire; river surge; imperial banner morph — aesthetic continuous across eras, theology shifts.

### 5.4 Four Symbols (*Si Xiang*) — directional beasts
- Azure Dragon (E), White Tiger (W), Vermilion Bird (S), Black Tortoise/Xuanwu (N) — correlative cosmology crystallizing in late Warring States–Han (**TXT/ATT** lacquer, tombs).
- **Ability:** Four map-quarter buffs; Han “military banner schools.”
- Pair with Five Phases (*wuxing*) rock-paper elemental towers — **Han peak** for this grammar.

### 5.5 Daoist talismans, Celestial Masters, Thunder rites
- Han: Yellow Turbans / Five Pecks millenarianism — **TXT** with **FOLK** growth.
- Medieval Daoism: *fu* talismans, spirit soldiers, *leifa* thunder magic in ritual manuals — **late** relative to Qin (**ATT** manuscripts from later centuries).
- **Era-tag:** Yellow Turban scenario; Tang–Song–Ming ritual specialists as support heroes — label **FOLK/ritual**, not physics.

### 5.6 Buddhist guardians (late arrival)
- Buddhism enters Han, blooms Northern Dynasties–Tang.
- Guardians: Four Heavenly Kings, Vajrapani-type door gods, Guanyin mercy shields — temple iconography **ARCH**.
- **Era-tag:** Not for Warring States. Tang cosmopolitan peak = best fit. Shaolin warrior-monk **FOLK** layer much later/overstated for Sui-Tang turn.

### 5.7 Guan Yu → Guandi cult
- Historical Guan Yu: officer under Liu Bei, executed after Jingzhou loss — *Sanguozhi* **TXT**.
- Cultus grows in Song–Ming; official titles escalate; becomes God of War/Wealth/Loyalty — **FOLK**.
- **Era-tag:** *Hero unit* in Three Kingdoms historical mode; *divine ult* only in Ming+ temple DLC.

### 5.8 Eight Immortals (*Ba Xian*)
- Daoist transcendents ensemble popularized mid-imperial / Ming drama — **FOLK**.
- **Do not** drop into Qin campaign as historical. Fine as Song–Ming skin pack.

### 5.9 Other ideology hooks
- **Confucian just war / righteous army (*yijun*)** — Song loyalism, Ming resistance rhetoric.
- **Legalism** — antithesis buff: +conscript efficiency, −Mandate regeneration.
- **City God / local earth altars** — garrison morale (late imperial especially).

## 7. OBSCURE BUT GAMEABLE DETAILS

### 7.1 Terracotta Army as formation manual (**ARCH**)
- Pit layouts: infantry ranks, chariots, cavalry — idealized order of a buried *peiyong* host for the First Emperor.
- Not a literal order-of-battle spreadsheet, but unmatched visual grammar for “Qin formation towers.”
- Crossbow locks of extraordinary uniformity → quality-control tech flavour (**ARCH/MOD**).

### 7.2 Bamboo military libraries — Yinqueshan & friends (**ATT**)
- **Yinqueshan** (Linyi, 1972): Sunzi fragments + **Sun Bin bingfa** resurrected after ~1400 years lost; also Wei Liaozi, Six Strategies pieces, Yanzi, yin-yang military lore.
- **Shuihudi** (Yunmeng): Qin statutes — conscription, grain, armour issue, timekeeping on official travel — the “boring” backbone of war.
- **Zhangjiashan**, **Liye** Qin slips: more admin muscle.
- **Game:** Discoverable “slip texts” as tech fragments; incomplete chapters = partial buffs.

### 7.3 Beacon towers & smoke/fire code (**TXT/ARCH**)
- Frontier beacon chains (Han watchtowers in Gansu/Inner Asia **ARCH**; Ming wall beacons famous).
- Zhou “false beacon” story to amuse Baosi is cautionary **FOLK/TXT** — teach players that false alarm destroys response trust (cry-wolf debuff) without claiming 8th-c. BCE ops research accuracy.
- **Game:** Vision network; fuel cost; spoofable signals.

### 7.4 Tea-horse trade (*chama maoyi*) (**TXT/MOD**)
- Song–Ming institutions trading tea for Tibetan/steppe horses — fiscal answer to cavalry deficit.
- **Game:** Soft-power horse income vs moral cost; raidable caravan lanes; Ming *tea-horse* offices as buildings.

### 7.5 Trebuchet transmission debates (**MOD**)
- China: long history of **traction** trebuchets (man-powered) — indigenous development.
- **Counterweight** trebuchet: Mediterranean/Islamic crystallization; Mongol–Ilkhanid engineers (Ismail, Al-Din) at Xiangyang 1272–73 as standard narrative (*huihui pao*).
- Controversies: possible earlier Jin “arresting trebuchet” (1232) hints; how explosive shells interact with new engines; Chinese reverse-engineering speed after 1273.
- **Game:** Two artillery branches; “Foreign Engineer” unique that angers traditionalists but cracks star forts.

### 7.6 Crossbow & repeating crossbow (**ATT/ARCH/TXT**)
- Han bronze triggers = interchangeable high craft.
- Zhuge repeating crossbow tradition — name association often later; treat rapid-fire as tech with era gates (**MOD** caution on attributing to Zhuge Liang).

### 7.7 *Tuntian* military farms
- Han Cao Cao; Tang colonies; Ming weisuo ideals — food-as-ammunition.
- **Game:** Towers that farm; starve if raided.

### 7.8 Gunpowder genealogy (Song–Ming)
- Formulas in military compendia (*Wujing zongyao* 1044 **TXT**); bombs, fire-arrows, early guns **ARCH**.
- Avoid steampunk rifles in Tang.

### 7.9 South-pointing chariot & other tech myths
- Many “inventions” are reconstructed or legendary — label carefully; magnetic compass navigation firmer in Song maritime world.

### 7.10 Wall systems ≠ one wall
- Qin “long walls” link older state walls; Han & Northern dynasties rebuild; **Ming brick megaproject** is the tourist Wall.
- **Game:** Era skins for the same map mesh.

### 7.11 Drum & gong doctrine / flag signals
- Military classics stress drums advance, gongs withdraw, flags for formation — perfect non-verbal command UI (**TXT** Sunzi et al.).

### 7.12 Horse policy & *mujian* / tea-horse / Ming horse administration
- Chronic structural problem of agrarian empires vs steppe — recurring campaign rule set.

## 10. QUICK “DO NOT ASSUME” CHECKLIST

1. ***Romance of the Three Kingdoms* ≠ history** — Zhuge’s magic, arrow boats, east-wind altar, many duel set-pieces are **FOLK**.  
2. **Chen Shou’s Zhuge** = administrator-strategist with failed northern campaigns — still brilliant, not omniscient.  
3. **Changping body-counts** in *Shiji* are not census audits; archaeology proves slaughter scale, not exact figures.  
4. **Fei River** happened; Fu Jian’s “877,000” choreography did not — use moral-panic & rout mechanics, not literal megastack.  
5. **Qin Great Wall ≠ Ming Great Wall** — different materials, routes, purposes.  
6. **Sunzi the man** is foggy; **Sunzi the text** is layered; **Sun Bin** is archaeologically rehabilitated.  
7. **Song** is not “militarily stupid” — it is structurally horse-poor and civil-over-military by design, yet technologically inventive.  
8. **Counterweight trebuchet** at Xiangyang is a transmission story with debate at the edges — keep traction vs counterweight split.  
9. **Mandate / dragons / Four Symbols / Guandi / Ba Xian / Buddhist kings** arrive on different clocks — era-tag abilities.  
10. **An Lushan** is an institutional story (frontier professionalism + patronage), not only “fat villain betrays emperor” opera.  
11. **Yellow Emperor** is mythic charter, not a dated historical campaign lord.  
12. **Qing conquest** ends Ming via rebel + Manchu interaction — not a single fair-field “final boss duel.”
