# MAYA & MEXICA (AZTEC) — Exhaustive Dual Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Bible)

> Dense notes for a **two-act / two-faction** campaign bible.  
> **Act I — Classic Maya lowland hegemony wars** (Preclassic → Classic peaks → Terminal Classic rupture → Postclassic Yucatán coda).  
> **Act II — Central Mexican imperial arc** (Teotihuacan → Epiclassic → Toltec debates → Mexica rise → Triple Alliance → 1521).  
> **Hard rule:** treat **Maya** and **Mexica/Nahua** as **separate civilizations**. Shared Mesoamerican traits (calendars, ballgame, feathered-serpent *iconography*, maize cosmologies) are **areal parallels**, not one religion, one empire, or one pantheon. Never merge Quetzalcoatl and Kukulkan into a single “god card” without era/culture tags.  
> Companion style to `CHINESE-IMPERIAL-TOWER-DEFENCE-RESEARCH.md` / Egyptian / Persian / Roman TD research (same project).

### Source classes (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ATT** | Contemporary or near-contemporary indigenous primary: Classic Maya hieroglyphic texts on stone/wood/ceramics; Postclassic Maya codices (Dresden, Madrid, Paris — fragmentary); Mexica/Nahua pictorial manuscripts (pre- and early-colonial); indigenous-language texts where recoverable |
| **EPIG** | Epigraphic decipherment & dynastic reconstruction (Martin–Grube school and successors; Houston, Stuart, etc.) — often the *only* political narrative for Classic Maya |
| **ARCH** | Archaeology / bioarchaeology / paleoclimate without a matching “battle diary” (fortifications, mass graves, lake cores, ceramic dumps, Templo Mayor offerings) |
| **SPN** | Spanish colonial sources: Cortés letters, Bernal Díaz, Sahagún *Florentine Codex*, Durán, Alva Ixtlilxóchitl, Motolinía, Landa (*Relación*), *Chilam Balam* compilations (Maya, mediated), *Popol Vuh* (K’iche’, colonial transcription) — invaluable, partisan, theological, decades–centuries after many events |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or live debate (Martin, Grube, Houston, Webster, Demarest, Aimers, Hassig, Restall, Smith, López Luján, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Later legend, romanticized conquest narrative, game-lore inflation, temple cult survivals, popular “bee grenade” retellings — **abilities yes; chronology no** |

**Legend vs history rule for design:** Prefer **EPIG/ATT/ARCH** for Classic Maya “what happened.” Prefer **SPN** cross-checked with **ARCH/MOD** for Mexica politics and 1519–21. Use **FOLK** freely for *abilities, skins, boss lines*, never as silent chronology.

**Naming rule:** Prefer **Mexica** (Tenochca) / **Triple Alliance** / **Nahua** for the imperial faction; “Aztec” is historiographic shorthand (**MOD**). Prefer **Maya** as a cultural-linguistic family of *many polities*, not a unitary empire.

**Chronology note:** Maya Long Count dates convert cleanly once the GMT correlation is accepted (standard **MOD**). Central Mexican absolute dates before Spanish contact rely on **SPN** year-counts + **ARCH** phasing; Toltec–Chichen links remain contested.

**Script note:** Maya names in current epigraphic orthography where stable (e.g. Jasaw Chan K’awiil); older nicknames (Ah Cacao, Ruler A) noted once. Nahuatl in classic scholarly forms (Motecuhzoma, Huitzilopochtli).

**Design takeaway:** Maya history is **multipolar dynastic warfare inside a shared cultural sphere**, then **asynchronous collapse**, then **northern reconfiguration** — not a single throne to topple until colonial contact.

## A2. STRONGEST PEAKS — Why strong (Maya)

### A2.1 Late Preclassic Mirador–sphere (optional prologue peak)
**Why strong**
- Monumental labor mobilization at basin scale; early divine-king aesthetics; long-distance trade in jade/obsidian (**ARCH**).
- Shows that “Maya power” is older than Classic stelae politics.

**Caveat:** Political mechanics less readable than Classic (**EPIG** sparse). Use as tutorial atmosphere, not main campaign ruleset.

### A2.2 Early Classic Tikal under Teotihuacan-linked dynasty (late 4th–5th c.) — **Entrada military-prestige peak**
**Why strong**
- **Regime tech:** New military costume, atlatl emphasis in imagery, foreign charter ideology (**EPIG/ARCH**).
- **Network power:** Ability to seat allied dynasts (Copán foundation pattern) — hegemony by *installation*, not only razing (**EPIG**).
- **Demographic/architectural weight:** Tikal already a primate city relative to neighbors (**ARCH**).

**Game tone:** “Foreign charter” buffs + vassal coronation missions; risk of later nativist backlash narratives.

### A2.3 Calakmul / Kaan hegemony under Yuknoom Ch’een II (mid–late 7th c.) — **Snake web peak**
**Why strong**
- **Macro-politics:** Martin–Grube model — two superpowers with client kings; Calakmul often superior in the 7th century (**EPIG/MOD**).
- **Alliance engineering:** Marriage, overlord phrases (*yajaw*, “his lord”), gift events, star-war timing — soft empire of patronage + selective hard war (**EPIG**).
- **Proxy mastery:** Turning Tikal’s own prince (Dos Pilas) into a knife against the mother city — asymmetric strategy (**EPIG**).

**Why ranked strongest “Classic Maya imperial” moment:** Closest approach to lowland-wide hierarchy without becoming a Mexica-style tribute machine. Still a **hegemonic web**, not a centralized territorial bureaucracy (**MOD**).

**Game tone:** Diplomacy board + star-war calendar cooldowns; vassals flip; overlord aura vs local ajaw autonomy meter.

### A2.4 Late Classic Tikal restoration (Jasaw Chan K’awiil I → early 8th c.) — **Resurgent Mutul peak**
**Why strong**
- **Narrative victory:** 695 as explicit reversal of Snake dominance; flint-and-shield war idiom; deity-effigy capture as prestige nuke (**EPIG**).
- **Monumental propaganda machine:** Temple I/II program; revived stelae after Hiatus (**ATT/ARCH**).
- **Memory politics:** Victory rites timed to Teotihuacan-linked ancestral anniversaries — legitimacy as deep time (**EPIG**).

**Caveat:** Restoration ≠ perpetual empire. 8th-c. multipolarity returns; Terminal Classic still comes (**MOD**).

### A2.5 Early Postclassic northern Chichen Itza (approx. 9th–11th c. peak window) — **Militarized cosmopolitan node**
**Why strong**
- Large-scale architectural program; warrior orders imagery; extensive trade (obsidian, gold, turquoise) (**ARCH**).
- Feathered-serpent public cult (Kukulkan) as territorial/political religion distinct from Classic vision-serpent kingship (**MOD** — do **not** equate to Mexica Quetzalcoatl cult).
- Possible multiethnic elite (Maya + Gulf / central Mexican connections) — contested “Toltec invasion” story (**MOD** debate; prefer interaction over cartoon migration).

**Game tone:** Cenote map control; merchant-warrior hybrids; pilgrimage prestige.

### Peak summary (Maya)

| Peak | Strength type | Signature systems |
|---|---|---|
| Mirador Late Preclassic | Monumental labor state | Mega-platforms, early kingship art |
| Early Classic Tikal (+ Teotihuacan charter) | Dynastic transplant hegemony | Entrada, vassal seating |
| 7th-c. Calakmul/Kaan | Alliance-web superpower | Overlord glyphs, proxies, star wars |
| Jasaw’s Tikal | Restoration hard-power | 695 victory, temple propaganda |
| Chichen Early Postclassic | Cosmopolitan militarized capital | Kukulkan public cult, long-trade |

## A3. WEAKEST POINTS — Why weak (Maya)

### A3.1 Tikal Hiatus after 562 — **Epigraphic–political knockout**
**Why weak**
- Star-war defeat + Caracol/Snake pressure silences Tikal’s monumental voice (**EPIG**).
- Does not mean the city empties; it means the *royal historical machine* fails — perfect TD “disabled hero altar” state (**MOD**).

### A3.2 Fratricidal proxy spiral (Dos Pilas vs Tikal, 7th c.)
**Why weak**
- Kinship weaponized; secondary capitals become permanent enemies; war devastates Petexbatun later (**EPIG/ARCH**).
- Shows Classic strength (personal dynastic bonds) flipping into structural fragility.

### A3.3 Terminal Classic southern collapse (~800–950) — **Multi-causal system failure**
**Do not assume a single cause.** Flag the debate explicitly:

| Hypothesis cluster | What it claims | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Drought / climate** | Severe 9th–11th c. droughts stress agrarian bases | Strong paleoclimate signal **ARCH**; timing vs political failure imperfect (**MOD** Douglas, Kennett, Medina-Elizalde) |
| **Warfare intensification** | Endemic conflict, fortification, refugee aggregation, trade rupture | Clear **EPIG/ARCH** (Petexbatun, walls); can be feedback not prime mover |
| **Political–ideological system limits** | Divine kings fail to deliver rain/victory; stelae cease; population rejects or flees sacral centers | **MOD** (Demarest and others); explains *political* collapse better than total biological extinction |
| **Agrarian degradation** | Erosion, deforestation, soil stress under high population | Regional **ARCH** variability; not uniform |
| **Trade-route reorientation** | Gulf/northern circuits undercut southern inland primacy | Plausible **MOD** co-factor |

**Modern consensus shape (**MOD**):** collapse is **asynchronous, multi-causal, regionally varied**. Southern dynastic civilization ends; Maya peoples and northern/eastern societies continue. “Disappearance of the Maya” is a 19th-c. myth (**FOLK** if used uncritically).

**Design lesson:** Stack meters (Drought × War × Legitimacy × Trade). Winning too many star wars can still fail the campaign if Drought ticks to max.

### A3.4 Mayapan late failure & contact fragmentation
**Why weak**
- Confederacy fractures into rival houses; no single command against Spaniards (**SPN/ARCH**).
- Resilience exists (coastal trade, flexible provinces) but **strategic unity** does not (**MOD**).

## B2. STRONGEST PEAKS — Why strong (Central Mexico / Mexica)

### B2.1 Teotihuacan (esp. Tlamimilolpa–Xolalpan centuries) — **Metropolis without Maya-style dynastic texts**
**Why strong**
- Demographic and craft scale unmatched in its day in the Basin; obsidian export engine; planned avenues (**ARCH**).
- Soft power: costume, weapons, and political models radiate into Maya highlands/lowlands (**EPIG/ARCH**).
- Strength is **urban–economic–ideological**, less “named general” readable than Tikal (**ATT** scarce).

### B2.2 Tula Toltec horizon — **Warrior charter peak (archaeology + later myth)**
**Why strong (archaeological):** Fortified urbanism, warrior orders imagery, regional influence (**ARCH**).  
**Why strong (Mexica memory):** Tollan becomes the golden template of civilization; Mexica kings claim Toltec descent (**SPN**).  
**Design:** Separate “Toltec archaeology skin” from “Tollan myth buff” — players should feel the **MOD** gap.

### B2.3 Triple Alliance mature empire (Motecuhzoma I → Ahuitzotl, ~1440–1502) — **Strongest Mexica imperial peak**
**Why strong**
- **Military–tributary machine:** Conquest → leave local ruler → impose tribute lists (codex Mendoza tradition) → threaten return (**SPN/ATT** pictorial).
- **Logistics:** Causeways, canoe supply, porters, roads; rapid seasonal campaigns (**SPN**; operational reading **MOD** Hassig).
- **Warrior aristocracy incentives:** Captive-taking ranked prestige (eagle/jaguar orders) — high offensive morale (**SPN**).
- **Ideology:** Huitzilopochtli–Tlaloc twin temple program sacralizes war + agriculture at the capital (**ARCH** Templo Mayor; **SPN**).
- **Alliance core:** Texcoco administrative/intellectual partnership; Tlacopan lesser third — real coalition, not pure monarchy (**SPN/MOD**).

**Why Ahuitzotl often = maximum footprint:** Aggressive southern/coastal pushes; terrifying dedication ceremonies as deterrence theater (**SPN**; numbers critical **MOD**).

**Game tone:** Tribute UI as oxygen; flower-war training grounds; pochteca vision on fogged provinces.

### Peak summary (Central Mexico / Mexica)

| Peak | Strength type | Signature systems |
|---|---|---|
| Teotihuacan | Metropolitan export hegemony | Obsidian, urban plan, foreign charter |
| Tula / Tollan memory | Warrior prestige template | Toltec claim buffs |
| Triple Alliance (Motecuhzoma I–Ahuitzotl) | Tributary empire | Tribute lists, ranked warriors, twin temple |

## B3. WEAKEST POINTS — Why weak (Mexica empire)

### B3.1 Structural fragility of a **hegemonic tribute empire**
**Why weak**
- Indirect rule leaves local dynasties intact — efficient until a credible alternative patron appears (**MOD**).
- “Empire” is a mosaic of resentful subjects, autonomous enclaves, and open enemies inside the geographic envelope (notably **Tlaxcala**) (**SPN/MOD**).
- Not a Roman provincial bureaucracy; revolt is always one defeat away.

### B3.2 Unconquered enemies & failed fronts
- **Tlaxcala–Huexotzinco–Cholula** bloc: permanent hostile pocket (**SPN**).
- **Purépecha (Tarascan) state:** Axayacatl’s western disaster shows peer competitor with copper-bronze metallurgy edge in some armaments (**SPN/ARCH**).
- Frontier garrisons (e.g. Oztuma vs west) advertise fear of rebellion (**SPN**).

### B3.3 Subject revolt pattern under Motecuhzoma II
**Why weak**
- Sources describe repeated provincial rebellions requiring reconquest — classic overstretch symptom (**SPN**).
- Centralization and terror increase short-term compliance, long-term defection incentive (**MOD**).

### B3.4 Spanish–Indigenous alliance structure (1519–21) — **terminal exploit of fragility**
**Do not assume “hundreds of Spaniards defeat millions alone.”**
- Cortés wins as **coalition entrepreneur**: Tlaxcala, later Texcoco factions, Totonac polities, disgruntled subjects (**SPN**; synthesis **MOD** Restall *Seven Myths*, Hassig).
- Tech edges (steel, cavalry, guns, brigantines) matter tactically; **demography of allies + epidemic** matter strategically (**SPN/ARCH/MOD**).
- Lake siege + smallpox + famine = system kill, not single duel.

**Design lesson:** Enemy faction can recruit your oppressed tributaries. High Tribute meter lowers Foreign Ally resistance.

### B3.5 Ideological–political single points of failure
- Hostage-taking of Motecuhzoma II paralyzes court signaling (**SPN**).
- New Fire cosmic anxiety (every 52 years) is cultural strength and panic button (**SPN**) — optional “calendar apocalypse” mechanic for Mexica campaigns only.

# PART C — OBSCURE / DEBATED TOPICS (BOTH, STILL SEPARATED)

## C1. Flower wars (*xochiyaoyotl*) — debates
**What sources say (**SPN**):** Arranged, limited conflicts especially Triple Alliance vs Tlaxcala–Puebla polities; captive harvest; training; post-famine institution under Motecuhzoma I / Tlacaelel (Durán, Crónica X traditions; Alva Ixtlilxóchitl variants).

**Hassig (**MOD**):** Also political — attrition, intimidation, status, keeping enemies off-balance without full annexation costs; not “purely ritual sport.”

**Design:** A selectable war mode: lower destruction, higher captive yield, slow political pressure. Do not portray as bloodless pageant.

## C2. Maya “star wars”
**What it is (**EPIG**):** A war-event glyphic expression in Classic inscriptions; clusters at key dates in Tikal–Calakmul macro-history.

**What it is not:** A franchise crossover; not automatically “Maya fought only when Venus said so” in every case (**MOD** caution).

**Design:** Omen meter opens a temporary “Star War” declaration with bonus vs rival superpower; misfires possible.

## C3. Pochteca as spies?
**Attested functions (**SPN** Sahagún):** Long-distance merchants; corporate guilds; market judges; diplomatic travelers (*teucnenenque*); intelligence on routes, products, political mood; sometimes pretext for conquest when pochteca abused abroad.

**MOD reading:** Not a modern CIA, but **yes — institutionalized commercial intelligence** tied to imperial expansion.

**Design:** Invisible scout unit that paints tribute targets; killing them is a casus belli card.

## C4. New Fire ceremony (*xiuhmolpilli* / *toxiuhmolpilia*)
**Mexica only as full state apocalypse ritual (**SPN**):** Every 52-year Calendar Round; extinguish fires; drill new fire in sacrificial context on Huixachtlan / Cerro de la Estrella; relay torches; household ceramic dumps possible archaeologically (**ARCH** Elson & Smith).

**Deeper roots:** Year-binding ideas may reach toward Teotihuacan/Epiclassic (**MOD/ARCH** Helmke et al.) — still **central Mexican**, not Maya Classic kingship ritual.

**Last pre-Hispanic:** 1507 under Motecuhzoma II (**SPN/ATT** date stones).

**Design:** Empire-wide blackout night; if ritual fails, morale collapse; if succeeds, long buff. Maya campaigns use different calendar mechanics (k’atun endings, not this exact rite).

## C5. Maya scorpion / bee weapons — attestation check
| Claim | Verdict | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| K’iche’ *Popol Vuh* episode: gourds/jars of yellow jackets & wasps released to break an assault | **Attested as mythic-literary war story** in colonial K’iche’ text | **SPN** (indigenous myth in colonial transcription) / **FOLK** if generalized to all Maya armies |
| Classic lowland armies routinely throwing beehive grenades | **Not established** by **EPIG/ARCH** | Do not present as standard kit |
| Scorpion pots as Maya siege weapon | **No solid Maya military attestation** found in standard corpora; scorpions appear in **iconography/zodiacal** contexts; live-scorpion pots are better known as a **cross-cultural siege folklore motif** elsewhere | Treat as **FOLK**/optional fantasy toggle, labeled |
| Stingless beekeeping in Yucatán | Well attested culturally | **SPN** Landa; **ARCH**/ethnohistory — economy, not proven bomb weapon |

**Design recommendation:** Bee/wasp release = **K’iche’ mythic ability skin** (Hero/Tohil narrative), not a generic “Maya civ bonus.” Scorpion pots = **off** unless explicitly in a Folklore mode.

## C6. Tzompantli (skull racks)
**Mexica:** Prominently described beside Templo Mayor; skull towers as terror/visibility of sacrifice & war death (**SPN**). **ARCH:** Huey Tzompantli excavations — real towers of skulls, including women/children in some samples — complicates “only enemy warriors” simplification (**MOD** INAH reports).

**Maya:** Skull presentation and trophy heads occur; monumental tzompantli *architecture* is more characteristic of Late Postclassic / central Mexican-influenced contexts (e.g. discussions around Chichen) than of Early Classic stelae politics (**ARCH/MOD**). Do not paste Tenochtitlan skull-towers onto Tikal Temple I as default.

**Design:** Mexica terror landmark building; Maya captive-display as stela/altar spectacle instead.

## C7. Other quick obscure hooks
- **Macuahuitl:** Obsidian-edged sword-club — Nahua fame via **SPN**; related edged clubs in broader Mesoamerica — don’t unique-lock only to “Aztec” without skins.
- **Atlatl:** Deep Teotihuacan/Maya Entrada resonance (**ARCH/EPIG** imagery).
- **Fortified landscapes:** Maya La Cuernavilla-type citadels (**ARCH** lidar); Mexica frontier garrisons (**SPN**).
- **Women in war narratives:** Mixed — some **SPN** anecdotes; elite male captive focus dominates Classic Maya art (**ATT**).

## QUICK “DO NOT ASSUME” CHECKLIST

1. **Maya ≠ Aztec.** Separate timelines, pantheons, political forms.  
2. **Maya were not one empire**; Tikal and Calakmul were rival hegemons among many courts.  
3. **“Aztec”** is shorthand; prefer Mexica / Triple Alliance in bible voice.  
4. **Terminal Classic collapse ≠ extinction of Maya peoples.**  
5. **Drought is a co-factor, not a settled single cause.**  
6. **Star wars** are epigraphic war events, not pop-culture astrology certainty.  
7. **Flower wars** are debated (ritual + political), not carnival LARP.  
8. **Pochteca** = merchants with intelligence roles, not fantasy rogue class alone.  
9. **Bee/wasp weapons** = *Popol Vuh* literary; **scorpion bombs** ≠ standard Maya kit.  
10. **Tzompantli** monumental racks = primarily Mexica/central Late Postclassic signature.  
11. **Quetzalcoatl / Kukulkan / Q’uq’umatz** = parallel types, three cards minimum.  
12. **1521** = indigenous civil war coalition + siege + epidemic, not pure conquistador duel.  
13. **Toltec–Chichen** link = archaeological interaction + later legend; avoid “Toltec conquest of Maya” cartoon.  
14. **Teotihuacan Entrada** at Tikal is historically serious (**EPIG**); “Spearthrower Owl = named Teotihuacan emperor” remains interpretive.  
15. **New Fire** is central Mexican Calendar Round ritual — don’t paste onto Classic Maya ajaw campaigns.  
16. **Wayob** are Classic Maya co-essence/sorcery beings (**EPIG**) — not Nahua *nagualli* clones (related Mesoamerican idea-family, different dossier).  
17. **Spanish numbers** for sacrifice and army sizes are often inflated — keep order-of-magnitude humility (**MOD**).  
18. **Chilam Balam / Hunac Ceel** dates are chronicle traditions, not GPS-locked battle logs.
