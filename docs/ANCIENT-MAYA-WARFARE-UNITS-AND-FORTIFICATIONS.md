# ANCIENT MAYA WARFARE, UNITS & FORTIFICATIONS — Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Bible)

> English research notes for a Maya-themed tower-defence campaign bible. **Classic period primary** (ca. 250–900 CE); Preclassic / Postclassic flagged. Attested **Classic Mayan / Yucatec** terms preferred over Spanish glosses.  
> Companion style to Egyptian / Persian / Chinese / Roman TD research (same project).

### Method (do not skip)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EPI** | Hieroglyphic texts: emblem glyphs, war statements, titles, dates (Martin–Grube corpus tradition) |
| **ICON** | Murals, stelae, lintels, ceramics (Bonampak, Yaxchilan, Chichen Itza ballcourt reliefs, etc.) |
| **ARCH** | Archaeology: fortifications, weapons, burn layers, abandonment assemblages |
| **ETHNO** | Early Colonial Maya / Spanish accounts (Landa, Bernal Díaz, Relaciónes) — **conquest-era Yucatec / Petén Maya**, not Classic |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or live debate |
| **MYTH** | Outdated models (esp. “peaceful Maya”); colonial moralizing; popular Toltec/Mexicanization tropes |

**Bias flags.** (1) Mid-20th-c. Morley–Thompson “peaceful theocratic astronomer-priests” = **MYTH** — shattered by Bonampak (1940s find; slow acceptance), Becán earthworks (Webster 1970s), and glyph decipherment (Proskouriakoff; Schele; Martin & Grube). (2) Spanish sources exaggerate Aztec/Maya “bloodlust” for evangelizing/justifying conquest **and** understate Classic territorial war because they saw Postclassic / Contact societies. (3) 1980s–90s “ritual war only / Killer King Complex” (Schele–Freidel vein) over-corrected: prestige captive-taking is real (**EPI/ICON**) but **not** mutually exclusive with conquest, burning, and fortification (**ARCH** + Witzna *puluuy*). (4) Do **not** treat Osprey plates, Apocalypto, or Age of Empires lists as primary.

**Chronology (conventional).**
| Span | Label | Military notes |
|---|---|---|
| ~1000 BCE–250 CE | **Preclassic / Formative** | Early earthworks (Los Naranjos; Becán ditch ca. 100–250 CE) |
| 250–600 | **Early Classic** | Teotihuacan-linked intrusion at Tikal (entrada 378); atlatl/dart rise; emblem-glyph polities |
| 600–800 | **Late Classic** | Tikal–Calakmul “superpower” rivalry; Petexbatun fortification boom; richest war texts |
| 800–950/1000 | **Terminal Classic** | Collapse violence uneven by region; Dos Pilas / Aguateca endings |
| ~950–1540s | **Postclassic** | Mayapan wall; Tulum cliff+wall; Contact-period cotton armour, bows, *holkan* militia (**ETHNO**) |

**Script note.** Classic Mayan (Chʼolan-related) for Classic titles/verbs; Yucatec for Contact armour/weapons when Classic term unknown. Transcriptions vary (ʼ / '); keep consistent within a feature.

## 0. Framing: what Maya “warfare” was

**Not one army.** Classic lowlands = dozens of **emblem-glyph** polities (*ajaw* / *kʼuhul ajaw* dynasties) in shifting alliances and overlordships (*yajaw* “vassal of…”). No standing Roman legion; forces = royal household + secondary elite + levies, scale debated.

**Evidence quality.**
- **High:** war verbs & captive titles on monuments; fortification morphology; Aguateca weapon floors; Witzna burn + sediment charcoal.
- **Medium:** unit sizes, logistics, how often commoners fought; Venus–war statistical claims.
- **Low:** TO&Es, drill manuals, exact shield construction for Classic (perishables); “who died in the ballcourt.”

**Game-use:** Faction should **feel dynastic and glyph-driven** (emblem as “flag”), not Aztec clone. Unlock Preclassic ditch age → Classic star-war age → Terminal fortress panic → Postclassic walled capital.

## 1. Ritual-only vs conquest / territorial — current consensus

### Verdict (**MOD**)
**Both.** Classic Maya warfare had **strong ritual and prestige dimensions** (captive display, sacrifice, cosmic kingship) **and** **strategic-political / territorial consequences** (overlordship, tribute, burning of places, demographic disruption, fortification). The old either/or is rejected.

### Historiography (flag myths)
1. **Peaceful Maya (Morley 1946; Thompson 1954)** — Classic as peaceful stargazers; war = late “Mexicanization.” **Falsified.** **MYTH.**
2. **Ritual raid only / elite duel model (1980s)** — war mainly to seize high-status captives for sacrifice; little impact on commoners. **Partially true as *one* mode**, overstated as *the* mode (Webster satire: “Killer King Complex”).
3. **Current (**MOD** Scherer & Golden; Webster; Chase & Chase; Inomata; Wahl et al. 2019; *Ancient Mesoamerica* 2021 Cultural Dimensions of Warfare):** ritual and “real” war are not opposites. Raiding, siege-avoidance, scorched attacks, and hegemonic overlordship coexist.

### Epigraphic war lexicon (**EPI**)
| Expression | Approx. sense | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *chuhkaj* | “was captured / seized” | Most common war verb (passive of *chuk*) |
| *chʼahkaj* | “was chopped / axed” | Lethal / destructive; distinct from capture |
| *puluuy* | “burned” (place) | Can mean **city-scale burning** — Witzna / Bahlam Jol 697 CE (Wahl, Estrada-Belli, Tokovinine 2019, *Nature Human Behaviour*) |
| *jubuy utoʼkʼ upakal* | “his flint-and-shield fell” | Defeat of an **army** (*toʼkʼ pakal* = flint + shield metonym) |
| *och chʼeʼn* | “enters the cave / settlement” | Armed entry into a place; contested nuance |
| **“Star war”** glyph | Major war event (reading debated: *jom*? *jub*? etc.) | Schele’s label; **not** a proven Venus verb; association with Venus = **debated** (Aldana; Voit critique) |
| *baak* | captive **and** bone | Emic link trophy / captive |
| *uchaʼan X* | “guardian / captor of X” | Prestige title |
| *aj N baak* | “he of N captives” | Count-of-captives (e.g. Yaxchilan *Aj Jun Kʼal Baak* “he of 20 captives”) |

### Archaeology that breaks “ritual-only”
- **Becán** Late Preclassic dry ditch = industrial-scale defence (**ARCH** Webster) → war mattered before Classic “collapse.”
- **Petexbatun** (Dos Pilas, Aguateca, Punta de Chimino): concentric walls, escarpment forts, triple ditches, rapid abandonment under fire (**ARCH** Demarest et al. 1997; Inomata 1997).
- **Witzna (Bahlam Jol):** charcoal layer in Laguna EkʼNaab + plaza burning + Naranjo stela *puluuy* dated **21 May 697** → “total warfare” tactics **during** Late Classic prosperity, not only Terminal Classic (**ARCH/EPI**).
- **Aguateca:** epicenter burned ~810; elite houses left with spears/darts; aim looks like **termination**, not gentle vassalage (**ARCH** Inomata).

### Controversies
1. How “total” was *puluuy*? Witzna is the best-calibrated case; do not assume every *puluuy* equals Hiroshima.
2. Hegemony vs annexation: Calakmul / Tikal often installed **overlordship** and vassal dynasties rather than erasing local emblems (Martin & Grube model) — still **territorial politics**.
3. Venus timing of star wars: influential; **statistical and reading critiques exist** — use as flavour, not hard science lock.

**Game-use:** Campaign modes = *chuk* raid (capture VIP), *pul* burn wave (map fire), *toʼkʼ pakal* field army clash, star-war boss event. Do **not** make all levels “sacrifice minigames.”

## 2. Weapons & protective gear

### 2.1 Spear / lance (primary Classic shock weapon)
**What.** Long wooden shaft + **chert** (sometimes **obsidian**) bifacial point — thrusting / close combat. Dominant in **ICON** (stelae, Bonampak) and **ARCH** (Aguateca floors: dozens of bifacial points per elite house).

**Terms.** Classic often shows the weapon; Colonial Yucatec spears = *nabteʼ* / short *lanzuelas* with flint (**ETHNO** Landa). Prefer descriptive “lance / spear point” unless a secure Classic lexeme is needed in UI.

**Sources.** Aoyama 2005 (*Ancient Mesoamerica*): Aguateca + Copan microwear — bifacials as spears/darts **and** knives; elites = warriors. Aoyama & Graham 2015: lance primary Preclassic–Classic.

**Game-use:** Core melee tower troop; high capture chance vs kill chance (design lever for *chuk* economy).

### 2.2 Atlatl / spear-thrower + darts
**What.** Levered dart-thrower; Classic adoption intensifies with **Early Classic highland / Teotihuacan-linked** military fashion (not full replacement of spears).

**Terms.** Nahuatl *atlatl* widely used in scholarship; Yucatec **hulʼche** / *hulche* for spear-thrower (**ETHNO**/lexicographic). Dart points = medium-width bifacials (classification debated; microwear > shoulder-width alone — Aoyama vs Thomas/Rovner rules of thumb).

**ICON.** Teotihuacan-style goggle/“Tlaloc” warrior kits; dart bundles on Early Classic monuments.

**Controversy.** Popular “Mexican atlatl revolution” overstated — **supplemented** lances (Aoyama & Graham).

**Game-use:** Mid-range armour-piercing / anti-elite unit; Early Classic tech unlock tied to “entrada” event.

### 2.3 Bow & arrow (late / secondary in Classic; strong by Contact)
**Classic (**ARCH**).** Small prismatic-blade points and side-notched points at **Aguateca** and **Copan** = arrowheads by microwear; **rare** in art; low % of assemblages → **not** the main Classic war weapon (Aoyama 2005). Present **earlier** than the old “Terminal Classic Mexican introduction” model.

**Postclassic / Contact (**ETHNO**).** Landa: bows of strong wood, reed arrows with flint or fish-tooth tips, great skill; no poison. Bernal Díaz: Maya with bows, lances, shields, stones at first landings (1517+).

**Game-use:** Cheap mass tower DPS unlocked late Classic → dominant Postclassic age; do not make Classic Tikal an archer blob.

### 2.4 Obsidian & chert
- **Obsidian:** razor volcanic glass; sourced (El Chayal, Ixtepeque, Pachuca green for Early Classic prestige). Better bleeding wounds (Lacandon ethno-analogy — treat as colour, not Classic proof).
- **Chert / flint (*toʼkʼ*):** local workhorse for spear/dart points; *toʼkʼ pakal* = army metonym.
- **Macuahuitl-like** edged clubs: clearer in **central Mexico**; Maya Contact accounts describe wood + inset stone “two-handed swords”; Classic depictions of bladed clubs exist but **do not** assume every Maya warrior carried Aztec *macuahuitl*. Chichen Itza reliefs often cited — **Postclassic / Epiclassic** context.

**Game-use:** Obsidian = high crit / bleed; chert = standard ammo; green Pachuca = rare Early Classic import buff.

### 2.5 Shields (*pakal*)
**Classic (**ICON**).** Flexible sizes: small rectangular (Teotihuacan-linked), round, flexible hide; war sergeants / kings shown with *pakal*. Word *pakal* secure in compounds.

**Contact (**ETHNO** Landa).** Round shields of split cane, woven, faced with deer hide (*rodela*).

**Game-use:** Shield-wall upgrade; *pakal* as unit icon and army noun.

### 2.6 Cotton / quilted armour
**Contact certainty (**ETHNO**).** Yucatec warriors wore **quilted cotton jackets** to the knee (Bernal Díaz); Landa describes quilted cotton “sacks” — text says “salt,” but Tozzer correction: confusion of Yucatec *tab* (salt) vs *taab* (twisted cord) → **cord-quilted cotton**, not brine-soaked necessarily. Spanish often **adopted** cotton armour over steel in heat. Nahuatl *ichcahuipilli* is the central Mexican label; Maya had the **practice**, not necessarily that name.

**Classic evidence.** Perishable → thin. Possible quilting on Jaina figurines / Yaxchilan costumes (**ICON** — suggestive, not proof of battlefield armour). Do **not** claim Classic *ichcahuipilli* as attested term.

**Helmets.** Landa: few wooden helm-like pieces for captains; Classic *koʼhaw* “helmet” in texts (**EPI**).

**Game-use:** Cotton armour = mid-tier HP vs darts/arrows; weak vs Spanish steel in conquest scenario DLC. Classic era: light textile / hide default.

### 2.7 Other
- **Sling / stones:** Contact accounts; clay slingstones reported archaeologically in some Mesoamerican contexts.
- **Axes:** stone / rare metal (Contact soft copper-ish axes — Landa) for war and woodwork.
- **Fire:** *puluuy* + Aguateca burn = strategic weapon.

## 3. Military organization

### 3.1 Kingship apex
- ***Ajaw* / *kʼuhul ajaw*:** holy lord; war leader in texts and art — captures attributed to rulers; rulers shown as warriors (spears, shields, Teotihuacan war costume).
- Warfare legitimizes accession (captive-taking early in reign — Yaxchilan pattern).

### 3.2 Secondary elite & titles (**EPI** — functions debated)
| Title | Gloss | Military weight |
|---|---|---|
| *sajal* | provincial / subordinate lord | Often wartime actors; regional captains |
| *yajaw kʼahkʼ* | “lord of fire” | High office; sometimes war-credited (Safronov et al. readings vary: priest vs commander) |
| *yajaw teʼ* | “lord of the tree/wood/spear?” | Debated |
| *baah pakal* / *baah toʼkʼ* / *baah teʼ* | “head shield / flint / spear” | Achieved / appointed; strongly martial flavour |
| *lakam* | banner-ish office? | Possibly mid-tier officials / levy organizers (Stuart) |
| *uchaʼan [Name]* | captor of [enemy] | Prestige from specific captive |
| *aj N baak* | he of N captives | Scoreboard title |

**Do not invent** a neat general staff. Titles ≠ European ranks.

### 3.3 Contact-period militia (**ETHNO** — Postclassic lens)
Landa: war captains (*nacom* in some colonial vocabularies) held office for terms; *holkan* / *holcan* “warriors” supported by community during campaigns; town labour carried supplies (no pack animals). Useful for **Postclassic campaign**, anachronistic if pasted onto Tikal 700 CE without label.

### 3.4 Captives: sacrifice vs labour vs politics
**Sacrifice (**ICON/EPI**).** Bonampak murals: display, torture, heart/blood themes; captive bodies under rulers’ feet on stelae; scaffold sacrifice imagery (Taube). Elite captives especially prized.

**Not only sacrifice (**MOD** Earley 2023; Graham).** Texts and ethnohistory allow: **hostage / political leverage**, **slavery / labour**, **adoption into court**, delayed killing. Monument captive ≠ automatic next-day altar death.

**Labour.** Enslavement and porterage attested at Contact; Classic labour extraction via *puluuy* aftermath and vassal tribute plausible but hard to quantify.

**Trophy logic.** *Baak* = captive/bone; trophy-taking and finger-breaking of scribes (Johnston; Aguateca context) = targeted elite terror.

**Game-use:** Capture resource separate from kill XP — spend on ritual power **or** worker slots **or** diplomatic ransoms. Capturing enemy *ajaw* = regime change victory condition.

## 4. Fortifications (site dossier)

### 4.1 Becán (Campeche) — best Classic-adjacent **designed** fortress
| Feature | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Plan | Kidney-shaped **dry** ditch + inner parapet enclosing civic core | Webster 1976 |
| Circumference | **~1.9 km** (also cited ~6,200 ft) | Webster; INAH |
| Ditch width | **avg ~16 m** (~52.5 ft) | Webster |
| Ditch depth | **avg ~5.3 m**; range cited **~1.2–5.7 m** (4–18.7 ft) | Webster; INAH |
| Parapet | Originally **~5 m** high (ditch fill) | Webster |
| Effective face | Ditch + parapet ≈ **~11 m** vertical obstacle | Webster |
| Causeways | **7** bedrock bridges left in place | Webster / INAH |
| Enclosed area | **~0.19 km²** | Webster |
| Fill volume | **~117,600 m³** (~352,000 person-days estimate) | Webster |
| Date | Late Preclassic **~100–250 CE** (Ball ceramics); maintained into Early Classic; later cleaning debated | Ball & Andrews; Webster & Freidel “Rehabilitating Becán” 2021 |
| Landscape | Seasonal **bajos**/swamps NE & SW as screens; Aguada Carmelita water ~500 m south | Webster 2021 |

**Critical controversy.** Popular “moat” images are wrong — **dry ditch** (no water-laid sediments; ditch bottom elevations prevent continuous flooding). Secondary roles: borrow pit for fill, drainage, elite precinct demarcation (**INAH** stresses multi-function). Webster: still **primarily defensive**.

**Game-use:** Gold-standard **military keep** map — 7 gates as chokepoints; dry ditch as anti-siege (attackers funnel on causeways).

### 4.2 Mayapan (Yucatán) — Postclassic urban wall
- Outer wall **~9.1 km** — largest known Mesoamerican enclosure wall (Shook 1952; Russell 2013).
- Height **~1.5–2.5 m** (breastwork scale; likely **palisade** on top).
- Inner ceremonial precinct wall **~700 m**.
- Gates with defensive baffling; ethnohistoric “gate guardians” by lineage direction (Roys).
- Secondary functions: traffic control, ritual inclusion/exclusion of cenotes, internal population control, symbolism (**MOD** Russell — still affirms defence as primary gate logic).

**Game-use:** Late-age mega-map; low stone wall + wooden stakes; many gates; civic vs ceremonial double wall.

### 4.3 Tulum (Quintana Roo) — cliff + wall
- Three-sided masonry wall; **Caribbean cliffs** = fourth side (**ARCH** Lothrop 1924; Sanders 1960; Webster summary).
- Great Wall landward run **~400 m**; N/S arms **~170 m**; enclosed **~7 ha**.
- Wall **~3–4 m** high, **~8 m** thick (Webster 1976 synthesis; Armillas figures sometimes cite ~5 m / 7 m — note variance).
- Smaller southern enclosure + cliff breastworks.
- Late Postclassic (~1200–1450 intensive).

**Game-use:** Coastal map; sea = impassable; land gates only; naval raid variant.

### 4.4 Aguateca (Petexbatun) — landscape fortress + hasty walls
- **~90 m** escarpment; natural **grieta** (chasm) splits site.
- **>4 km / ~4,506 m** of stone defensive walls (hastily built late 8th c.), concentric emphasis on Palace Group (**ARCH** Inomata).
- Wall heights often modest (**~0.5–1.8 m**) — bases for **timber palisades**.
- Rapid elite abandonment under attack **~810**; rich weapon floors.

**Game-use:** Vertical map — cliff denies half approaches; chasm as hazard lane; final “burn the palace” lose condition.

### 4.5 Dos Pilas — concentric panic walls
- After **761** defeat / flight of dynasty: refugees strip temples/palaces to build **concentric rubble walls** + wooden palisades around Main Group & El Duende — **ignoring** prior sacred plan (**ARCH** Demarest; Houston).
- Embodies Terminal crisis: sacred stone recycled into keep.

**Game-use:** Disaster scenario — demolish your own temples for wall HP; morale penalty; short campaign.

### 4.6 Punta de Chimino — engineered isthmus kill-zone
- Peninsula cut by massive ditch **~150 m** long, **~12 m** deep → artificial island (**ARCH** Demarest / Wolley).
- Additional outer ditches + stone walls; inter-ditch space possibly protected fields.
- Control of lake canoe routes.

**Game-use:** Triple-ditch puzzle map; water logistics.

### 4.7 Other / landscape defence
| Site | Feature | Period |
|---|---|---|
| **Tikal** earthworks | N ditch ~**9.5 km**, ~**4×4 m**, parapet; articulates with bajos; S earthwork also known | Long read as defence (Puleston & Callender); **reassessed** — may be unfinished / hydraulic / non-functional as fortress (Webster et al. 2007; Silverstein hydraulic hypothesis). **Flag controversy** |
| **Chacchob** | Stone wall ~2 km perimeter, 7–8 m thick rubble, 1–2 m high | Late Classic? |
| **Cuca** | **Concentric** double walls | Late Classic / Terminal |
| **Edzná** | Possible defensive earthworks in hydraulic landscape | Classic |
| **Los Naranjos** (Honduras fringe) | Early ditch+bank ~10 m wide / deep profiles | Middle Preclassic candidate |
| **Calakmul** | Wall fragments reported (Ruppert & Denison) — understudied | ? |
| Walled **residential compounds** | Ubiquitous low walls (Mayapan especially) | Social control + last-ditch |

**Landscape tactics:** bajos, escarpments, lakes, peninsulas, *sacbe* control, seasonal flooding as force multipliers — Maya “walls” often **complete** natural screens.

## 5. Best TD centerpiece — recommendation

### Dual recommendation (preferred)
1. **Sacred brand core: Tikal Temple I (Temple of the Great Jaguar) + North Acropolis / Great Plaza**  
   - Instantly readable Maya silhouette; dynastic *Mutal* emblem; Late Classic resurrection after 562 defeat.  
   - North Acropolis = ancestral necropolis = “lose the bones / lose the dynasty” stakes.  
   - **Not** primarily a fortress — pair with military keep (below).

2. **Military keep: Becán ditch-and-parapet**  
   - Best-measured Preclassic/Classic defensive machine; 7 causeway gates; dry ditch clarity; swamps as outer layer.  
   - Visually distinct from pyramid cliché; teaches that Maya war engineering is **real**.

### Why not the others as sole centerpiece
| Candidate | Pros | Cons for sole hub |
|---|---|---|
| **Calakmul** | Snake kingdom (*Kaan*) superpower; huge site | Fortifications poorly characterized; less iconic single tower |
| **Chichen Itza** | Ballcourt + Castillo fame; militarized Postclassic art | Chronology/politics contested; “Toltec” baggage; less Classic primary |
| **Tikal alone** | Brand king | Earthwork defence **controversial**; looks “open city” |
| **Becán alone** | Perfect keep | Weak global brand vs Tikal silhouette |

**Design split:** Hub world = **Tikal sacred precinct** (Temple I as final tower). Challenge maps / endless = **Becán**. Seasonal villains = Calakmul *Kaan* overlords. Postclassic expansion = Mayapan / Tulum.

## 6. Layered defence options (~7 layers from Maya sites)

Compose a ladder **outside → inside** using attested types (not one site’s literal section):

| Layer | Maya-rooted feature | Attestation |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Landscape screen** | Bajos, swamps, lake, seasonal flood, escarpment | Becán bajos; Aguateca cliff; Tulum sea; Petexbatun lakes |
| **2. Outer earthwork / long ditch** | Linear ditch + bank across approaches | Tikal N earthwork (function debated); Los Naranjos |
| **3. Dry fosse + parapet / palisade** | Broad excavated ditch, inner embankment, timber top | **Becán** gold standard |
| **4. Concentric rubble walls** | Multi-ring hastily built stone + wood | Dos Pilas; Aguateca wall nets; Cuca double wall |
| **5. Urban enclosure wall + gated baffles** | Low masonry circuit, controlled gates | **Mayapan** 9.1 km; Tulum Great Wall; Chacchob |
| **6. Walled compounds / civic precinct wall** | Elite patio groups; inner ceremonial wall | Mayapan precinct ~700 m; house-lot walls |
| **7. Pyramid / acropolis redoubt** | Temple-pyramid & palace as last hold | Ubiquitous (**MOD** Webster); Aguateca Palace Group focus; Tikal North Acropolis / Temple I as symbolic core |

**Optional hazard inserts:** *grieta* chasm (Aguateca); triple isthmus ditches (Punta de Chimino); cliff breastworks (Tulum).

**Game-use:** Mirror Roman *stimuli→principia* ladder and Babylon ziggurat tiers — Maya flavour via **bajo → Becán ditch → concentric → Mayapan gate → compound → Temple I**.

## 7. Famous conflicts & collapse violence

### 7.1 Tikal (*Mutal*) ↔ Calakmul (*Kaanul* / Snake) rivalry (**EPI** Martin & Grube)
Long-run “superpower” contest via vassals (Naranjo, Caracol, Dos Pilas, etc.), not only pairwise field battles.

| Date (CE) | Event (summary) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **378** | Teotihuacan-linked *entrada*; Spearthrower Owl / Sihyaj Kʼahkʼ; new Tikal regime | Military costume/atlatl ideology shift |
| **562** | Star war: **Caracol / Calakmul** defeat **Tikal** | Tikal monumental hiatus |
| **631 / 636** | Calakmul–Caracol vs **Naranjo** | Snake sphere enforcement |
| **672–677** | Tikal pushes Dos Pilas; La Corona actions | See Dos Pilas cycle |
| **695** | Tikal’s Jasaw Chan Kʼawiil I defeats Calakmul’s Yuknoom Yichʼaak Kʼahkʼ | Temple I lintels celebrate; revival |
| **697** | Naranjo burns **Bahlam Jol (Witzna)** — *puluuy* | Geoarchaeology match |
| **8th c.** | Continued proxy wars; Petexbatun militarization | |
| **761** | Dos Pilas falls to local revolt (Tamarindito etc.) | Concentric walls aftermath |
| **~810** | Aguateca epicenter destroyed | Termination warfare |

### 7.2 Dos Pilas story (game narrative gold)
Founded mid-7th c. as **Tikal splinter**; flips to **Calakmul** vassal under Bʼalaj Chan Kʼawiil; wars against Tikal (star wars 650s–700s); conquers Seibal 735; collapses **761**; remnant fortification from temple stone; Aguateca twin capital dies later.

### 7.3 Star war glyph
- Visual: star / Venus sign over earth, shell, or place glyph, often with droplets.
- Marks **major** inter-polity wars in tables compiled from monuments.
- **Reading undeciphered** (proposals: *jom*, *jub*, *hay*, *chʼay*, etc. — see TWKM notes).
- Venus link = research tradition from Lounsbury/Schele; **not settled science**.

### 7.4 Collapse violence (**MOD**)
- **Not** one pan-Maya war apocalypse.
- **Petexbatun:** warfare = proximate cause of political fall (Demarest).
- **Witzna:** shows destructive war **before** 9th-c. drought narrative peak.
- **Copan:** Acropolis weapon spikes + building destruction → violent end to centralized rule (Aoyama; Fash) — internal and/or external.
- Drought, trade break, elite rivalry, and war **interact**; avoid single-cause collapse.

**Game-use:** Act I Snake overlordship; Act II Mutal revenge; Act III Petexbatun fortress panic; epilogue Postclassic walled cities.

## 8. Ballgame ↔ war / captives (careful)

### Secure points
- Ballgame is **politically charged** elite ritual sport with cosmic overtones (**ICON/ARCH** courts in civic centers).
- Miller & Houston 1987: Classic ballgame as **ritual correlate of war and sacrifice** — influential; still a scholarly frame, not a rulebook.
- Captives appear in ballgame **imagery** (Yaxchilan HS.2; stairway “prisoner-as-ball” motifs; Chichen Great Ball Court reliefs with decapitation themes).
- La Amelia / other texts: victims “thrown” as the ball of a holy king — metaphorical political violence language (**EPI**).

### Cautions (do not cartoon)
1. **Not every match ends in death.** Frequency of sacrifice unknown; many games likely diplomatic / festival / status without killing.
2. **Popol Vuh** Hero Twins narrative is **Kʼicheʼ colonial-era** text — use for Postclassic highland flavour; **dangerous** as direct key to all Classic courts.
3. Chichen Itza decapitation panels ≠ proof Classic southern lowlands same liturgy.
4. “Winners sacrificed” tourist claim = usually **unsupported**.
5. Recent work (e.g. Yaxchilan HS.2 theses): prisoner-as-ball may signal **dominion / tribute strength**, not always literal game execution.

**Game-use:** Optional **Ballcourt event** after capturing a VIP — political spectacle minigame. Decapitation finisher = rare, Postclassic Chichen skin, **content-flagged**. Do not make ballcourt the primary war resolution.

## 9. Obscure gameable details (attested hooks)

1. **Seven Becán gates** — numbered causeway bosses; swamp “lanes” disable two approaches in rainy season.
2. **Dry ditch ≠ moat** — teach players: depth + parapet, not water (anti-Hollywood).
3. ***Toʼkʼ pakal* meter** — army strength as flint+shield icon; *jubuy* = army break debuff.
4. **Count-of-captives titles** — leaderboard *aj 1 baak* → *aj 20 baak* prestige tree (Yaxchilan).
5. **Scribe-warriors** — Aguateca: palettes beside spear points; enemy targets hands/fingers (Johnston). Tower: “Scribe Captain” support unit that buffs glyphs / loses DPS if “finger-broken.”
