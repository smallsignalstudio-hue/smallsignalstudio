# JAPANESE MILITARY UNITS AND TACTICS — Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Doc)

> English research notes for a tower-defence game bible (same project family as Greek / Viking / Chinese TD research).  
> **Method:** Prefer **archaeology** + **period texts** (chronicles, war tales with bias flagged, Azuchi–Momoyama campaign records, Jesuit letters) over samurai cinema and Victorian *bushidō* myth. Flag every claim with an evidence class. Do **not** invent TO&Es, company sizes, or a “standard samurai loadout.”  
> **Hard framing:** **Japan ≠ one timeless army.** Periodize. **Katana-primary battlefield** = **FOLK**. **Black-clad ninja army** = mostly **FOLK**. **Bushidō as medieval warrior code** = largely **late / Victorian–Meiji ideology**, not Sengoku field doctrine.

### Evidence tags (use throughout)
| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Archaeology (castles, weapons, armour, battlefield survey, ship remains, production sites) |
| **LIT** | Japanese literary / chronicle tradition (*gunki monogatari*, bakufu chronicles, domain histories—**bias and inflation flagged**) |
| **CHR** | Near-contemporary campaign records, letters, diaries; **Azuchi–Momoyama** sources; **Jesuit letters** (Frois et al.) as outsider eyewitness with missionary framing |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarship (Friday, Conlan, Berry, Hall, Asakawa tradition, Japanese castle / arms archaeology; Turnbull = popular—**cross-check**) |
| **FOLK** | Samurai cinema, anime/games, Victorian *bushidō*, tourist “ninja village” kitsch, wargame TO&Es treated as fact |

|---|---|

### Core modern scholarship (entry points)
Karl F. **Friday** (*Hired Swords*; *Samurai, Warfare and the State*; archery/warfare essays); Thomas D. **Conlan** (*State of War*; Mongol invasion studies; Sengoku force composition debates); Mary Elizabeth **Berry** (*Hideyoshi*); John Whitney **Hall** / **Jansen** / **McClain** (eds.) on castles and state formation; William Wayne **Farris**; Stephen **Turnbull** (readable popular synthesis—**verify**); Matthew **Stavros** (Kyoto urban militarisation); Japanese specialists on castles (*jōkaku*) and arms (e.g. Fujimoto and peers in Japanese-language military archaeology); Geoffrey **Parker** tradition on “military revolution” applied cautiously to Japan; Imjin War: Kitajima / Swope / Turnbull (filter). On *bushidō* invention: Oleg **Benesch**, *Inventing the Way of the Samurai*.

## 0. Framing: what Japanese armies were

**Not one timeless “samurai army.”** Military organisation, primary weapons, and social recruitment change sharply across eras. A Heian mounted archer household is not a Sengoku ashigaru pike-and-shot column. **MOD**

**Periodisation for this bible (mandatory unlocks, not costume skins).**

| Era (broad) | Approx. | Military character |
|---|---|---|
| **Yayoi → Kofun** (light) | c. 300 BCE–600 CE | Village / chiefly conflict; bronze→iron; **horse complex enters** in Kofun via continent—elite mounted culture seeds later warrior prestige. **ARCH** |
| **Asuka–Nara–early Heian** | 7th–9th c. | Ritsuryō conscript ideals (*gundan*) weaken; provincial strongmen rise. **LIT** + **MOD** |
| **Heian warrior households** | 10th–12th c. | *Bushi* as landed fighters; **mounted archery** prestige; Genpei War climax. **LIT** *Heike* + **MOD** Friday |
| **Kamakura bakufu** | 1185–1333 | *Gokenin* vassalage; Mongol invasions (1274, 1281); defensive coastal mobilisation. **LIT** *Azuma kagami* + **ARCH**/**MOD** Conlan |
| **Muromachi → Sengoku** | 1336–c. 1568 | Fragmentation; **ashigaru** massing; *yamajiro* networks; pike culture grows. **MOD** + **LIT** |
| **Azuchi–Momoyama** | c. 1568–1600 | Unification wars; **teppō** integration; monumental castles; naval *atakebune*. **CHR** *Shinchōkōki*, Jesuits + **ARCH** |
| **Early Edo** | 1603–c. mid-17th c. | Demilitarisation pressures; *rakujo* castle reductions; sankin-kōtai; ideology of warrior peace—**not** Sengoku field reality frozen in amber. **CHR** + **MOD** Benesch on later *bushidō* |

**Social engine (conceptual—not a parade TO&E).**
1. **Bushi / samurai households** — warrior status groups serving lords for land/stipend; mounted and foot roles both exist; kit wealth-variable. **MOD** Friday  
2. **Ashigaru 足軽** — “light feet”; commoner / low-status infantry mass of Sengoku armies (yari, yumi, teppō). Not a timeless ancient class. **MOD** + **CHR**  
3. **Monastic armies (*sōhei*)** and **ikki leagues** — parallel force structures, not “flavour NPCs.” **LIT** + **MOD**  
4. **Shinobi** — real infiltration/espionage specialists in some domains; **cinema ninja corps** = **FOLK**  
5. **Naval specialists** — Inland Sea pirates (*kaizoku*), domain fleets, later armoured *atakebune*. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Evidence quality (framing).**
| Topic | Quality |
|---|---|
| Period change / no single army | **High** |
| Bow prestige in Heian–Kamakura elite war | **High** (**LIT** + **MOD**) |
| Sengoku spear + ashigaru mass | **High** (**MOD** + **CHR**) |
| Teppō adoption after 1543 | **High** (**CHR** + **ARCH**) |
| Exact “Oda three-rank rotating volley” as European clone | **Contested** (**MOD** debate) |
| Katana as primary battlefield weapon | **False / FOLK** for mass combat |
| Victorian *bushidō* = Sengoku ethics | **False** (**MOD** Benesch) |

**Game-use potential:** Faction = **period unlock tree** (Heian horse-archers → Kamakura vassal defence vs Mongols → Sengoku ashigaru/castle nets → Azuchi teppō–tenshu climax → Edo demilitarised epilogue). Never one “Samurai Empire” blob.

## 1. Units

### 1.1 Samurai — mounted and foot

**What.** Members of warrior houses (*buke*) serving a lord; status category more than a single weapon class. Across centuries they fight **mounted and on foot**. **MOD**

**Heian–Kamakura emphasis.** Elite combat ideology centres on **horse + yumi** (mounted archery), individualised challenge rhetoric in war tales, and household retainers—not European knightly lance charge as default. **LIT** *Heike* + **MOD** Friday

**Sengoku shift.** Large-field war and castle warfare push many samurai into **foot combat with yari**, command of ashigaru blocks, and firearm supervision. Mounted shock still exists but is **terrain- and horse-limited** (see §1.7). **MOD** Conlan / Friday

**Kit reality.** Lacquered lamellar / plate-laced armours (*yoroi*, later *tōsei gusoku*), helmets (*kabuto*), variously spears, bows, sidearms. Quality tracks wealth and role—do **not** give every named warrior full parade armour. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Controversies.**
1. War-tale **duels** deciding battles = literary craft, not operational norm. **LIT** vs **MOD**  
2. “Samurai never retreated / always sought death” = *bushidō* **FOLK**; Sengoku politics are full of negotiation, defection, and survival. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Mid/high-cost elites; early ages = horse-archer heroes; Sengoku = officer auras buffing ashigaru lanes; expensive to replace.

---

### 1.2 Ashigaru — yari, yumi, teppō

**What.** Mass infantry of the Sengoku–Azuchi armies: conscripts, volunteers, mercenaries, and low-status professionals under samurai officers. Name = “light feet.” **MOD**

**Weapon branches (roster, not rigid ratios).**
| Branch | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Yari ashigaru** | Dense spear lines / screens | Battlefield numerical backbone in many late Sengoku forces. **MOD** |
| **Yumi ashigaru** | Massed archery | Continues older bow culture at lower status cost. **MOD** + **ARCH** |
| **Teppō ashigaru** | Matchlock shot | Post-Tanegashima growth; specialised *teppō-gumi* in progressive domains. **CHR** + **MOD** |

**Equipment floor.** Often simpler armour (or partial), *jingasa* conical hats, sashimono back-banners for identification in smoke/dust—**do not** invent universal issue lists. **ARCH**/pictorial + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Cheap–medium wave units; yari = lane blockers; yumi = early tower-pressure; teppō = armour-pierce / tower-suppress with slow reload.

---

### 1.3 Sōhei (warrior monks)

**What.** Armed forces tied to major temples/shrines (e.g. Enryaku-ji on Hiei, Kōfuku-ji / Kinpusen networks in Nara region)—monastic labourers, affiliates, and hired fighters under religious institutions’ political power. **LIT** + **MOD**

**Kit associations.** **Naginata** strongly linked in art and tale tradition; bows and other arms also used. White hoods / monastic markers in iconography are partly conventional. **LIT**/art + **MOD**

**Role.** Capital politics, gate control, vendetta against rival temples and courtiers; later crushed or subordinated by unifiers (Nobunaga’s 1571 burning of Enryaku-ji is a major **CHR**/**LIT** landmark—atrocity and state-formation signal).

**Controversies.** “Monk army” as fanatic zealots only = oversimplify; they were **landed political actors**. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Faction mercenaries / map neutrals; high morale vs “desecration” events; naginata reach; temple-keep maps.

---

### 1.4 Ikkō-ikki

**What.** Militant leagues of **Jōdo Shinshū** (True Pure Land) adherents—peasants, priests, local warriors—holding territory and resisting daimyō (famous Ishiyama Hongan-ji struggle vs Nobunaga, 1570s). **CHR** + **MOD** Berry / Hall tradition

**Military character.** Fortified temple complexes, mass mobilisation, religious solidarity as cohesion technology—not a “peasant rabble only” meme. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Defence-leaning faction: temple-fort HP pools, fanatic regen, poor cavalry, strong vs morale shock.

---

### 1.5 Ninja / shinobi — myth vs espionage

**Cinema / FOLK package.** Black pyjamas, shuriken primary weapon, superhuman parkour clans as standing special-forces regiments, smoke-bomb fantasy. Mostly **Edo popular literature + 20th-c. media**. **FOLK**

**Historical core.** *Shinobi* / *kanchō* / scouting-infiltration practices: night entry, arson inside castles, gate-opening, rumour, map-theft, assassination rare but attested in campaign narratives; **Iga** and **Kōka (Kōga)** regions develop reputable specialist labour markets later hired by daimyō (incl. Tokugawa uses—**CHR**/**MOD**). Skills overlap with ordinary scouts, thieves, and local guides.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Specialists in infiltration exist | **Supported** (contextual) **CHR**/**MOD** |
| Black uniform battlefield ninja armies | **FOLK** |
| Shuriken as primary war weapon | **FOLK**/sidearm niche overstated |
| Every castle siege needs a ninja unit | Design choice—not historical law |

**Game-use:** **Saboteur hero**: opens gates, silences a tower, starts fire DoT inside keep—**not** a main lane infantry class. Label cinema skins as **FOLK** cosmetics.

---

### 1.6 Other land types (brief)

- **Attendants / labour** — porters, sappers, camp followers; siege earthworks depend on them. **MOD**  
- **Allied / defecting contingents** — Sengoku warfare is coalition politics; betrayal is a system, not an exception (Sekigahara). **CHR**  
- **Women in defence** — occasional documented castle defence participation and literary heroines (e.g. Tomoe Gozen—**LIT**); **onna-musha** as mass unit = overreach; use as rare heroes or garrison events. **LIT** + **MOD**

---

### 1.7 Cavalry limits

**What exists.** Mounted archery and mounted spearmen; messenger/command mobility; pursuit on suitable ground. **LIT** + **MOD**

**Constraints (do not Europeanise).**
1. **Japanese horses** historically smaller than modern Western warhorses—shock weight limited. **ARCH**/**MOD**  
2. **Terrain**: mountains, rice paddies, narrow roads, timber—poor cavalry country vs open steppe. **MOD**  
3. **Castle belts** and field entrenchments blunt charges (Nagashino palisade problem). **CHR** + **ARCH**  
4. Mass **ashigaru yari** screens punish reckless horse. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Fast flankers / archer cav with **terrain penalties** on mud/paddy tiles; weak vs spear wall and stake towers.

---

### 1.8 Navy (brief) — *atakebune* and coastal war

**Inland Sea / coastal warfare.** Piracy, tolls, domain fleets; Murakami and other sea powers. **MOD** + **CHR**

***Atakebune* 安宅船.** Large, often **timber-armoured** warships used in late Sengoku–Azuchi naval fights (Seto Inland Sea campaigns; Oda–Mōri contests). Not “Japanese galleons” clones—oared/sailed hybrid traditions; gun and archery platforms. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Smaller classes (names vary by source).** *Sekibune*, *kobaya*—faster escorts/raiders. Do not invent a rigid navy TO&E. **MOD**

**Imjin War note.** Japanese amphibious lift to Korea succeeds initially; Korean/Ming naval counters (incl. Yi Sun-sin’s campaigns; *geobukseon* “turtle ship” fame) show **logistics and sea control** decide overseas TD maps. **CHR** Korean/Ming + **MOD** Swope

**Game-use:** Coastal lanes: landing craft spawns; *atakebune* as floating artillery towers; chain booms / shore batteries as defence.

## 2. Weapons

### 2.1 Yumi — asymmetric longbow

**What.** Japanese longbow: characteristically **asymmetric** grip (lower limb shorter), laminated bamboo/wood/cane construction; shot on foot and especially from horseback in elite tradition. **ARCH** + **MOD** Friday

**Role by period.** Prestige primary of Heian–Kamakura elite war; remains important through Sengoku even as yari/teppō rise—do not delete bows in 1543. **MOD**

**Effective culture.** Training, arrow types, and rate-of-fire skill matter; mass ashigaru archery ≠ every archer is a *Heike* hero. **LIT** vs **MOD**

**Game-use:** Early-age signature tower + horse-archer unit; high skill ceiling; later shares sky with teppō.

---

### 2.2 Yari — spear primacy on Sengoku fields

**What.** Shafted spear; lengths vary (short fighting spears to long ashigaru pikes). Thrusting primary; specialised blades exist. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**Why primacy.** Cheaper training than mastery archery; dense formations; anti-cavalry; castle-gate fighting. Modern consensus: **polearms dominate mass melee**, not swords. **MOD**

**Game-use:** Default ashigaru wall; anti-cav bonus; upgrade length = reach vs cavalry but slower turn.

---

### 2.3 Naginata

**What.** Curved blade on a pole (glaive-like). Associated with *sōhei* and some warrior-house use; later Edo curricula link naginata to **women of samurai households**—partly **peacetime gender ideology**, not proof that Sengoku armies were naginata-female by default. **LIT**/Edo pedagogy + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Sweep / anti-infantry specialist; sohei faction weapon; optional female hero kit **labelled**.

---

### 2.4 Tachi / katana — status vs battlefield

**What.** Curved single-edged swords: earlier **tachi** (hanging edge-down for cavalry), later **katana** mounting styles; paired with short blades in peacetime wear culture. Superb metallurgy traditions (**ARCH** sword archaeology / craft history).

**Battlefield role.** **Sidearm / status / close work** when polearm lost or in confined fighting—not the statistical primary of ashigaru megabattles. Cinema “katana duel wars” = **FOLK**. **MOD** Friday / Conlan

**Nodachi / ōdachi.** Oversized field swords—specialist / prestige / anti-cavalry claims in tradition; rare, not standard issue. **ARCH** + **LIT**

**Game-use:** Officer sidearm finisher; honour duel **minigame optional**; never replace yari line as core DPS.

---

### 2.5 Teppō after Tanegashima (1543)

**What.** Portuguese-style **matchlock arquebus** introduced via Tanegashima (tradition date **1543**—**CHR**/island tradition + **MOD**). Rapid domestic copying; major production at places like **Sakai** and **Kunitomo**. **ARCH** + **CHR**

**Tactical properties.** Slow reload; fearsome at close–medium against armour/horse; smoke, noise, morale shock; needs dry match drill—rain is a mechanic. **CHR** Jesuits remark on Japanese enthusiasm for firearms.

**Organisation.** Domains form shot companies; combined with spears and palisades. Exact drill choreography debated (next subsection).

**Game-use:** Mid-late unlock tower + unit; high damage, low RoF; “wet season” debuff; production building = Sakai workshop.

---

### 2.6 Tanegashima tactics and the Oda volley debate

**Popular claim (FOLK/popular MOD).** At **Nagashino 1575**, Oda Nobunaga invents/uses **three rotating ranks** of continuous volley fire like early modern Europe—guns alone smash Takeda cavalry.

**What is securer.**
- Nobunaga–Tokugawa forces deploy **large numbers of teppō** behind **wooden stockades / obstacles** that break cavalry impetus. **CHR** *Shinchōkōki* tradition + **MOD**  
- Concentrated firearms + terrain engineering + combined arms matter more than a single “invented volley drill” myth. **MOD** Conlan and others critique Turnbull-style set-piece volley narratives.  
- Whether a strict European-style rotating three-rank system was used as often depicted remains **debated**—do not hard-lock as undisputed fact. **MOD**

**Jesuit / European analogies.** Outsiders describe disciplined Japanese firearm use; analogy ≠ photocopy of Dutch drill manuals. **CHR** + **MOD**

**Game-use:** Nagashino scenario = **stakes + gun towers** beat pure cavalry rush; optional “volley doctrine” tech is a **labelled hypothesis**, not gospel.

## 3. Formations, campaigns, and tactics

### 3.1 Sengoku castle-siege norms

**Castle evolution.** Mountain castles (*yamajiro*) → more complex networks → Azuchi–Momoyama monumental castles with **stone walls (*ishigaki*)**, multiple baileys (*maru*), gates, and *tenshu* keeps. **ARCH** + **MOD**

**How sieges often work (patterns, not one script).**
1. **Investment** — surround, cut roads/water, build rival forts.  
2. **Negotiation / bribery / defection** — extremely common. **CHR**  
3. **Starvation / disease** — time as a weapon.  
4. **Mining, fire, night escalade, gate betrayal** — more typical than Western cannon-breaching (Japan lacks heavy siege-gun culture on the European scale for much of the period). **MOD**  
5. **Assault** — costly; attempted when politics demand speed.

**Defence tools.** Moats (*hori*), earthen ramparts, timber palisades, loopholes for bows/guns, maze-like gate complexes (*masugata* and related bent-entry designs in mature castles), stone-drop / missile from walls. **ARCH**

**Game-use:** Multi-bailey maps = layered tower rings; “politics” resource buys enemy defection; starvation clock on long sieges.

---

### 3.2 Nagashino 1575 — flag myths vs usable history

**Event.** Oda–Tokugawa defence vs Takeda assault near Nagashino/Shitaragahara region; Takeda defeat as landmark in firearm-era narrative. **CHR** + **MOD**

| Myth / overclaim | Better framing |
|---|---|
| Guns alone invent modern war overnight | Combined arms + obstacles + numbers + command |
| Perfect three-rank conveyor-belt volleys proven in detail | **Debated reconstruction** |
| Takeda = stupid pure cavalry romantics | Oversimplified; still a serious army caught in bad tactical conditions |
| Samurai refuse guns as dishonourable | **FOLK**—elites adopt and supervise teppō eagerly |

**Game-use:** Tutorial in “stakes beat chargers”; do not moralise firearms as anti-samurai.

---

### 3.3 Sekigahara 1600

**What.** Climactic coalition battle in the struggle ending in Tokugawa hegemony; fog, terrain corridors, and **side-switching** (notably Kobayakawa Hideaki’s pivotal turn in standard narratives). **CHR**/**LIT** tradition + **MOD**

**TD lesson.** Not only DPS—**loyalty meters**, delayed traitor waves, fog-of-war. Field battle ≠ castle siege; use open-map mode with ridge towers.

---

### 3.4 Amphibious / coastal operations

- **Seto Inland Sea** control as strategic oxygen for western Honshū powers. **MOD**  
- **Kyushu** and island stepping-stones—piracy and daimyō navy blends.  
- **Imjin invasions** — large-scale landing, castle-taking in Korea, then naval interdiction nightmares for Japanese supply. **CHR** + **MOD**  
- Shore forts, watch, and chain/boom fantasies as defence modules (document per harbour—do not invent universal hardware).

**Game-use:** Dual-map: sea lane + beach spawn; destroy transports before troops hit shore.

---

### 3.5 Earlier anchors (light)

- **Genpei (1180–85):** horse-bow prestige war in tale memory—early-age aesthetic. **LIT**  
- **Mongol invasions (1274, 1281):** coastal defence, boarding fights, weather disasters in sources (*kamikaze* tradition)—defence-leaning campaign; numbers and divine-wind theology need **LIT**/**CHR** caution. **MOD** Conlan  
- **Nanbokuchō:** prolonged irregular/castle warfare—attrition mode. **LIT** *Taiheiki*

## 4. Best attack vs best defence for TD

### 4.1 Attacker toolkit (what actually pressures a keep)

| Approach | Why it works historically | TD implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Ashigaru mass + yari** | Overwhelms thin garrisons | Cheap wave spam with shield-peer morale |
| **Teppō suppression** | Forces heads-down on walls | Armour-pierce / silence towers briefly |
| **Yumi / fire arrows** | Soften, ignite, harass | DoT on wooden towers |
| **Sappers & labour** | Earthworks, fills moats, mines | Build points: fascines (*taketaba*), fill-moat ability |
| **Shinobi / betrayal** | Opens gates without breach | Targeted gate debuff / convert tower |
| **Starvation clock** | Classic Sengoku win condition | Map timer: defender gold decay |
| **Naval landing** | Flank inland forts | Shore spawn behind river line |
| **Ikki / sōhei zeal** | High cohesion assaults | Resist flee effects |
| **Cavalry** | Only on open tiles | Flank spawn; dies to stakes |

**Poor attacker ideas (avoid as “historical default”).** Katana hero soloing gates; endless honourable 1v1s; European cannon parks in 1500; ninja armies replacing ashigaru.

---

### 4.2 Defender toolkit (what holds)

| Approach | Why | TD implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bailey layering** | Fall-back rings | 3–7 concentric path folds |
| **Moat + bridge kill zones** | Channel attackers | Slow debuff water tiles |
| **Masugata-style bent gates** | Break rush momentum | Corner towers with crossfire |
| **Yumi/teppō towers** | Missile density | Classic TD towers |
| **Yari wall at breaches** | Hold once stone fails | Barricade deployable |
| **Stockades / stakes** | Nagashino lesson | Anti-cav field towers |
| **Sorties** | Active defence | Timed defender wave |
| **Negotiation UI** | Historical | Bribe to delay a wave |
| **Temple-fort HP** (ikki maps) | Mass civilian-fighter pool | Militia regen buildings |
| **Coast batteries / booms** | Amphibious deny | Pre-shore damage |

**Defence wins when** attackers lack time, dry weather for matchlocks, loyalty, or naval cover. **Attack wins when** politics cracks the gate faster than walls crack.

---

### 4.3 Period “best” pairings (design shortcuts)

| Period unlock | Best attack flavour | Best defence flavour |
|---|---|---|
| Heian / Genpei | Mounted archery raids | Manor / stockade, bow towers |
| Kamakura / Mongol | Boarding & beach push | Coastal watch, mass levy bows |

## 6. Designer do / don’t

### Do
- **Periodise** roster and maps (Heian ≠ Sengoku ≠ Edo).  
- Make **yari + yumi + teppō ashigaru** the numerical core of late eras.  
- Treat **castles as layered systems**, not single keeps.  
- Use **negotiation, betrayal, starvation** as first-class mechanics.  
- Flag **Nagashino volley** details as debated if you show rotating ranks.  
- Keep **cavalry limited** by terrain and stakes.  
- Use **Jesuit letters** and *Shinchōkōki* colour carefully (bias tagged).  
- Present **shinobi** as sabotage/espionage, cinema as cosmetic.  
- Separate **Imjin overseas** logistics from home-island castle wars.  
- Cite evidence tags in internal design locks.

### Don’t
- Make the **katana** the primary battlefield weapon of mass armies. (**FOLK**)  
- Ship a single timeless “samurai honour army.”  
- Equate **Victorian/Meiji *bushidō*** with Sengoku field ethics. (**FOLK**/late ideology)  
- Field **black-pajama ninja regiments** as historical core. (**FOLK**)  
- Claim Nobunaga “invented modern warfare single-handed.”  
- Give Japan **European siege trains** as default 16th-c. kit.  
- Ignore **ashigaru**, temples, and ikki.  
- Paste **Dynasty Warriors / cinema** choreography as doctrine.  
- Invent precise **company TO&Es** or universal loadouts.  
- Treat war-tale duels as after-action reports. (**LIT**)  
- Overstate **cavalry shock** on paddies and mountains.  
- Use **horned / fantasy** armour kits as archaeology.
