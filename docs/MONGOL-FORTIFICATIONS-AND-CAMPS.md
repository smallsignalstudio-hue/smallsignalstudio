# MONGOL FORTIFICATIONS AND CAMPS — Tower-Defence Game Bible

> Dense research notes for **keep / map / layer** design across Mongol imperial practice (~1206–1368 core; Yuan late-empire extension).  
> **Method:** Prefer measured archaeology and near-contemporary travel / chronicle testimony. Flag soft estimates and secondary handbook numbers. **No invented European-style concentric stone castles** as the native Mongol default keep.  
> Companion frame (same project style): Chinese imperial wall grammar for Yuan Dadu; European / Japanese / Chinese keeps as **attacker-facing** reverse maps.

### Evidence tags (use throughout)

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ARCH** | Excavation, survey, geophysics, UNESCO / museum site documentation, measured wall ruins |
| **CHR** | Near-contemporary chronicles / travel (William of Rubruck, *Yuan shi*, Juvaynī, Rashīd al-Dīn, *Secret History* tradition where noted) |
| **MOD** | Modern scholarly consensus or active debate (Bemmann / Reichert / Hüttel / Allsen / Di Cosmo, etc.) |
| **FOLK** | Popular culture / tourist myth (“Mongols never fortify,” endless golden yurt mega-castles, pure horse-wave memes) — flavour only |

**Design rule:** Prefer **ARCH/CHR** for map geometry and layer stacks. Use **MOD** where excavation numbers disagree or secondary handbooks compress ranges. Never let **FOLK** overwrite wall thicknesses, gate counts, or the fact that steppe power often **borrowed** Chinese urban fortification rather than inventing Western castles.

**Chronology note:** Absolute CE years for Ögödei’s palace programme (**1235**), Rubruck’s visit (**1253–1254**), Dadu wall works (**1267–1276** band), and Zhongdu’s fall (**1215**) are hard anchors **[CHR]**. Soft estimates below are labelled **SOFT EST.**

## 0. What “Mongol fortification” is — and is not

### 0.1 Is
- **Mobile *ordu* / ger camps** as the real operational headquarters — portable, seasonal, pasture-tied **[CHR][MOD]**
- **Implanted steppe capitals** with modest enclosure walls (Karakorum) serving ideology, logistics, artisans, and court ritual more than high-medieval siege endurance **[ARCH][CHR][MOD]**
- **Conquered / adapted Chinese cities** — Jin Zhongdu sacked then superseded; Yuan **Dadu / Khanbaliq** built as a full Chinese imperial capital with rammed-earth (*hangtu*) enceinte **[ARCH][CHR]**
- **Siege camps facing walls** — blockade rings, engineer parks, captive artisan corps, famine pressure — the Mongols’ signature *offensive* fortification grammar **[CHR][MOD]**
- Late-empire **palace city + outer city + moat** stacks identical in language to Chinese imperial TD keeps (because they *are* Chinese imperial keeps under Mongol rule) **[ARCH]**

### 0.2 Is not (default TD keep)
- A native tradition of **European concentric curtain castles** with corner donjons as the Mongol brand **[MOD]**
- Treating Karakorum as a **Beijing-scale military megawall** — archaeology and contemporaries both limit its defensive ambition **[ARCH][CHR][MOD]**
- “Mongols never used walls / cities” — false; they founded, occupied, and administered them when politics required **[ARCH][CHR]**
- Pure **FOLK** golden-yurt fortress with fantasy floating banners as the only keep option

### 0.3 Geographic / chronological frame

| Horizon | Defence language | Signature sites / modes |
|---|---|---|
| United empire (Chinggis–Möngke) | Ordu mobility + early Karakorum | Steppe camps; 1215 Zhongdu siege |
| Ögödei–Möngke capital phase | Modest city wall + palace enclosure | Karakorum (Orkhon Valley) |
| Yuan (Khubilai+) | Chinese capital fortification | Dadu / Khanbaliq; Shangdu seasonal capital |
| Attacker signature (all phases) | Siege ring vs sedentary walls | Zhongdu, Kaifeng, Baghdad, European / Japanese / Chinese city maps |

## 1. Mobile ordu / ger camps as operational bases

### 1.1 What an *ordu* is
- **Ordu** (also *ordo*): the ruler’s / prince’s **camp-court** — political centre that moves with pasture, season, war, and ritual calendar **[CHR][MOD]** Allsen, Atwood, Di Cosmo syntheses
- Physical core = **ger** (felt tent / yurt) clusters, wagon parks, horse herds, kitchens, smithies, treasury wagons, captive artisan workshops — not a stone keep **[CHR]** Rubruck; Carpini; Persian historians
- Karakorum itself is repeatedly framed as one node in a **itinerant court circuit**: Ögödei and Möngke stayed for extended periods but did not abolish mobility **[MOD]** Atwood 2015; Bemmann & Reichert

### 1.2 Camp geometry for TD (playable, evidence-shaped)

| Layer idea | Historical hook | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Outer herd / picket screen | Livestock markets and horse parks at camp edges (Rubruck’s gate markets at Karakorum echo camp logic) | **[CHR]** |
| Wagon / cart laager options | Steppe armies used wagons for baggage; circular wagon defence is attested in wider Inner Asian practice — **do not** invent a single universal Mongol “wagon castle” plan without case citation | **[MOD]** flag |
| Ger rings by rank | Elite proximity to the khan’s ger; commoners / captives outward | **[CHR][MOD]** |
| Guard lanes / felt walls | Temporary screens, ditches, and posted sentries rather than masonry curtains | **[MOD]** |
| Workshop / smith / siege-park zone | Captive Chinese / Muslim engineers — the camp *is* the siege factory | **[CHR]** |
| Sacred / standard pole / *sulde* ideology node | Banner / spirit standards as morale totem (final room) | **[CHR][MOD]** |
| Remount herd as “HP regen” | Remount depth = operational endurance | **[MOD]** |

**Game bible stance:** Early–mid Mongol “keep” = **moving ordu arena**. Win/lose can be the **khan’s ger + treasury wagons**, not a donjon HP bar. Mobility unlocks = relocate the keep between waves (unique Mongol mechanic).

### 1.3 Why this matters vs European castles
- A European castle forces the attacker to come to a fixed rock. An ordu can **refuse decisive siege** until pasture, remounts, and engineer parks are ready **[MOD]**
- When Mongols *do* sit still against walls, they build **siege camps** (§5) — fixed aggression, not native castle culture
- TD implication: Mongol **defence** maps feel like layered camps + light earthworks; Mongol **offence** maps feel like Chinese / European / Japanese wall stacks under assault

## 2. Karakorum — ideological capital (ARCH limits)

### 2.1 Foundation and role
| Claim | Status | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Site chosen in Chinggis era (~**1220** tradition for camp / capital decision) | Historical tradition; urban build under successors | **[CHR][MOD]** |
| Ögödei establishes / expands the city; palace programme associated with **1235** | Strong | **[CHR]** *Yuan shi*; modern syntheses |
| Building programme continues under Möngke | Strong | **[CHR][ARCH]** |
| After ~**1260**, Khubilai develops **Dadu**; Karakorum retains ideological / regional status (later Lingbei Province centre) | Strong | **[CHR][MOD]** |
| Erdene Zuu monastery (**1586**) built on ruins of the walled palace area | Strong | **[ARCH]** |

### 2.2 Measured / surveyed size (do not inflate)

| Metric | Value | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| Walled area | **~1.33 km²** (**~135 ha** excluding Erdene Zuu in some statements) | **[ARCH]** Bemmann et al. *Antiquity* 2022 mapping; Bemmann & Reichert urbanism paper |
| Built-up share inside walls | Only ~**60%** of walled area shows dense build | **[ARCH]** |
| Settlement beyond walls | Extends into suburbs / roads; older literature sometimes ~**1300 ha** activity envelope including extramural zones — **order-of-magnitude landscape**, not a continuous megacity floor | **[ARCH][MOD]** flag as landscape envelope |
| Popular rectangle shorthand | ~**1.5 × 2.5 km** irregular enclosure appears in older / handbook descriptions | **[MOD]** soft secondary; prefer area figures from modern survey |
| Population (Rubruck-era soft) | Roughly **~7,000–12,000** derived from Rubruck’s comparison to Saint-Denis | **[CHR]→[MOD]** **SOFT EST.** — not a census |

**Design rule:** Karakorum is a **capital brand**, not a Dadu-scale HP pool.

### 2.3 Walls — deliberately limited defence

| Feature | What we know | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Outer city wall | Rammed earth (*hangtu*); relatively **low / modest**; defensive function long **questioned** by excavators and historians | **[ARCH][MOD]** Kiselev; Dardess; Bemmann & Reichert |
| Brick cladding | Outer wall often **without** full fired-brick face (unlike Chinese urban norms); palace enclosure **brick-veneered** and more serious | **[ARCH]** |
| Palace enclosure wall | Excavation under Erdene Zuu walls: earlier rammed-earth wall faced with bricks; reported ~**8 m** thick (much wider than later monastery fence) | **[ARCH]** Hüttel / Franken notes |
| Gates (city) | Rubruck: **four** main gates with specialised outer markets (E grain; W sheep/goats; S cattle/wagons; N horses) | **[CHR]** |
| Extra apertures | Old maps / survey imply **additional openings** beyond Rubruck’s four | **[ARCH][MOD]** |
| Street grid | Central N–S / E–W cross; excavated paved main road ~**5.6 m** wide (slate slabs in ~**3 × 2.8 m** fields separated by timber beams) | **[ARCH]** Pohl |
| Water | No clear piped system; track from west gate toward Orkhon meander for water fetch | **[ARCH]** |

**Rubruck on the palace (CHR colour for final room):** palace “resembles a church” with nave/aisles, three south doors, and the famous **silver tree** fountain of Guillaume Boucher opposite the middle door; khan seated elevated at the north end **[CHR]**.

### 2.4 ARCH limits — what designers must not invent
1. Do **not** give Karakorum Ming-Beijing wall thickness / height by default  
2. Do **not** place the excavated “large SE building” as the proven palace — Soviet identification is **superseded**; current consensus leans to palace **under Erdene Zuu**, still partly unexcavated out of respect for the living monastery **[ARCH][MOD]**  
3. Do **not** treat Rubruck’s four markets as proven excavation polygons — markets are **CHR-attested**, not yet cleanly matched to dig units **[ARCH]** silence  
4. Do **not** erase extramural sprawl — the city bleeds into the pasture **[ARCH]**

### 2.5 Karakorum TD reading (brand keep)
1. Orkhon floodplain / pasture approaches (mobility denial soft)  
2. Extramural artisan / kiln / suburb clutter  
3. Modest *hangtu* city wall + four market gates  
4. Craftsmen / “Cataian” quarter + “Saracen” merchant quarter  
5. Central crossroads / paved street choke  
6. Palace brick enclosure (Erdene Zuu footprint)  
7. Silver Tree / throne ideology room  

**Ideology damage type:** burning the silver tree / looting treasure barns hurts more than cracking the low outer wall — historically plausible because the outer wall was never the main military story **[MOD]**.

|---|---|---|
| Fall | **1215** (often 31 May / early June in modern summaries) | **[CHR]** hard event |
| Wall base thickness | ~**15 m** almost | Secondary synthesis repeating older descriptions — **SOFT EST. / secondary** until tied to a named excavation section |
| Parapet / wall height | ~**12 m** crenellated in the same secondary cluster | **SOFT EST.** |
| Gates | **13** in secondary accounts | **SOFT EST.** pending primary citation in design docs |
| Watchtowers | “**900+**” in secondary accounts | **SOFT EST.** — treat as flavour magnitude, not balance gospel |

### 3.3 Shangdu (Xanadu) — seasonal brand twin (optional)
- Khubilai’s Upper Capital on the steppe fringe — hunting / summer court ideology **[CHR]**
- Excellent **ordu + city hybrid** map if the team wants a second Yuan brand without repeating Dadu’s full wall math
- Do not substitute Shangdu metrics for Dadu without separate ARCH cards (out of scope numbers here = **no assumption**)

### 3.4 Late-empire design rule
When the player selects **Mongol late empire**, the military keep should read as **Yuan Dadu** (or siege-adapted Chinese city), **not** as a dressed-up Karakorum wall fantasy.

## 4. Siege camps facing walls — attacker TD reverse maps

### 4.1 Why this is the Mongol signature map
Mongol armies repeatedly win by **isolating cities**, defeating field armies, starving capitals, and deploying **captured Chinese / Muslim siege engineers** (traction/counterweight trebuchets, rams, mines, artillery) **[CHR][MOD]**. The “fortification” the Mongols build in these scenarios is the **siege camp ring**, not a castle.

### 4.2 Reverse-map grammar (player = Mongol attacker)

| Layer (outer → breach) | Function | Evidence hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Remount / herd parks | Operational fuel | Steppe logistics **[MOD]** |
| 2. Patrol / blockade cordon | Prevent relief & escape | Zhongdu isolation narrative **[CHR]** |
| 3. Engineer / artisan park | Trebuchet yards, sapper tools, Chinese captive crews | Empire-wide pattern **[CHR][MOD]** |
| 4. Forward earthworks / screens | Approach cover vs wall fire | General siege craft **[MOD]** |
| 5. Moat fill / bridge / dam ops | Negate wet ditch | Chinese city defence norms **[ARCH]** counterpart |
| 6. Gate / wall assault nodes | Focused breach lanes | Barbican / gate science of the defender |
| 7. Inner city collapse / palace sack | Win condition; famine already softened HP | Zhongdu 1215 **[CHR]** |

### 4.3 Recommended reverse-map opponents
- **European Castle** pack — Mongols historically threaten sedentary stone/brick systems with mobility + borrowed engines; excellent fantasy–history crossover lane  
- **Japanese** keeps — as attacker content (13th-c. invasions are a separate naval/typhoon story; use for *assault fantasy* carefully tagged)  
- **Chinese** keeps (Jin/Song/Ming grammar) — historically tightest fit  
- **Zhongdu 1214–1215** as the tutorial reverse map  
- **Dadu under Ming assault (1368)** as the tragedy mirror — Mongol player defends Chinese walls they once built

### 4.4 What not to fake
- Do not invent a universal “Mongol siege castle” blueprint identical at Baghdad, Kaifeng, and Zhongdu  
- Do not quote massacre totals from Persian chronicles as precise unit caps without **CHR exaggeration** tooltips  
- Do not erase Chinese engineer agency — Mongol siege success is often **coalition tech**

## 5. Dual-keep recommendation (mandatory design stance)

### 5.1 Brand keep — **Karakorum / Ordu**
- **Visual identity:** ger rings, Orkhon pasture light, low *hangtu* wall, four market gates, brick palace enclosure, Silver Tree throne room  
- **Fantasy promise:** “This is the Mongol world” — steppe empire ideology  
- **Combat truth:** weaker static wall HP; stronger mobility, remount, and ideology mechanics  
- **Best modes:** defence of a steppe-imperial capital; campaign hub map; early–mid tech tree

### 5.2 Military keep — **Yuan Dadu *or* siege-of-Chinese-city**
- **Visual identity:** 28.6 km-class *hangtu* rectangle, moat, eleven gates, nested palace city — Chinese imperial silhouette under Mongol dynasty banners  
- **Fantasy promise:** late empire ruling China from a real capital wall  
- **Combat truth:** full Chinese layer stack; Mongol cavalry as sortie / relief / lane-clear elite  
- **Best modes:** late-game defence; mirror campaigns vs Ming; shared assets with Chinese civilisation pack

### 5.3 Attacker identity (where Mongols shine)
Mongols should feel strongest as **attackers** against:
1. European Castle keeps  
2. Japanese castle / fort maps  
3. Chinese city keeps (Jin Zhongdu, Song cities, even rival Yuan/Ming walls)

**One-line pitch:** *Ordu brand for soul; Dadu / siege-camp for teeth.*

### 5.4 Dual-keep table

| Slot | Keep | Wall seriousness | Unique mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Brand | Karakorum + ordu overlay | Low–moderate outer wall | Relocate camp nodes; ideology totem; market-gate economies |
| B — Military | Yuan Dadu | High (*hangtu* megawall) | Chinese nested courts; 11-gate lane book; late barbicans |
| B-alt — Reverse | Siege camp vs foreign keep | Attacker earthworks | Engineer park tech; famine timer; blockade score |

## 6. Two ~7-layer stacks

### 6.1 Defending a steppe-imperial capital (Karakorum / ordu hybrid)

| # | Layer | Historical cue | TD job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Pasture approaches / remount screen | Herds and pickets beyond the wall | Slow cavalry raids; reveal stealthed scouts |
| **2** | Extramural suburbs / kilns / road villages | Settlement beyond enclosure | Chaff buildings; optional burnable loot |

| **4** | Four market gates + extra posterns | Rubruck markets; archaeological extra openings | Named lane bosses; trade buffs if held |
| **5** | Quarter maze (craft / merchant / empty north zones) | Uneven density; thick central layers vs sparse north | Ambush tiles; unequal lane value |
| **6** | Palace brick enclosure | ~8 m brick-veneered rampart under Erdene Zuu | True military shell |

**Optional bleed lane:** west-gate track to Orkhon water — siege timer if cut **[ARCH]**.

### 6.2 Attacking a Chinese / sedentary city (siege-camp reverse stack)

| # | Layer | Historical cue | TD job (attacker advances inward) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Outer blockade / herd parks | Isolation of Zhongdu | Map control score; starve defender regen |
| **2** | Siege engineer park | Captive artisans / trebuchet yards | Buildable attacker towers that *move forward* |
| **3** | Moat / flooded ditch | Chinese wet defence | Fill / bridge mini-game; drowning hazard both ways |
| **4** | Face wall / mamian enfilade | Chinese wall science | Highest defender DPS zone |
| **5** | Gate barbican / wengcheng trap court | Late Yuan barbicans at Dadu; Chinese gate tradition | Classic kill-box; Mongol sappers counter |
| **6** | Inner / imperial city screens | Nested capital grammar | Second wind for defender |
| **7** | Palace city / yamen / granary core | Political win | Surrender / sack cinematic; famine already weakened |

## 12. Do / Don’t

### 12.1 Do
- Lead with **ordu mobility** and **Karakorum ideology**
- Unlock **Dadu** as the serious late military keep
- Build **siege-camp reverse maps** as a Mongol specialty
- Reuse Chinese wall grammar honestly for Yuan cities
- Tag soft Zhongdu metrics
- Let Mongols shine as **attackers** vs European / Japanese / Chinese keeps

### 12.2 Don’t
- Default to European concentric castles as “Mongol style”
- Inflate Karakorum into Dadu
- Pretend Mongols “never fortified”
- Hard-code 900 Zhongdu towers as balance fact
- Excavate fantasy palace plans inside living Erdene Zuu for lore certainty beyond the brick-wall evidence
- Erase Chinese / Muslim engineer contributions to Mongol sieges
- Merge Shangdu metrics into Dadu without ARCH

## 15. Recommendation (one paragraph)

Ship a **dual keep**: **(A) Karakorum / Ordu** as the civilisation brand — mobile camps, modest *hangtu* enclosure, four market gates, brick palace shell, Silver Tree finale — and **(B) Yuan Dadu (or a siege-of-Chinese-city military map)** as the late-empire combat keep with measured Chinese capital walls. Lean the faction’s highest skill ceiling into **attacker reverse maps** against European Castle, Japanese, and Chinese keeps, using siege-camp engineer parks and blockade/famine timers rather than inventing native concentric castles. Karakorum’s archaeology caps wall fantasy; Dadu’s **28.6 km** enceinte and ~**24 / 16 / 8 m** type-section supply real military numbers; Zhongdu soft metrics stay labelled. That split preserves Mongol identity without lying about how the empire actually held or took cities.
