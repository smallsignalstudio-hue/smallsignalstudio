# ANCIENT EGYPTIAN MILITARY UNITS AND TACTICS — Research Brief (Tower-Defence Game Doc)

> English research notes for a later Turkish audience. Terms given in **Egyptian (Egyptological transliteration)** and **Greek** where attested.  
> **Method:** Prefer primary evidence (reliefs, papyri, inscriptions); flag uncertainty and Greek/later bias explicitly. Do **not** treat Osprey plates, Assassin’s Creed Medjay lore, or wargame army lists as primary evidence.

### Core primary sources

| **Karnak Annals of Thutmose III** (scribe **Tjaneni** / *Ṯꜣnny*) | Megiddo campaign day-book excerpts; siege of Megiddo (~7 months); booty lists (horses, chariots, armour). Propagandistic but unusually logistical. |

Anthony J. **Spalinger**, *War in Ancient Egypt* (2005); Alan R. **Schulman**, *Military Rank, Title, and Organization in the Egyptian New Kingdom* (1964); Mark **Healy**, *Qadesh 1300 BC* (Osprey—useful synthesis, reconstructive); Ian **Shaw**; John C. **Darnell** & Colleen **Manassa**, *Tutankhamun’s Armies* (2007); Kate **Liszka** on Medjay ethnogenesis (*JEgH* 2011); Wallace **McLeod** on Egyptian bows; Nic **Wernick**, *Logistics of the New Kingdom Egyptian Military in the Levant* (Liverpool PhD, 2014); Mohamed Raafat **Abbas** on Sherden (*ENiM* 10, 2017); Jeffrey **Emanuel** on Sherden acculturation; Christelle **Fischer-Bovet**, *Army and Society in Ptolemaic Egypt* (2014); Yadin / Drews debates on chariot-age warfare.

## 0. Framing: what “the Egyptian army” was

**What it is.** Across three millennia, not one institution. Rough phases: (1) Predynastic–Old Kingdom **nome levies + royal expeditionary cores**, river-borne; (2) Middle Kingdom **standing cadres + fortress system** (esp. Nubia); (3) New Kingdom **professional combined-arms army** (infantry *mšꜥ*, chariotry *nt-ḥtry*, navy) projecting power into Canaan/Syria and Nubia; (4) Late Period **mercenary-heavy** forces (Libyan lineages, Greek/Carian hoplites); (5) Ptolemaic **Hellenistic core + Egyptian *machimoi**/cleruchs**.

**Historical placement (primary focus).** Predynastic → New Kingdom (~4000–1069 BCE), with Late Period mercenaries and brief Ptolemaic notes.

**Evidence quality.** High for royal campaign rhetoric and visual kit (temple reliefs, tomb models); **medium** for unit sizes and ranks (titles attested; numbers often modern estimates); **low** for drill manuals, exact company TO&Es, and “doctrine” statements. Greek sources (Herodotus, later) invent ethnographic clarity Egypt’s own texts rarely provide.

**Game-use potential:** Faction evolves by **age unlocks**—flint/mace river militia → bronze fortress age → chariot empire → Sea Peoples mercenaries → iron/Greek Late Period—rather than one timeless “Egyptian” blob.

## 1. Infantry classes

### 1.1 Archers (*pḏtyw* / bow-troops; Nubia as *Tꜣ-Sti* “Land of the Bow”)

**What / kit.** Dominant Egyptian missile arm from Predynastic onward. Early: **self-bows** (single piece of wood; segment, decurved, double-curved profiles—Cook 2018 typology). Arrows: reed shafts; tips flint → copper → bronze. Middle Kingdom siege art (Beni Hasan etc.) shows massed foot-archers. New Kingdom: **composite / angular bow** (horn + sinew + wood, often birch-bark covered) enters alongside continued self-bow use.

**Timeline (bow tech).**
| Period | Bow type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Predynastic–OK | Horn-bows; wooden self-bows | Hunting + war; flint tips common |
| MK | Self-bows dominate infantry/siege | Composite **not** yet standard Egyptian kit |
| SIP / early NK (~1700–1550) | Asiatic-style **composite** appears | Usually linked to Hyksos / Levantine contact—**debate** on exact pathway |
| NK | Composite for chariots & elites; self-bows for mass infantry | Self-bows never vanish (cost, moisture sensitivity of composites) |

**Placement.** Foot-archers = backbone before chariots; thereafter still majority of shooters. Nubian archers prized from Old Kingdom (Weni) through Mesehti models to New Kingdom auxiliaries.

**Sources.** McLeod (Tutankhamun bows); Cook, *Variability and change in ancient Egyptian archery* (Liverpool PhD 2018); Darnell & Manassa; Beni Hasan tomb paintings; Karnak/Medinet Habu archer ranks.

**Controversies.**
1. **Hyksos as sole introducers** of composite bow: conventional narrative; some Middle Kingdom images show Asiatic-looking bows earlier—**do not over-claim** a clean “Hyksos package.”
2. Range figures (~175–200 m max / ~50–80 m accurate) are **modern estimates**, not ancient specs.
3. Popular claim that “every Egyptian was an archer” is cultural stereotype (*Nine Bows* enemies), not TO&E.

**Game-use:** Cheap mass tower DPS (self-bow) vs expensive high-penetration composite (anti-armour chariot counters); Nubian archer upgrade path.

---

### 1.2 Spearmen / close-order infantry

**What / kit.** Old–Middle Kingdom standard line: **copper/bronze spearhead on wooden shaft** + **large wooden shield faced with hide** (often cattle hide). Little body armour before NK. New Kingdom: spears remain common; shorter spears/javelins also chariot sidearms; scale armour and helmets appear for some troops (18th Dyn. onward)—**not universal**.

**Placement.** Holds ground, screens archers, finishes broken foes; less glamorous in royal art than chariots/king.

**Sources.** Mesehti spearman models; OK/MK tomb scenes; Healy/Spalinger syntheses.

**Controversies.** Degree of “phalanx-like” drill before Late Period is **unknown**; do not import Greek *phalanx* language into NK Egypt without flagging anachronism.

**Game-use:** Basic wall-line unit; high HP vs charge, low DPS; pairs with archer towers as “shield wall + fire.”

---

### 1.3 Axe and mace troops

**What / kit.**
- **Mace** (*ḥḏ*): Predynastic–Early Dynastic **signature** weapon and royal smiting symbol; discoid then pear-shaped stone heads. Later mostly **ceremonial / royal iconography**, though impact weapons persist.
- **Battle-axe**: copper then bronze heads (cutting and piercing types); MK–NK common sidearm for shock infantry; effective vs unarmoured foes and for **gate-breaking** (see sieges).
- **Daggers / short swords**: cast bronze with integrated tang after Asiatic contact; sidearm.

**Placement.** Shock specialists and assault troops in sieges; mace = early-game / ritual “king power” unit.

**Sources.** Narmer Palette (smiting); weapon finds; Medinet Habu / Karnak assault scenes (axes at gates).

**Controversies.** How often maces remained battlefield weapons after MK is unclear—art may fossilize archaic royal violence.

**Game-use:** Early smash unit (mace); mid-game “breacher” axe infantry that ignores a portion of gate/tower armour.

---

### 1.4 Shields

**What.** Typical pharaonic infantry shield: **tall, roughly rectangular or slightly tapering wood frame covered with animal hide** (cowhide frequently assumed from art/ethno-analogy; exact species rarely proven per find). New Kingdom: variety increases; **round shields** strongly associated with **Sherden** and some Sea Peoples / Levantine kit in Egyptian art—not the classic Egyptian tower shield.

**Placement.** Spearmen and close infantry; chariot drivers sometimes shown with shields; archers may fight with lighter or no shield.

**Sources.** Tomb models; temple reliefs; rare surviving examples (organic materials poorly preserved).

**Controversies.** “Cowhide” is **reasonable default**, not chemically proven for every depiction. Do not invent named Egyptian shield subtypes without attestation.

**Game-use:** Egyptian tower-shield infantry vs round-shield Sherden mercenaries (different armour profiles / flanking bonuses).

---

### 1.5 *Khopesh* / *ḫpš* (sickle-sword) users

**What.** Bronze **sickle-shaped slashing weapon** (~50–60 cm typical), cutting edge on the **outer** curve; inside curve usable to **hook** shields/limbs (tactical reconstruction—plausible, not drill-manual proven). Origin: Levantine/Mesopotamian curved weapons / crescent axes; enters Egypt in **SIP**, common in **NK** elite and royal iconography (gods present *ḫpš* to the king). Many excavated examples are **unsharpened / ceremonial**.

**Placement.** Elite sidearm, officers, royal weapon of smiting—not the mass infantry default (spear/axe/bow remain more common). Popular culture overstates ubiquity. Field use declines in later NK while **symbolic** power remains; iron examples are late/rare.

**Sources.** Weapon archaeology; temple “presentation of the scimitar” scenes; Tutankhamun and other burials.

**Controversies.**
1. “Fell out of use ~1300 BCE” (common web claim) vs continued ceremonial depiction—separate **combat** from **icon**.
2. Hooking-shield tactics = modern functional reading.
3. Wikipedia-style “changed from bronze to iron in the New Kingdom” overstates NK iron weapons (see §7).

**Game-use:** Expensive elite melee hero weapon; “hook” ability that temporarily disables enemy shield buffs.

## 2. Chariots

### 2.1 Introduction and the Hyksos debate

**What.** Light **two-wheeled, two-horse** war chariot (*wrrt* / vehicle; corps *nt-ḥtry*). Egyptian NK type: relatively light, often rear-axle emphasis for speed; crew of **two**: driver (*ḳdn*) + fighter (*snny* / “chariot warrior”), typically with **composite bow**, javelins, sometimes sidearms; quiver/bowcase on the cab.

**Placement.** Appears in Egyptian record at end of SIP / dawn of 18th Dyn. (Kamose; Ahmose son of Ibana). Becomes elite prestige arm and operational spearhead for Levantine campaigns (Thutmose III–Ramesses II).

**Sources.** Kamose texts; Ahmose son of Ibana; Karnak Annals booty lists; Kadesh reliefs; Tutankhamun chariots; Tell el-Dabʿa (Avaris) horse/chariot archaeology.

**Controversies (flag clearly).**
1. **“Hyksos introduced the chariot”** is the **standard textbook story**. Complications: (a) broader Near Eastern chariot diffusion; (b) possible earlier horse evidence debated; (c) Egyptians rapidly **adapted** design rather than copying blindly; (d) technological-determinist accounts (Yadin’s “tripartite package”: chariot + composite bow + scale armour) are challenged by **logistics-first** readings (Wernick 2014—Egypt did not hold a unique tech monopoly over Canaan).
2. Chariots as **shock rams** vs **mobile archery platforms**: Egyptian art and most specialists favour **missile platform + pursuit/flank**, not heavy scythed charge. Horses generally will not crash into dense infantry.
3. Hittite chariots at Kadesh often reconstructed as heavier, 3-man crews—**comparative, partly inferential**.

**Game-use:** Mid–late tech unlock; high mobility glass cannon; weak in mountains/forests/fortified lanes (historical constraint as design rule).

---

### 2.2 New Kingdom crews, organization, tactics vs infantry

**Organization (modern reconstructions from titles + Healy et al.).** Rough hierarchy often cited: troop ~10 → squadron ~50 → *pḏt* (“host”) ~250 chariots under a *ḥry-pḏt* (“commander of a host”). ~25 vehicles per infantry company sometimes proposed for Kadesh—**estimates, not a surviving roster**. Chariotry was an **arm** attached to divisions, crewed from wealthier strata (horse + vehicle cost).

**Tactics vs infantry (reconstructed).**
1. Soften with massed composite-bow fire while mobile.
2. Threaten flanks/rear of infantry; disrupt formations.
3. Pursue routers (spears/javelins).
4. Require **infantry support**—alone, chariots are vulnerable to broken ground, stakes, massed archers, and close assault if halted (Kadesh camp crisis).

**Vs Asiatic / Hittite foes.** Mutual chariot arms dominate open Levantine plains; infantry secures camps, sieges, and rough terrain. At **Kadesh**, Hittite chariot strike shattered the marching **Re** division—showing Egyptian vulnerability when divisions were **strung out** and poorly screened.

**Game-use:** Bonus on open map tiles; penalty in desert scrub/hills; must be paired with infantry “anchors” or die to spear swarms.

## 3. Elite and special contingents

### 3.1 Medjay (*mḏꜣ.y*)

**What (diachronic—Gardiner / Liszka model).**
1. **Old Kingdom:** people/land of **Medja** (Nubian Eastern Desert / near 2nd Cataract)—appear in Weni’s levy list.
2. **Middle Kingdom–SIP:** desert pastoralists; often equated archaeologically with **Pan-Grave** culture (circular graves, distinctive pottery)—equation is **scholarly consensus but not proven identity in every case**.
3. **New Kingdom:** term shifts toward **occupation**: elite **paramilitary police / desert rangers** guarding tombs, temples, capitals, borders; eventually includes ethnic Egyptians with Egyptian names as “Chiefs/Captains of the Medjay.”

**Placement.** Scouts, borderers, internal security; Kamose tradition uses Medjay against Hyksos. **Not** primarily a chariot corps. After 20th Dynasty the term fades from records.

**Sources.** Weni; Semna Despatches; Kamose; New Kingdom titles; Liszka 2011; ISAC Pan-Grave notes.

**Controversies.** Ethnicity vs job-title; Pan-Grave = Medjay?; modern pop-culture Medjay as “pharaoh’s ninjas” (**Assassin’s Creed etc.**) is **fiction**—use NK police/scout evidence instead.

**Game-use:** Reveal stealthed enemies; desert-map bonus; anti-tomb-robber/patrol tower specialty; late unlock allows Egyptian-trained “Medjay” (occupational) vs early ethnic Nubian Medjay skins.

---

### 3.2 King’s bodyguard / “Braves”

**What.** Royal entourage troops at Kadesh and elsewhere. Kadesh Poem/Bulletin stress the king fighting with his **immediate guard** when the camp was overrun. Reliefs show **Sherden** prominently among bodyguards (Spalinger, Breasted, Abbas). Separate labels in secondary literature—“**Braves of the King**,” *nakhtu-aa* / “strong-arm” troops—appear in popular and some Egyptological writing for elite shock infantry; treat exact Egyptian corps-name mappings carefully (title lists ≠ Hollywood unit names).

**Placement.** Palace/camp defence; last reserve; prestige escort.

**Sources.** Kadesh reliefs/texts; Medinet Habu guard depictions.

**Controversies.** How “Sherden-dominated” the guard was vs mixed Egyptian elites; size unknown.

**Game-use:** Hero-aura unit that buffs nearby towers while king avatar is deployed; catastrophic morale hit if wiped.

---

### 3.3 Sherden (*Šrdn*) and other Sea Peoples in Egyptian service

**What.** *Šrdn* (Shardana/Sherden): first securely in Ramesses II’s **Tanis Stele II** as sea-raiders defeated and **impressed** into Egyptian service; fight at **Kadesh**; under Ramesses III appear at Medinet Habu as **Egyptian auxiliaries** (horned helmets, round shields, spears/long swords)—notably **absent** from enemy lists of Year 8 Sea Peoples texts (Emanuel). Other Sea Peoples names in Egyptian texts: **Peleset**, **Tjeker**, **Shekelesh**, **Denyen**, **Weshesh**, etc.—mostly as **enemies** in Year 8; some groups later settled under Egyptian oversight (esp. Philistine problem in Levant—outside pure “Egyptian unit” scope).

**Placement.** Elite infantry / guard / marine-capable shock troops in Ramesside armies.

**Sources.** Tanis II; Kadesh; Medinet Habu; Amarna *še-er-ta-an-nu*; Abbas 2017; Emanuel.

**Controversies.** Homeland (Sardinia Nuragic hypothesis vs broader E. Mediterranean); “mercenary” vs captive-soldier; homogeneity myth.

**Game-use:** Premium anti-infantry mercenaries unlocked after defeating a sea-raid event (historical capture → hire loop).

---

### 3.4 Libyan, Nubian, Canaanite contingents

**Libyans** (*Tjehenu*, *Tjemehu*, later **Libu**, **Meshwesh**): long-time western desert foes **and** recruits; by late NK large Meshwesh groups fight for and against Egypt; Libyan officers/families rise, culminating in **22nd Dynasty** Libyan-line kings. Kit in art: often distinctive dress, feathers, side-locks; light troops.

**Nubians / Kushites:** archers and southern campaign specialists; also enemies when Kush is strong. Colonial fortress system (Buhen, Semna, etc.) structures long-term interaction.

**Canaanites / Levantines:** vassal levies, garrison troops, *maryannu* (Hurrian-derived term for chariot nobility—used widely in LBA Near East). **Nearin / *nꜥrn*** at Kadesh: poorly documented contingent, possibly Canaanite auxiliaries or Egyptian troops left in Amurru/Sumur—**arrival saves Ramesses’ camp** in Egyptian narrative. Identity **uncertain**.

**Apiru / Habiru:** social bandits/uprooted fighters in Amarna-age texts—not a neat ethnic “unit,” but usable as irregulars.

**Sources.** Medinet Habu Libyan wars; Kadesh Nearin passages; Amarna Letters; Nubian fortress archaeology.

**Controversies.** Nearin ethnicity; how “Egyptianized” Meshwesh units were; maryannu as class vs ethnicity.

**Game-use:** Faction wheel of auxiliaries—Nubian range, Libyan skirmish speed, Canaanite maryannu chariots, Nearin as “reinforcement timer” special event.

## 4. Navy and riverine warfare

**What.** For most of Egyptian history the **Nile is the strategic highway**. Troop transport by ship is attested from late Old Kingdom; SIP–early NK war is **amphibious** (Kamose/Ahmose vs Avaris harbour). Ships: papyrus reed craft (light) vs **wooden planked** Nile vessels (main war/transport); steering oar; rowers; fighting decks. True blue-water battle evidence peaks visually at **Medinet Habu** naval relief (Ramesses III vs Sea Peoples)—earliest large-scale naval battle depiction.

**Delta defence doctrine (Year 8, Medinet Habu).** Egyptian narrative: prepare Nile mouths “like a strong wall” with warships; **archers on shore** form a “wall of metal”; enemy fleet lured/trapped in confined water; Egyptian ships still under oars while enemy craft are disordered (sails furled, oars unused in the relief’s ideology); grappling, boarding, archery—**ramming not clearly primary**. Treat relief as **propaganda diagram**, not photorealistic AAR.

**Placement.** River logistics always; coastal/Delta defence in Ramesside crisis; Levantine ports (Sumur etc.) as combined sea–land nodes.

**Sources.** Kamose; Ahmose son of Ibana; Medinet Habu naval scenes + texts; Wachsmann on LBA ships.

**Controversies.** Exact Delta battle geography unknown; how much “navy” was specialized vs seconded river ships; Sherden role in teaching maritime tech = speculative.

**Game-use:** Nile lane control = supply buff; Delta map with shore-archer towers + boarding ships; amphibious Hyksos-capital assault mission.

## 5. Command and organization

### 5.1 God-named divisions (Kadesh)

**Attested at Kadesh (Ramesses II, Year 5):** four divisions (*sꜣ* / army corps in modern parlance):
1. **Amun** — advanced with the king; camp NW of Kadesh.
2. **Re** (*Pꜣ-Rꜥ*) — hit in march order by Hittite chariots and broken.
3. **Ptah** — still south; summoned in crisis.
4. **Seth** (*Sutekh*) — on the road, furthest.

Egyptian text gives Amun the epithet-like designation tied to Ramesses’ throne name (“He Gives Victory to Usermaatre-Setepenre”). Earlier 18th Dyn. may have operated with fewer corps (Amun/Re often suggested for Thutmose III era)—**reconstruction**.

**Also at Kadesh:** royal **bodyguard**; **Nearin** contingent from the Amurru/coastal direction.

### 5.2 Ranks and unit sizes (attested titles vs modern numbers)

**Attested / commonly cited New Kingdom titles** (Schulman 1964 cornerstone):
| Egyptian | Usual rendering | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *imy-r mšꜥ* | Overseer of the army / general | High command |
| *ḥry-pḏt* | Commander of a host/troop | Infantry or chariotry host |
| *ṯꜣy-sryt* | Standard-bearer | Company-level leadership; standards matter for cohesion |
| *ꜥꜣ n 50* | Greatest of Fifty | Junior officer tier |
| Troop commander / fortress overseer titles | Various | Frontier commands |

**Modern size estimates (useful but soft):** division ~5,000; company (*sꜣ*) ~200–250; ~20 companies per large infantry corps; chariot *pḏt* ~250 vehicles—figures circulate from Healy/Schulman-era synthesis. **Ramesses II “100,000+ army”** estimates are speculative.

**Scribes** accompany units (Tjaneni at Megiddo)—administration is part of the weapon system.

**Sources.** Schulman; Kadesh texts; Faulkner on standards; Horemheb Edict (late 18th) for army discipline/administration themes.

**Controversies.** Exact nesting of *sꜣ* / *pḏt* / division; whether four god-divisions existed as standing peacetime org or campaign grouping; projecting Kadesh structure backward onto Thutmose III.

**Game-use:** Four division banners as mutually buffing lane assignments (Amun tank, Re mobile, Ptah siege/labour, Seth aggressive flanking); officer upgrades named with real titles.

## 6. Combined-arms doctrine; strengths / weaknesses vs Asiatics and Hittites

**Reconstructed Egyptian NK “system” (not a written field manual):**
1. **Logistics first:** Ways of Horus (*Wꜣt-Ḥr*) fort chain across N Sinai; coastal supply; vassal grain; river transport inside Egypt (Wernick).
2. **Missile dominance:** mass archers + chariot composite bows.
3. **Mobile exploitation:** chariots for shock-of-presence, pursuit, and flexible fire.
4. **Infantry decision:** holds camp, fights in rough/siege, mops up.
5. **Psychological / royal centre:** king as tactical pivot (dangerous—see Kadesh).
6. **Foreign specialists:** Medjay scouts; Sherden shock; vassal levies.

**Strengths vs Canaanite city-state coalitions.** Superior strategic depth, supply, and ability to sustain **siege** after field victory (Megiddo); professionalization; fortress imperialism.

**Weaknesses vs Hittites.** Peer chariot power; intelligence failures (Shasu decoys at Kadesh); divided march; Hittite ability to mass chariotry for a single hammer blow; political limits—Kadesh ends in **stalemate / treaty**, not Egyptian conquest of Hatti.

**Strengths vs Libyan / Sea People infantry migrations.** Archery + fortified Delta + fleet trap; incorporation of defeated warriors.

**Weaknesses generally.** Chariots poor in hills/forests; overdependence on prestige chariot arm; bronze-age logistics ceiling far from Nile; later iron-age foes and Assyrian-style armies expose Egypt’s **metal and cavalry lag**.

**Game-use:** Egyptian win condition = **supply + missile control + siege timer**, not pure deathball; Hittite counter-faction = one massive chariot strike that punishes split lanes.

## 7. Weapons technology timeline: flint → copper → bronze → iron lag

| Stage | Approx. horizon | Military note |
|---|---|---|
| **Flint / stone** | Predynastic–OK (lingering later) | Arrowheads, knives, maceheads; OK spear/arrow tips still often stone |
| **Copper / arsenical copper** | Early Dynastic–MK | Spears, axes, daggers; soft relative to later tin bronze |
| **Tin bronze** | Increasing MK–SIP; large-scale NK | True bronze weapons common; casting improves; khopesh, scale armour possible |
| **Meteoritic iron** | From Predynastic (Gerzeh beads) through NK prestige | Tutankhamun’s iron dagger = **meteoritic** (Comelli et al. 2016)—**not** evidence of smelted-iron armies |
| **Smelted iron weapons** | Sparse early Iron Age; wider use **1st millennium BCE**, esp. from ~7th–6th c. contexts (Defenneh, Naukratis debates) | Egypt **lags** Levant/Anatolia; bronze remains central through Ramesside wars |
| **Iron common for tools/weapons** | Late Period → Ptolemaic/Roman | Greek/Carian mercenaries + Assyrian-era contacts accelerate change; mass iron tools clearer under Ptolemies |

**Controversies.** “Iron Age Egypt” is not a sharp 1200 BCE flip; cultural/economic **rejection or slow uptake** of smelted iron is itself a research problem (Erb-Satullo 2019 overview; Ogden; Snodgrass). Do **not** equip Ramesses II’s rank-and-file with iron swords for “realism.”

**Game-use:** Tech tree where iron is a **Late Period expensive unlock** that suddenly buffs mercenary hoplites more than native bronze troops—historical irony as a balance hook.

| **“Men of bronze” (Herodotus)** | Greek/Carian hoplites for Psamtik I | Late hero unit that terrifies native bronze-age enemies |
| **Machimoi** | Egyptian warrior class into Ptolemaic era | Native militia line under Hellenistic overlords |
| **Raphia Egyptian phalanx (217 BCE)** | 20k Egyptians drilled as sarissa infantry (Polybius) | Ultimate native heavy unlock with revolt risk |

## 9. Siege methods Egyptians used — and faced

### 9.1 Methods Egyptians used

| Method | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Field battle then blockade** | Megiddo (Karnak Annals) | After victory, enemy fled into city; Egyptians built **enclosure** (ditch + timber palisade from felled trees); ~**7 months** until surrender |
| **Starvation / isolation** | Megiddo; Sharuhen (~3 years, Ahmose son of Ibana) | Preferred when assault costly |
| **Escalade (ladders)** | Karnak reliefs (e.g. Askalon under Ramesses II); Medinet Habu Tunip-type scenes | Documented visually |
| **Gate assault with axes** | Same assault reliefs | Officers/soldiers hacking gates |
| **Archery suppression of walls** | Ubiquitous in siege art | Soften defenders during escalade |
| **Riverine blockade / harbour attack** | Avaris operations (Kamose/Ahmose) | Amphibious siege |
| **Ruse / infiltration** | *Taking of Joppa* (P. Harris 500) | Literary; historical kernel debated (Burke et al. on Jaffa destructions) |
| **Diplomatic intimidation after investment** | Annals rhetoric (“capture of Megiddo is capture of a thousand cities”) | Psychological warfare as doctrine |

**Notable Egyptian sieges:** Avaris (Hyksos capital); Sharuhen; Megiddo; later Ramesside stormings (Askalon, Dapur, Tunip scenes). Egyptians were **competent besiegers** but did not leave clear evidence of Assyrian-style advanced siege trains (ram towers, elaborate sapping narratives) in NK texts.

### 9.2 Methods Egyptians faced / defensive posture

| Threat | Egyptian response |
|---|---|
| Desert nomad raids | Forts, Medjay rangers, small garrisons + call-up |
| Hyksos fortified Delta base | Long amphibious-civil war culminating in Avaris siege |
| Canaanite city walls | Combined field army + blockade |
| Hittite peer fortresses (Kadesh) | Often **failed assault** → battle outside / treaty politics |
| Sea Peoples coastal invasion | Shore archers + fleet ambush (Medinet Habu narrative) |
| Internal cities | Most Egyptian **home cities lacked heavy walls** for long stretches—defence was border-centric |

**Controversies.** Buhen “catapult”; how often sapping was used (poorly attested vs Mesopotamia); Joppa tale historicity; length-numbers in autobiographies may be rounded/ideological.

**Game-use:** Two siege minigames—(1) Egyptian **starve-the-city** timer with palisade-build; (2) **ladder & axe rush** for when the timer is too slow; enemy Sea People waves force **shore tower + navy** defence of the Delta core.

## 10. Late Period mercenaries (brief, required)

**Saite renaissance (26th Dyn.).** Psamtik I (*Psmṯk*) consolidates power with **Ionian and Carian** mercenaries (Herodotus 2.152–154)—Egyptians allegedly call them **χάλκεοι ἄνδρες** (“brazen/bronze men”) for hoplite armour. Settled at **Stratopeda** (“The Camps”) on the Pelusiac Nile; later Amasis moves many to Memphis as a guard against Egyptians; **Naukratis** becomes Greek trade node. Abu Simbel Greek graffiti (mercenaries of Psamtik II) confirm real Hellenic soldiers upriver.

**Other Late Period colours.** Libyan *machimoi* traditions; Nubian/Kushite episodes (25th Dyn. reversed—Kush rules Egypt); Persian conquests (525, 343) interrupt native armies; iron use rises unevenly.

**Bias warning.** Herodotus is indispensable and **biased**; Diodorus etc. later still. Read against archaeology (Defenneh, Naukratis).

**Game-use:** Late campaign DLC—hire hoplite towers that outfight bronze khopesh elites but trigger “native resentment” debuffs (Amasis’s political problem).

## 11. Brief Ptolemaic notes (if useful)

After Alexander (332) and the **Ptolemies**, the main field army is **Macedonian/Hellenistic**: sarissa phalanx, Greek cavalry, elephants at times, cleruchic land grants (*kleroi*). Native Egyptians serve as **machimoi** (guards, police, lower infantry) and in fleets; large Egyptian heavy infantry contingent drilled for **Raphia** (217 BCE) vs Antiochus III (Polybius)—a scale-up of earlier practice, not absolute first use (Fischer-Bovet; Johstono). Military institutions become Greco-Egyptian hybrids; temple–army links persist.

**Game-use:** Alternate final age where Egyptian faction can hybridize into **phalanx + native machimoi**, with Raphia as a double-edged wonder (power spike, revolt risk).

## 12. Quick “do not assume” checklist for designers

1. NK Egyptians are **not** iron-armed.  
2. Medjay are **not** fantasy shadow-magistrates.  
3. Khopesh is **not** every soldier’s main weapon.  
4. Chariots are **not** medieval heavy cavalry.  
5. Kadesh texts are **not** neutral journalism.  
6. Division sizes are **estimates**.  
7. Hyksos “invention package” is **debated in detail**.  
8. Herodotus on Saite mercenaries is **Greek memory**, not archival Egyptian.  
9. Sea Peoples names in art are **Egyptian labels**, not self-ethnonyms.  
10. Pop-history “Egyptian navy ruled the Mediterranean” overstates brown-water + episodic Delta defence realities.
